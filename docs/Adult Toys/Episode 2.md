"Please, um, please keep this a secret from school and my parents..."

One minute later. Standing by the register of the customer-free adult corner, Yuki, her ears dyed bright red with shame, her head hanging like a condemned criminal awaiting execution, pleaded with Aya like that.

Aya, who never would have imagined that the Kayano Yuki could have this kind of hidden side, gazed at her face and groaned in deep deliberation. It was fair to say the two of them had almost no point of connection.

They were in the same class in high school, and both of them had fairly wide social circles. After spending several months in the same class, naturally there had been chances to talk. But the content of those conversations was limited to light small talk and the bare minimum of administrative matters. Their mutual impression of each other was based solely on the superficial, inferred from everyday behavior.

And the Kayano Yuki seen through that lens was an honor student who boasted grades at the very top of the year, a cut above everyone else.

Supposedly the daughter of a doctor. Said to be aiming for medical school.

It wasn't that Aya had ever paid particular attention to her grades, but even so, her academic ability was rumored often enough to reach her ears regularly. When tests were handed back, the area around her would grow noisy, and the word "perfect score" could be heard again and again, that sort of thing. Her personality was good too: sociable and gentle. In other words, an honor student everyone relied on.

Aya crossed her arms and stole a glance at Yuki's face.

"Well, y'know, there's confidentiality and all. I won't tell anyone."

At that, Yuki's face showed blatant relief as she confirmed, "Really?"

Chuckling wryly at this human side of an honor student who'd always seemed somehow removed from the world, Aya pointed at the floor.

"This place is my dad's shop. If a customer sued us, my whole family would be out on the street. I won't do anything stupid."

Yuki stared wide-eyed at Aya's face, then pressed her five fingers together in front of her chest and looked around the store.

"...So it's your family's shop, Mizuki. Wasn't the name 'Sagara Books'? A previous generation or something?"\
"It's my dad's former surname. He married into the family and became a Mizuki. Well, he and my mom are already divorced, though."

At that, Yuki made an awkward face and gave her a look of sympathy, saying, "Oh, I see."

Then she apologized, "Sorry, that was kind of insensitive," so Aya laid it bare: "You don't have to apologize. We're living comfortably off the alimony."

", But wow, I never figured you'd be interested in this kind of thing, Kayano. It was so unexpected that even after looking at your face for months, I was slow to catch on."

Utterly deflated, Yuki, looking like a leafy vegetable tossed into a kimchi hot pot and gone limp, kept her face red as she tried to make feeble excuses with "But y'know" and "I mean."

"...I have confidentiality obligations, so I won't spread it around school. But as a store employee, technically I'm supposed to kick you out. I'd like you to understand where I stand on this."\
"...Are you going to tell the police, after all?"\
"No, it means, 'If anything happens, I'll cut ties and deny everything.' I'm a bad employee, so I won't say anything."

At that, Yuki's face lit up as if a light had switched on, and her cheeks slackened sloppily.

"Th-thank you!"

Her body swayed a little, like a dog wagging its tail. Her heels danced.

She really was a charming girl. Aya let her expression soften just slightly as she watched, and quietly closed her eyes.

"So, what'll it be? Buying one? A quiet rotor. I'll give you advice."

She'd meant to say it as sincerely as possible, but she hoped Yuki would forgive the little bit of teasing that leaked out. As Aya spoke with the faintest smile at the corners of her mouth, Yuki glared at her bitterly with a face the color of early autumn leaves. Groaning "Ugh" as she wrestled with herself, she closed her eyes, tugged down the hem of her clothes that she'd been pinching, and said "O..."

"Please... I'll take it."\

She looked for something reasonably priced, prioritizing cost-performance based on quietness and price.

"Do you get to know a lot about this stuff, working at a shop like this?"

Yuki asked, stealing glances at Aya's profile. Curiosity dwelled in her eyes.

"Hmm, I wonder. That's a little hard to answer. If you're asking whether working here tends to make you knowledgeable, I can't really say, I've only ever worked here. But my dad's friendly with the suppliers. I get to hear product explanations a lot, so personally I'd say I'm pretty knowledgeable."

Aya looked at the products and picked up a few.

"You have your own room? Is it enough if the quietness is secured to some degree?"\

She looked at Yuki, who had faltered and trailed off awkwardly. Receiving Aya's gaze as she waited for the rest, Yuki faltered even more awkwardly, and with an embarrassed flush, she softly let it out.

As Aya recounted her review as if nostalgic for the past, Yuki's round eyes turned toward her.

She murmured, seemingly without thinking, surprised, so "Ah," Aya realized the cause of her surprise. And she nodded to convey that it wasn't a misunderstanding.

But for classmates shopping together, finishing the business and immediately parting ways was a lonely thing, wasn't it.

Having received it, Yuki said nothing and didn't move from the spot either, gazing at it with a tense expression for a while. Eventually, she turned eyes tinged with a little hesitation toward Aya.

"U-um... can I come again?"

Aya put a hand on her hip and thought for a moment.

To give the conclusion first: it was fine. Having let her off today, it probably wouldn't make much difference how many more times she let her in. That was Aya's thinking, but still, to come back again after being put through this kind of embarrassment showed quite a lot of nerve, which was admirable. So...

She found herself asking that out of pure curiosity. She wouldn't say pent-up with what, but it meant that, of course.

Whether probing was the right move, she didn't know, but Yuki responded, looking awkward.

But forcing out what the person herself didn't want to say felt wrong.

At that, Yuki's expression, which had been stiffening, softened gently, and she smiled with an impossibly delighted look.

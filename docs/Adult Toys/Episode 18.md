Before she knew it, midnight had come. Startled, Yuki lifted her head, blinking, checked the time on her desk clock through the pounding of a terrible headache, and dropped her eyes to her hands.

Pen still gripped in her fingers, the notebook held a string of characters she didn't recognize. Comparing it to the reference book, it turned out to be a coherent sentence — she'd apparently been studying half-unconscious.

Then, glancing at her bare arm peeking from her short-sleeved pajama top, she noticed a severe rash on her left arm, and the moment she registered it, the headache worsened. Setting down her pen, she scratched at the arm roughly, then glanced at the clock with eyes only half-open, deciding to call it a night for now, and stood from her desk.

She didn't have the strength left even to turn off the light; dragging her body along, she pressed her face into the pillow.

To her sister, who'd stopped, looking apologetic, Yuki added:

"It's fine. I should be the one apologizing. Sorry you had to see something weird."

The smile stayed plastered there the whole time.

A few days later, midterms arrived, and once the four days of testing wrapped up, peace returned to the students.

The following week, answer sheets came back for every subject, and Yuki maintained her usual results — perfect scores on most of them. But — *most.* Only in modern Japanese literature had she made a small mistake, dropping two points.

As papers were returned and classmates chattered noisily, friends peeking at her answer sheet and offering words of praise or comfort, Yuki let them wash over her with hollow, empty replies, feeling a throbbing ache behind her eyes, hit by a wave of dizziness and fatigue that felt like it could knock her out at any moment.

She barely remembered what happened after that.

Classmates seemed to be saying something, but Yuki, fearing it might be words of disappointment, fled home as if escaping. There seemed to be some talk of a celebration for finishing midterms, but she decided not to think too hard about it.

Getting home, she reported her results to her parents over dinner, and they praised her the moment she finished speaking.

And since anyone can make a small mistake, maybe she should double-check her work more carefully going forward — with advice no deeper than that, the conversation ended.

Back in her room, Yuki set the modern Japanese literature answer sheet on her desk and stared down at it for a while.

Staring fixedly at the number 98 through eyes blurred with eye strain, she kept scratching the inside of her left arm, over and over. Each scratch eased the headache a little. Her heartbeat echoed inside her skull now and then like an arrhythmia, her body temperature swinging wildly for no reason at all, sweat beading faintly under her clothes one moment, her throat parched the next, over and over.

Hearing faint laughter drift from the living room, Yuki glanced toward where the stairs outside her room would be. Then, turning her eyes back to the answer sheet, she scratched her left arm several more times, pulled out her study materials, and sat back down.

It was four in the morning. Her sister, waking unusually early, opened the curtains, captivated for a moment by the sky not quite bright yet, then yawned, stepped into the hallway, went downstairs, and headed to the bathroom.

Finishing up and heading back to her room, she noticed light leaking from under Yuki's door. The incident from the other night flashed through her mind. Guilt gripped her chest at the memory of the terrible thing she'd done, barging in without knocking and catching her little sister in such a state — but as someone who'd also taken the med school entrance exams herself, she felt a flicker of genuine worry too, and decided, as her sister, to check in, just in case.

This time, of course, she knocked first.

Careful not to wake their parents sleeping downstairs, her sister knocked softly on the door.

"Yes."

An answer came back, flat but clear. Her sister blinked a few times, glanced at the time on the phone she'd carried with her to the bathroom, said "coming in," to be sure, and stepped inside.

There, Yuki sat at her desk facing her notebook, dark circles under badly strained eyes.

Struck speechless by the unbelievable sight, her sister stood frozen for a while, and Yuki, as if eager to get back to studying, turned her eyes back to her reference book. Lost for words, her sister wet her dry mouth with saliva and managed, "Still at it? It's already four in the morning," stepping inside.

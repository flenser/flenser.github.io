Friday, 3 July, 3:48 p.m.

As Miyazono Chiharu stepped out through the school entrance to begin her journey home, she was greeted by a blue sky and the damp warmth of summer air.

A lukewarm breeze slipped between her hair and the back of her neck. She narrowed her eyes against the unbearably strong sunlight.

Immediately after the dismissal call that ended the final homeroom, Yukino had disappeared. And yet, lately, the two of them had been taking turns inviting each other to walk home together.

Had she hurt Yukino that badly? Or was there some other reason?

Dragging her heavy feet along the straight path from the entrance to the school gate, Chiharu spotted a female student standing near the gate. As she drew closer, the figure gradually came into focus. It was a thin girl with dark brown hair and unnaturally pale skin, almost sickly in its pallor.

Hasekura Yukino.

Yukino seemed to notice her, too. She raised her head, and a smile that looked almost on the verge of tears appeared on her face.

Thump.

Chiharu's heart gave an unpleasant jolt. Was it love, or anxiety?

She wasn't consciously aware of it, but perhaps the quickening pace of her steps gave her answer.

As Chiharu approached, Yukino gave a small wave near her chest, bowed her head, and took a few steps towards her.

“Good work today, Chiharu-san.”\
“Y-yeah, you too, but… I thought you'd already gone home.”

When Chiharu said so frankly, Yukino turned her gaze aside, a lonely smile lingering only around her mouth.

Then, almost immediately, she transformed that smile into something altogether different. It was a broad, radiant grin, utterly out of place.

“I was going to, but… [I remembered something I'd forgotten to tell you.]”

A terrible premonition seized Chiharu. She wanted to cover her ears and go home just as she was.

For an instant, she moved her feet as though to flee. But then she stopped, caught between hesitation and something that might have been hope.

“What is it?” she asked, her voice hoarse and trembling.

Yukino clasped her hands behind her back, looking embarrassed.

“I think we talked about this before. About how… there's a woman I like.”

Chiharu could hear her own heartbeat deep inside her ears. The rush of blood pounded against her eardrums.

An unpleasant sweat broke out across her back and forehead. Her lips pressed tightly together, and her eyes darted about restlessly.

Yukino gripped the front of her blouse tightly, then continued speaking in a clear, measured voice, wearing a smile that looked painfully strained.

“Recently, I've felt that you and I haven't been talking as well as we used to, Chiharu-san. [I found it strange. I wondered why that might be, and thought perhaps there was some misunderstanding between us. So I decided I should properly tell you who the person I like is, to clear things up.]”

That terrible premonition again. Chiharu almost covered her ears.

At the same time, her normalcy bias held her hand halfway up, whispering that there's no way that could be true.

Surely, she was about to hear her own name. Like a person desperately searching for water in a desert, Chiharu lowered her eyes, her face twisted beyond recognition.

As Yukino looked straight at her, the taste of a lie spread through her mouth. A horrible lie. An absolutely horrible one. Surely the worst she had ever tasted.

“[The person I like is Konatsu-san. I used you to get close to her.]”

At its core was the flavour of muscovado sugar, its sweetness betraying romantic feelings. But that sweetness was coated in the texture of vomit and a sour, putrid stench. The acidity, sharp and abrasive, spoke of a terror that their relationship might fall apart. It asserted itself violently, while the flavour of sewage, accumulating from negative emotions in general, shot up through her nose.

Her stomach convulsed violently.

Hasekura Yukino managed to put together the most unnaturally cheerful smile of her life.

Miyazono Chiharu narrowed her eyes in dismay and disappointment. She felt the blood drain from her face and covered it with one hand.

Her expression crumpled.

“After all this time, you think a lie like that is going to—…”

Her voice emerged from her throat, thick with disappointment. But Yukino continued to wear her impassive smile.

There was no room for doubt that it was a lie. Chiharu was certain of one thing: Yukino loved her.

And she understood why Yukino had lied.

Yukino, too, must have received some kind of criticism from Konatsu. On top of that, she probably felt guilty that her ability to detect lies was causing Chiharu pain. And if Chiharu's feelings for Yukino were merely sympathy, then Yukino must have decided that she ought to step aside herself.

Chiharu wanted to deny it. But when she opened her mouth, all that came out was air.

She knew she was partly to blame. She hadn't immediately rejected Konatsu's proposal, and she had lied to Yukino. She had felt that certain things were difficult to talk about, and had even let Yukino realise as much. She should have known better. Instead, she had refused to face the truth, continuing to look at everything through an optimistic lens.

And now this was the result.

She couldn't place all the blame on Yukino.

Even so, this was too cruel a way to do it.

“…Idiot.”

There was no taste of a lie in those words. For an instant, the smile fell from Yukino's face.

Seeing that familiar, vulnerable expression, Chiharu almost immediately wanted to take the words back.

And the moment she felt that way, she was forced to recognise that, in the end, she really did feel sympathy for Yukino because of her condition.

Yukino's smile gradually took on the look of someone about to cry. With heavy feet, she somehow managed to make her steps seem light, and began walking towards the glow of the setting sun, fanned along by the damp, lukewarm breeze like a summer cloud.

She passed through the school gate a step ahead of Chiharu, and said:

“I'm sorry.”

The following day, during lunch break, Yukino and Chiharu returned to the classroom after finishing their meal, only to find the eyes of a small group turning towards them.

They were boys and girls from the circle of friends Chiharu usually spent time with, gathered around her desk. Yamazaki, a girl with long, flashy blonde hair who occupied a central position in the group, waved at Chiharu with a smile.

“Oh, there you are! Chiharu! Hey!”

Chiharu glanced briefly at Yukino, who returned the greeting with a small wave held low by her waist.

“Mm.” Chiharu waved back at about the same height, smiling, then walked over to the group.

“All right, all right. It's Miyazono-san, whose tamagoyaki turned out terribly today and who's in a bad mood.”\
“What, did you put salt in instead of sugar?”\
“No, I got the sugar right. It was just eggs with sugar in them, rather than sugar with eggs in it.”

The group broke into laughter and sympathetic cries of dismay. Even among such a flashy, cheerful bunch, Chiharu was the centre of attention.

That really was sweet, Yukino thought as she sat down at her own desk and prepared for the move to another classroom for fifth period.

“So, who's the target, and what's the reward?”\
“You haven't been summoned to arrange a murder. Karaoke! What do you think? We're going today.”

Yamazaki gestured towards the three boys and four girls gathered there.

Since moving up to their second year, Chiharu hadn't been particularly proactive about socialising with them, but...

“Maybe it wouldn't hurt to go once in a while. I'll give it some serious consideration.”

Chiharu nodded, her response casual but sincere. Several members of the group exclaimed in surprise.

“Oh, nice!”\
“Come to think of it, this'll be our first time hanging out together, won't it? Looking forward to it.”\
“It's not decided that I'm going yet.”\
“Still, it's really unusual for you to come along to something like this, Miyazono-san.”

As the group began treating Chiharu's attendance as more or less a foregone conclusion, one of the girls commented on how unusual it was.

Aida Mana. A petite girl with a ponytail, whose looks stood out even in a class full of attractive students.

“Is it? If I'm going to be a nuisance, I can always sit this one out.”

Chiharu responded to the repeated remarks about how unexpected her decision was with a joking smile and a light-hearted quip.

There was nothing strange about the exchange: Chiharu had made an unusual choice, and her friends were commenting on it.

Yet Aida's smiling reply to Chiharu's joke carried a heavy, bitter taste.

“[Oh, no, not at all! I'm really looking forward to it!]”

*

In an instant, the food Yukino had eaten surged up towards her throat. She covered her mouth and lowered her head slightly.

Sweat began to bead beneath her blouse. She bit down hard on her lip to suppress the nausea, then pulled her chair back as though to leave. But she forced herself to stay, and even the hand reaching for her wireless earphones returned to her lap.

The meaning of Aida's lie was clear. She was genuinely displeased that Chiharu would be joining them.

Why? If there was strong hostility behind it, Yukino couldn't afford to cover her ears.

“What kind of songs do you sing, Miyazono? Actually, do you even listen to music?”

Of course, Yukino was the only one who knew about the lie.

Tanabe, one of the boys in the group, looked at Chiharu. He was particularly popular with the girls thanks to his good looks.

“Me? Well, let's see...”\
“Actually, I can't imagine Miyazono-san listening to music at all!”

Aida cut across Chiharu's answer, leaning in towards Tanabe's face with calculatedly cute enthusiasm.

“I listen to K-pop a lot, and I dance to it and everything, but you don't seem like that kind of person at all, do you? Oh, I know! [It'd be fun to turn it into a quiz and have everyone guess!] What do you think, Tanabe-kun?”

Chiharu blinked at Aida in surprise, momentarily taken aback. Tanabe also seemed to have something on his mind. He gave Aida a questioning look, then glanced at Chiharu with an awkward expression.

Chiharu, however, wore a casual smile that seemed to say, Please don't drag me into something so tedious.

“What do you think?” she asked, passing the question back to Tanabe.

“Hm, let's see...” Tanabe murmured, looking at Aida.

The two then began chatting animatedly with each other.

Chiharu looked away from them, already seeming a little fed up, and turned towards Yamazaki.

“Oh, right, Yamazaki. There's someone I'd like to invite along. Would that be all right?”

Yukino had a bad feeling about this. She pulled her chair back a little further and stood up.

“Sure. Who? Someone in this class?”\
“Yep. I'll tell you once I've got their agreement...”

As Chiharu turned her head towards her, Yukino slipped out of the classroom without a word.

She'd taken barely ten steps down the corridor, hurrying towards the toilets in an attempt to escape, when someone tapped her on the shoulder.

“...Why are you running away?”

Yukino turned around with a stiff, creaking motion. Chiharu's beautiful face was wearing a rather wicked grin.

Breaking into a cold sweat, Yukino gazed vaguely into space and hurriedly tried to make excuses with an uncertain smile.

“I-I'm not running away, I'm not! This is a tactical withdrawal, which is actually an advance.”\
“Right, right. Anyway, what do you think? The ‘Yukino Needs More Friends Project’. I think this is a good opportunity.”\
“When did you manage to get that project underway...? I appreciate it, though.”

As she'd suspected, the person Chiharu wanted to invite was Yukino.

Yukino had reflexively tried to avoid a place where so many people would gather, but she knew she ought to give the question serious consideration and answer it honestly.

“So, what do you think?” Chiharu asked, looking at her kindly.

Feeling a little less anxious, Yukino clasped her hands behind her back and thought it over. Then she gave an apologetic smile.

“Well... I'm genuinely grateful that you'd think of me.”

“But...” Chiharu seemed to anticipate what was coming, and looked a little disappointed. Yukino gave her an honest refusal.

“Spending time with a group of people is still a little difficult for me.”

The more people there were, the more often Yukino's synaesthesia would be stimulated.

Of course, she had no intention of telling everyone about her condition. That meant she'd be exposed to more lies, and if she suddenly threw up in a karaoke booth, it would cause trouble for everyone.

Yukino had confided in Chiharu because she'd come to understand Chiharu's character for herself.

She still wasn't ready to interact with these people in the same way, and Chiharu seemed to understand that.

“...Of course, I don't think you should tell them everything about your condition. But they're not bad people. I thought you might be able to tell them something like how you have trouble listening to people's voices. Then you could get used to things little by little...”

I'm sure you could find friends who'd try to make you happy, too.

Chiharu had said those words the previous night. She was surely keeping them in mind now.

Chiharu was right that Yukino ought to make more friends. And this was a good opportunity.

But throwing herself into socialising with several people at once and telling them her secret was a rough approach, even if it might be the most efficient. Yukino didn't think it suited her. She found it difficult to gauge her distance from several people all at once.

“...I'm sorry. I can't move forward at the same pace as you, Chiharu-san.”

When Yukino apologised again, Chiharu finally seemed to turn her attention to the limitations imposed by Yukino's condition.

She was different from Chiharu, who was sociable, knew how to wield lies as social lubricant, and was admired by so many people because she had the strength of character to stand by her principles. Yukino was inexperienced at dealing with people, clumsy at lying, and fragile at her core.

Having witnessed Yukino's struggles and uncertainty, Chiharu's eyes wavered before gradually dropping towards the ground.

“...I see. You're right.”

Chiharu's face twisted with self-reproach and self-loathing. She brought her hands together in front of her and bowed her head.

“I'm sorry. I was being... meddlesome and self-righteous.”\
“No, that's not true! I know you were saying it because you wanted to help me!”

Yukino hurriedly stopped her apology, reaching out as though to physically prevent her from bowing any further. She looked Chiharu straight in the eyes from close range.

“Honestly... I keep thinking that you're such a wonderful friend that I don't deserve you. Please don't apologise.”

Yukino could tell when people were lying. That meant she could also tell that every word Chiharu had just said came from the heart.

Yukino conveyed her feelings with enough force to sound almost angry. Chiharu's cheeks relaxed with relief, and she took a moment to think.

“...All right. I understand. Thank you.”\
“No, thank you. I'm genuinely happy that you care.”

They hadn't quarrelled, but that exchange of words was enough for them to make up.

Chiharu returned to her usual confident self, tilting her head towards Yukino with a slightly softened expression.

“But it's not that you hate karaoke itself, right?”\
“Hmm... I don't know many songs, but I'm interested!”\
“Then I'll invite you another time. Count on it.”

Chiharu grinned as though to say, You'd better be ready. Yukino smiled back.

Earlier, one of the boys in Chiharu's group had remarked that this would be their first time hanging out together. By that measure, Chiharu seemed to be a little closer to Yukino than she was to her other friends.

That made Yukino very happy. But if Chiharu's kindness was based on pity rather than genuine friendship, Yukino felt a little uncomfortable simply accepting it.

“I'm looking forward to it. But you don't have to go out of your way to invite me, all right? It sounds like you hardly ever hang out with your other friends, either. If you're only doing this out of pity for my condition... I'd feel bad.”

When Yukino added that, just to be sure, Chiharu's eyes widened in surprise and her lips puckered.

Then she gave a soft snort, lifted one corner of her mouth in an indignant smile, and flicked Yukino on the forehead.

“Didn't I tell you? I'm the person I care about most. I'm not doing this for your sake.”

Yukino's lips moved uncertainly as she searched for a reply. Eventually, her cheeks grew faintly pink, and she returned a bashful smile.

Later, after fifth-period chemistry, a familiar female student cautiously opened the classroom door at the front and peered inside.

“Chiharu, sorry. Do you have a biology textbook I could borrow? I forgot mine.”

It was Sayama Konatsu. Completely unbothered by the many eyes that turned towards her, she looked straight at Chiharu and asked her question.

Chiharu met that familiar gaze and raised a hand slightly.

“Oh, Konatsu. I do. Will one be enough?”\
“Is a sugar cube enough for tea? Of course it is.”

Biology had been their first-period subject that day, so Chiharu had made sure to put the textbook in her bag. She took it out and handed it to Konatsu as she came into the classroom.

“Here.”\
“Thanks.”

As Chiharu reached into her bag to prepare for sixth-period maths, she let out a surprised, “Huh?”

“What is it?” Konatsu asked, peering into the bag as she accepted the textbook.

“...My maths notebook's missing. It's due today, too.”\
“That's strange,” Chiharu murmured, checking between the textbooks and notebooks packed into her bag, all the way down to the bottom.

She was sure she'd packed everything she needed that morning. She organised her materials by subject, so if she'd brought the textbook, there was no reason she should have forgotten the notebook. And yet it was nowhere to be found.

“That's unusual. Chiharu, forgetting something? Did you bring two textbooks by mistake or something?”\
“Is a sugar cube enough for tea...? Hm. That's strange. I've put systems in place to make sure I don't forget things.”

Chiharu muttered glumly and gave up, taking out a few sheets of loose-leaf paper instead.

She could use those to copy down the lesson notes, then ask for a little extra time to hand in the assignment. Being diligent and well-liked meant that, when something like this happened, her usual good behaviour tended to work in her favour.

Even so, she'd have to change her plans for the day.

She'd have to give up on karaoke after school, go home to get the notebook, and hand it in before the end of the day.

Chiharu excused herself to Konatsu with a gesture, then went over to Yamazaki's desk a little way behind and to the left. Aida Mana and the other girls in the group were there, too.

“Sorry, Yamazaki. About today, I'm going to have to cancel after all.”\
“Huh? Well, that's fine. What happened?”\
“I forgot my notebook. I'll have to go home after school to get it and hand it in today.”\
“Oh, no... Everyone was looking forward to it. But I guess these things happen.”

The boys who'd overheard the conversation exchanged disappointed looks, while the girls voiced their dissatisfaction.

Among them, Aida had been listening silently, her eyes narrowed. Now she spoke in a drawn-out voice.

“[Could you have taken it out with your other textbooks and dropped it somewhere?]”

On the surface, it was an entirely ordinary question. Chiharu and Yamazaki responded with scepticism, then let the matter drop.

“Hmm, I don't think so.”\
“Not Chiharu. She wouldn't do that.”

But Yukino, who'd been listening in, quietly closed her eyes at the lie.

The taste was extraordinarily unpleasant. She covered her mouth, her face turning pale, confusion and agitation filling her eyes.

Yet Aida's expression was so skilfully composed into a pleasant smile that Yukino could only quietly let out a breath.

Meanwhile, Sayama Konatsu, who had been watching the exchange from Chiharu's desk, furrowed her brows and fixed Aida with a half-lidded stare. As though she'd sensed something, she turned her gaze into empty space and began to think.

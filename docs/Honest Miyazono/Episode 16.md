After retrieving the notebook and returning to the classroom, Chiharu told the group that she would be able to join them for karaoke after all, keeping the details of what had happened to herself. Aida looked thoroughly uncomfortable.

The group then filed out of the classroom and made their way down the corridor, chattering noisily as they went. At the back, however, Chiharu and Yamazaki walked together in a rather heavy silence, each lost in thought.

The landing. Humidity and warmth that brought a faint sheen of sweat to the skin. A sliver of sunlight, tinged with orange, coming through the window.

The entrance hall. The smell of shoe lockers and the clamour of students milling about. The scent of summer drifting in through the open doors.

As Chiharu took out her shoes and gazed through the glass entrance doors at the drowsy afternoon light, she found herself less than enthusiastic about the outing. Without thinking, she let out a sigh. Beside her, Yamazaki was taking off her shoes, her expression equally troubled.

“I…”

Yamazaki murmured the word. The rest of the group had gone on ahead, leaving the two of them behind. Aida seemed to be enjoying her conversation with the boys; she had apparently forgotten all about what had happened earlier and was smiling broadly.

“…I want to be able to enjoy myself with my friends without anything getting in the way. I don't want to waste the kindness those two showed me by giving me that opportunity. I can carry on as though nothing happened.”\
“That's admirable. It's not something you need to look so weighed down with self-loathing over. It's an important skill.”

Chiharu gave Yamazaki's troubled expression a faint, wry smile. A tired-sounding breath escaped Yamazaki in response.

They put their indoor shoes into their lockers and continued talking as they stood on the soft linoleum floor in their socks.

“But I don't expect you to do the same, Chiharu. Nor do I think you should.”\
“Why not?”\
“I'm not serious and virtuous like you, so I can turn a blind eye. But I don't think I could just carry on as though nothing had happened after someone did something like that to me. I couldn't. I'd slap them.”

Chiharu fell silent, focusing her attention on her fingertips as she felt the lining of her leather shoes with two fingers.

When she set her shoes on the floor, a little dust rose into the air, and the scent of summer grew stronger. Immersed in the warm, humid air carried by the breeze, Chiharu murmured, her long fringe casting a shadow over her eyes.

“I'm the same. I don't want to make light of what the two of them gave me, either.”\
“…You know, Chiharu, I think of you as a friend.”\
“I should hope so. What are you bringing that up for?”\
“But the other members of the group are just… friends of a friend, aren't they?”

That was a rather pointed thing to say. Yamazaki stood with her head bowed, her shoes still on the floor, her expression blank.

She was clearly conflicted. Chiharu put on both shoes, then stopped, still facing forwards.

“So, what you're saying is, ‘There's no particular reason I need to get along with everyone’?”\
“I'm not saying you shouldn't get along with them. I just don't think you have any reason to get close to them, at least.”\
“I came because you invited me. I'd been meaning to accept your invitation eventually.”\
“Then what about everyone else?”

Chiharu took a few shallow breaths, then evaded the question with an ambiguous, “Well…”

That was answer enough. Yamazaki gave a wry smile, put on her shoes, and the two of them set off together. The group was waiting near the school gate. Aida looked back at them, her expression anxious.

“Why do you think Aida did something like that?”

At Yamazaki's question, Chiharu gave an uncertain smile and let her gaze wander in the opposite direction.

“Well, I am popular, after all.”\
“…I see. So that's the reason. I suppose there are plenty of people who like you, Chiharu.”\
“Honestly. Being liked by someone isn't always a good thing.”

Chiharu spat the words out with an expression of weary exasperation. Yamazaki gazed at her profile pensively.

By the time they drew near the school gate, the voices of the other group members had grown louder.

“Ah, there they are! Oi! You're late!”

Chiharu did her best to suppress the sinking feeling inside her and switch herself into a vaguely sociable, vaguely human mode, so that she could honour the kindness her friend had shown her. They would all head to the karaoke place near the nearest station, sing a few songs, and then go home. That was all there was to it.

“I'm looking forward to it!”

Someone in the group said.

Miyazono Chiharu let a lie slip from her lips with a smile that looked almost pasted on. And then, in the very next instant…

A girl walking alone a little way behind the two of them broke into a run and grabbed Chiharu's hand.

Chiharu turned reflexively. The familiar scent of fabric softener and shampoo reached her nostrils. The hand was cold at the fingertips, owing to its owner's metabolism, and belonged to a slender girl with extraordinarily little body fat. Chiharu had held that hand once before, and even now remembered its distinctive feel. Her narrowed eyes flew open.

The girl, now the focus of everyone's attention, did not falter. Nor did she let go.

“Um… I'm sorry. I just… I really want to spend today with you, Chiharu-san.”

The words had not been a lie. They had come straight from the heart. And the person who had spoken them was…

Hasekura Yukino.

Her pale, unhealthy-looking cheeks flushed red with nervousness. Everyone present was so taken aback that they fell silent, apparently wondering what on earth she was talking about. Even Chiharu was so surprised that she could only stare, her mouth shut.

And yet, warmth travelled through those fingers that ought to have been cold. Looking into those gentle eyes, Chiharu felt the corners of her mouth soften.

Before she knew it, Chiharu had squeezed Yukino's hand in return. She entrusted her heart, her very centre of gravity, to Yukino, who had appeared just as she was reluctantly preparing to head for karaoke. She wanted to be swept away by her, just like this.

The moment Yamazaki saw Chiharu's expression, a faint smile appeared on her lips, too.

Yukino squeezed Chiharu's hand even more firmly.

“Um, actually… Chiharu-san and I already had plans!”

Once again, rather than merely making a claim, Yukino offered proof of their promise. She took a small piece of paper from her pocket.

Chiharu accepted it and read the words written there: One Ticket to Make a Selfish Request. She bit her lip and closed her eyes.

“That's right.”

She lowered her head, using the slip of paper to hide her face. Her voice trembled ever so slightly with the emotion she was trying to contain.

“We had a promise. We made a promise.”

Chiharu acknowledged it herself.

Several members of the group, apparently unhappy at the prospect of having their plans with the popular Chiharu disrupted, began to raise their voices. But Yamazaki, the central figure in the group, looked at Yukino with an exaggeratedly casual expression.

“Can't be helped if you already had plans. Obviously, you should honour those first.”

Yukino stared at her in surprise. Chiharu, meanwhile, saw through Yamazaki's true intentions and smiled at her.

“I'll make it up to you next time.”

She raised a finger, directing the gesture solely at Yamazaki. Not at the other people, who were merely friends of a friend with whom she had barely interacted.

“Looking forward to it.”

Yamazaki murmured the words with a happy smile. Then, without giving anyone else a chance to object, she hurried off, practically herding the others along with her.

The two of them watched the group leave, still holding hands.

Eventually, when they were fairly sure the others had disappeared from sight, Yukino tried to let go. But Chiharu firmly squeezed her hand again.

Yukino's eyes wavered with confusion as she tried to work out what Chiharu meant. Chiharu merely tilted her head, feigning innocence.

“Hmm?”

Yukino flustered, her face turning red as she struggled to find something to say. In the end, she gave up and simply accepted the sensation. With her free hand, she pointed towards the school gate.

“S-should we go home?”

Chiharu squeezed her hand firmly and replied with a genuine smile, quite unlike the one she'd worn a moment earlier.

“Yes, let's.”

A few seconds after they began walking, Yukino timidly started kneading Chiharu's hand with her fingers as she apologised.

“Um… I'm sorry for getting in the way of your plans. I heard what you said, you see.”

She meant that she had heard Chiharu's lie. That was why she'd come running.

Chiharu gave her a gentle smile, taking a moment to choose her words.

“No, honestly… I'd been thinking I didn't really want to go. Maybe I wanted someone to stop me.”

“I thought so… I knew it. I'm sorry. About the whole thing with Aida-san, I meddled when I shouldn't have…”

Yukino's face twisted with guilt, as though she were groaning through clenched teeth. Chiharu laughed, her shoulders shaking.

“Not at all. At least it didn't cause any friction. In its own way, what you did helped me, so don't put it like that. That said, I've realised all over again that I can't simply get along with everyone.”

Seeing the lonely expression on Chiharu's face, Yukino lowered the corners of her eyebrows. She had no idea how to comfort her. But, as though to tell her that there was no need, Chiharu looked straight at her.

“Which is exactly why, Yukino, thank you for pulling me out of there.”

The words were almost a repeat of something she'd said before. The moment Yukino heard them, the memory came flooding back, and her eyes widened.

You're welcome. She was about to open her mouth, forming the vowel sound for o, when she suddenly thought better of it and closed her lips.

Instead, with a rare, rather proud grin, she imitated Chiharu from that day.

“Chiharu-san. No, thank you, as always.”

At Yukino's smug little smile, Chiharu stared in surprise for a beat, then promptly burst into laughter.

“And thank you for finding my notebook, too. I'll have to thank Konatsu later.”\
“Yes! Please do!”\
“Still, you two seemed awfully friendly. What were you talking about?”\
“We have a mutual friend, so we were talking about her…”\
“Ah.”\
“No, no. Even if you have a mutual friend, there are people you just can't become close to.”

Aida and Chiharu were presumably not friends in their own right. Their connection came through Yamazaki, a mutual friend. And it was the relationship between people linked by such a mutual friend that had brought about this whole incident.\
Yukino hurriedly clapped a hand over her mouth. Chiharu gave her a wry, somewhat lonely smile in response.

Then, gazing into the distance, Chiharu continued.

“But I think that's fine. There's a limit to how many people you can truly care for.”

She lifted Yukino's hand, as though cradling it, and tightened her grip.

Yukino could feel the delicate softness of Chiharu's skin across her entire hand. Only now did she fully register that she was holding the hand of the person she loved. Her heart began to race. It pounded like a drum, and the rush of blood in her ears was deafening. The harder she tried to remain calm, the more her breathing faltered.

“U-um… this hand… how long are we going to…”

Yukino looked up at Chiharu with a bright red face. Chiharu tilted her head, wearing an innocent expression.

“Hmm? What's wrong with my hand?”

Apparently, unless Yukino said it plainly, Chiharu intended to pretend she hadn't understood. She continued walking as though nothing were amiss, still holding Yukino's hand.

As Yukino floundered, searching for words, Chiharu suddenly let go.

Yukino's mouth fell open in surprise. Right in the centre of her vision, Chiharu gave her an utterly unguarded smile.

“I'm only teasing. It wouldn't be right to force you to hold hands when you don't like it. Sorry.”\
“Ah…”

Yukino's voice caught in her throat, and her eyes darted about in confusion.

It wasn't that she disliked it. She simply didn't know whether she was allowed to enjoy something that felt so good. Perhaps Chiharu wanted to let go, too.

Thinking that, Yukino found herself reaching out to take Chiharu's gentle hand, now no longer joined to hers, and clasping it again.

Chiharu's eyes widened at the utterly unexpected sensation. Yukino, by contrast, narrowed her eyes timidly, almost as though frightened. With her free hand, she formed an awkward little fist, lowered her crimson face, and spoke in a frail voice.

“I-if you don't like it, you can let go…”

Instead of answering, Chiharu squeezed her hand in return, a happy little gesture.

She had decided to put a lid on her feelings of love, and yet, before she knew it, her hand had reached out.

How shameless, she thought.

She had no idea what Chiharu had meant by holding her hand earlier. In all likelihood, it was simply an extension of friendship. Even so, she couldn't help feeling happy at the thought of being able to share physical contact with Chiharu, as long as Chiharu didn't mind. And she was also harbouring the pathetic, pitiful hope that perhaps this might awaken romantic feelings in Chiharu.

But on the night of the party, Chiharu had said she didn't want to change the course of her life for someone else.

Was it really right for Yukino to continue harbouring romantic feelings for someone like that? She wondered.

And yet…

“So you should fight tooth and nail to win Chiharu over, too.”

Remembering the words of her romantic rival, Yukino felt the guilt vanish from her expression in an instant. Then she squeezed Chiharu's hand just a little tighter.

By the time the two of them reached the nearest station, they were riding up the escalator, still holding hands. Neither could find the right moment to let go, and they maintained a silence that seemed to reflect how reluctant they were to let this time together end.

When they reached the concourse and the ticket gates came into view, Chiharu spoke up softly, looking rather nervous.

“Hey, Yukino… um, are you free today?”

The words stirred Yukino's synaesthesia. They tasted strangely sweet, like candy.

Human emotions were not so simple. Apart from the emotion of wanting to conceal a lie, most feelings were mixed with others, making their flavours indistinct and difficult to separate. That was precisely why, when a pure emotion filled someone's words, it was so easy to recognise. Its meaning came through clearly.

Sweetness indicated affection. Love. Whether friendship, romance, or familial affection, she couldn't tell the difference.

But she could sense a powerful affection in those words, and her heart gave a sudden leap.

Yukino lowered her head, her face clouded with regret and guilt.

“I'm afraid I have work today…”\
“Oh, that's right. You do have a part-time job, don't you? Sorry, I'd forgotten.”

Silence fell again.

A few seconds later, it was Yukino who broke it, gathering her courage.

“Then… will you ask me again another time?”

She had thought that, as a friend, she ought to be treated equally with the others, just like Yamazaki and the rest.

It was hard not to despise herself for being so shameless, after having thought that way. But then the sun appeared, its gentle smile dispelling the clouds in her heart. Chiharu let go of her hand and held out her little finger.

“Of course. I promise.”

Yukino returned the gesture with a bashful smile, and the two of them linked little fingers in a childish pinky promise.

“Pinky swear,” Chiharu said softly, leading the familiar ritual.

Yukino smiled, her cheeks faintly flushed, and hid her hand behind her back. Then she squeezed her little finger, savouring the lingering sensation of their promise.

Chiharu, looking relieved, confidently held her own little finger up against her chest. Then she took a piece of paper from her pocket and held it out to Yukino.

“Oh, and one last thing.”

The words One Ticket to Make a Selfish Request were written on it.

“I told quite a few lies over the notebook business, so I'm issuing you another one.”

Yukino accepted it reverently with both hands. Her smile deepened as she drew it carefully against her chest.

“I-I'll use it carefully.”

Chiharu, however, let her gaze drift vaguely into the distance, her smile uncertain. She sounded a little dissatisfied as she replied,

“Use it more casually.”

She seemed a little frustrated that Yukino was holding back even at such an important moment.

“I'll do my best,” Yukino said, smiling with the corners of her eyebrows lowered.

“Good.”

Chiharu answered with a happy little nod.

They let a few more seconds of silence pass, finally bringing themselves to part with the moment. Then they waved to one another and headed towards different ticket gates.

“See you tomorrow.”\
“Yeah. See you tomorrow.”

The train home was as crowded as usual, with barely enough room for everyone to find a seat.

Chiharu leaned against the frame of the doors, almost like the figures on the Wind and Thunder Gods folding screen, and gazed absently at the city gradually turning crimson.

There was a beautiful city before her eyes, but what she saw in her distant gaze was a dear friend.

Hasekura Yukino.

At first, Chiharu had thought of her as a good person: responsible, sincere, and determined to fulfil her duties properly. As they met again and again, something resembling friendship had begun to grow between them. After learning Yukino's secret, Chiharu felt that they had developed a special relationship, one a little too complicated to call mere friendship.

But looking back, perhaps Yukino had already begun to occupy her thoughts around that time. The serious, awkward, kind, fragile girl had gradually become someone she couldn't help worrying about.

Chiharu recalled the sweet, almost bittersweet moments they'd just shared, and in the distance of her mind she could see Yukino's adorable expression.

She stroked the little finger of her right hand, the one they'd linked in their promise. Then she cupped that right hand with her left, as though checking it was really there. She felt as if some of the warmth of Yukino's hand, which ought to have been cold, still lingered. As though afraid it might escape, she closed her left hand tightly around it.

Then, suddenly coming to her senses and realising what she was doing, Chiharu's eyes darted about in agitation.

She narrowed her eyes. She examined her own feelings, tried to understand them, and before she knew it, her body had grown warm.

Hot. Warm. She felt as though a faint sheen of sweat were gathering on her back. Her heart was pounding.

Then the evening sun shone into the train. But Chiharu knew that the flush spreading across her face had another cause entirely.

As though trying to hide it, she raised both hands in front of her face and pressed her palms together. She let out a deep sigh into the space between them, then groaned softly in the back of her throat.

The narrowed slits of her eyes glittered in the brilliant evening sunlight.

It was a sensation she'd never experienced before. If this world had no such entertainments as manga, novels, or films, perhaps she might have mistaken it for some kind of illness.

But she knew. Though it was her first experience of it, she knew what this tumult in her heart meant, and what this breathlessness signified.

This feeling was…

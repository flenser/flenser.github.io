Under a clear blue sky, the school sports festival came to a successful close. By the time the remaining work of the organising committee had been completed, it was five in the afternoon.

Chiharu was changing in the girls' changing room, from which all the hidden cameras had now been completely removed, when the door opened.

A girl peered around the partition. The moment she spotted Chiharu, her face broke into a smile.

“Good work today. Congratulations on the Blue Team's victory, Chiharu.”

The girl with golden hair braided down her back and a red headband was Sayama Konatsu, a friend who had been in the same class as Chiharu during their first year. Her face was cute and youthful, at odds with the world-weary look in her eyes. Those deep amber eyes, with a depth that sent a shiver down one's spine, made an especially striking impression.

She was a talented young woman with extensive knowledge across a wide range of fields, and someone who could be utterly merciless in more ways than one.

Yet she was a person of firm convictions, in every sense of the word, and Chiharu liked that about her, warts and all.

“Good work to you, too. Well, there aren't many people around, so I'll say it honestly... I'm not particularly invested in who wins or loses.”\
“I feel the same. If we're going to do it, we might as well aim to win, but unfortunately, I don't care enough to feel disappointed about losing.”

As she spoke, Konatsu opened the locker beside Chiharu's and promptly began changing out of her sportswear.

The only girls using the changing room were members of the sports festival organising committee. Yukino was not among them.

Since they had been busy with leftover tasks after the closing ceremony, they hadn't been able to leave in step with their classmates.

“How have things been lately? Studies, friends, that sort of thing.”\
“Pfft! What kind of question is that? You sound like a dad who's run out of things to talk about with his teenage daughter.”\
“Don't laugh. I don't know, it just feels like it's been ages since we last had a proper conversation. I ran out of things to ask.”\
“Well, most of the organising committee's work was divided up by class, after all. To answer your question, things are going well.”\
“Hmm... What about the after-party? Is your class having one? Are you going to that?”

After taking off her sportswear, Konatsu wiped down her slender body with a deodorant sheet, then fixed her amber eyes intently on Chiharu.

Chiharu replied with a wry smile and an “Ahh” as she tied the necktie of her blouse with casual precision.

“Our class is having one, technically. But I'm not going.”\
“Oh, so you're going to the organising committee's after-party? In that case... maybe I'll go, too.”

Konatsu suddenly seemed enthusiastic. Leaving the front of her blouse unfastened, she rested a hand on her chin.

She had jumped to the wrong conclusion, though. Chiharu's eyes widened, and she let out a startled “Huh?”

“Wha?” Konatsu returned an equally foolish sound.

Three seconds passed as they stared at each other. Then Konatsu pointed at Chiharu and said, “I didn't know.”

“I didn't know either,” Chiharu replied, pointing back at her.

Before Konatsu could explain herself, however, Chiharu cut in.

“So the organising committee's having an after-party, too. Let me guess, the committee chair came up with the idea. Your senior's class has a bad atmosphere, if I remember correctly, so they're not having a class party. Now they're trying to fill the hole where their youth should be with a sense of camaraderie from an organising committee that barely worked together and has no real solidarity. And because they're jealous of people like me, whose classes are having parties, they're stubbornly scheduling theirs at the same time. How'd I do, senpai?”

Chiharu turned around in her skirt with a sly grin, without even looking back as she posed the question.

Behind her, the third-year committee members clustered around their lockers were grinning.

“BULLSEYE, YOU LITTLE BASTARD!” the committee chair shouted from the middle of the group, sounding thoroughly frustrated.

Laughter rippled through the changing room.

“Truly, your powers of observation are extraordinary.”

Konatsu laughed as she tied her ribbon beside Chiharu.

“Of course they are,” Chiharu replied, puffing out her chest.

“Anyway, if you're not going to the committee's after-party, what did you mean when you said you weren't going to the class one?”\
“I've promised a friend we'll have dinner together. That's where I'm going.”

Chiharu's cheeks softened into a smile as she thought about the plans she was looking forward to. For an instant, Konatsu's eyes narrowed. But she quickly arranged her features into a mild, unremarkable expression and voiced what seemed like a simple question, having tucked away anything less innocent in the corners of her mind.

“You mean separately from the after-party, right?”\
“Exactly. Oh, and it's not a group. Just one person. She's on the organising committee, too, from my class, 2-C.”

Konatsu searched her memories and suddenly recalled a girl with dark brown, shoulder-length hair.

It was the girl she'd seen retching in distress in the toilets just before the organising committee's first meeting.

Now that she thought about it, she'd often seen Chiharu and the girl interacting at school, looking rather close.

Remembering this, Konatsu covered her mouth with one hand and lightly bit her lip behind it. Her eyes narrowed involuntarily, but she quickly closed them to disguise the reaction.

“I'm glad you've managed to make a friend,” she said brightly, putting on a cheerful voice.

“Are you the mother of a teenage son now?”\
“Then what? You want your mum to die?”\
“That's an especially unpleasant sort of mother, and in a disturbingly realistic way.”\
“Joking aside, what about this other girl? Has she finished changing?”

Konatsu had thought she might at least get a look at her face, but Chiharu knew about Yukino's circumstances. She murmured an “Ahh” to fill the pause, letting her gaze wander aimlessly before glancing towards the door.

“She doesn't do well in crowds. I think she's changing in the toilets.”\
“Eh? Really? And she's still going to an after-party with you?”

Konatsu was surprised that the girl would choose to change in a cramped place so ill-suited to the task. She was also puzzled that someone who disliked crowds so much would nevertheless go out with Chiharu.

But Chiharu merely smiled with a confident, almost triumphant air.

“Well, I'm special to her. Special.”

Konatsu knew, objectively speaking, that Chiharu would never use that expression or say those words when talking about her relationship with Konatsu to a third party. Hiding her sense of inferiority, she managed a smile with only her lips.

“Oh? You two sound awfully close. I'm jealous.”

*

It was half past six in the evening. After going home to change, Yukino and Chiharu met at the station nearest their high school, then headed to a family restaurant a little further away.

“Right, then. Once again, good work on the sports festival organising committee!”\
“Yes, good work! C-cheers!”

Their food arrived, and the two of them clinked their glasses together once more.

Yukino drained the orange juice that had been reduced to just thirty per cent of a glass, while Chiharu took a small sip of the oolong tea that still filled hers almost to the brim. They set their glasses down at the same time, but only Yukino's made a light clink.

After proposing the toast, Yukino began to fidget, wondering whether it would be all right to go and get a refill. Noticing her hesitation, Chiharu smiled without a trace of embarrassment.

“Go on, then.”

Beaming with excitement, Yukino went off to refill her drink, taking the opportunity to get another helping from the salad bar as well.

Chiharu watched her childlike enthusiasm with a smile, resting her chin on one hand.

Yukino wore a monochrome outfit: a loose-fitting black shirt, black-and-white shorts, and grey socks. Her hair was unusually tied back, an attempt to make herself look a little more stylish.

Chiharu wore a short-sleeved black blouse, a checked skirt, and bright white trainers. She usually gave off a mature air, but her clothes were cute and youthful, just right for her age.

“Thank you for inviting me today! I wouldn't have come here otherwise, unless there'd been some special occasion.”\
“I'm glad you're enjoying yourself. I figured you wouldn't go to your class's after-party.”\
“Y-you guessed correctly. Though... I know they're not bad people.”

Yukino gave an uncertain smile as the ice in her orange juice rattled.

Given her condition, it was only natural that she would avoid gatherings of her own accord.

Chiharu flashed her a mischievous smile as she eagerly plunged her knife into her hamburger steak.

“Don't worry, I understand everything. More importantly, though, I'm concerned about you being somewhere as crowded as this. Are you all right? If it gets bad, just cover your ears straight away.”

The seating arrangement was thanks to Chiharu. Before the waiter could suggest a table, she'd asked, “Would it be all right if we sat over there?” and secured a spot in the corner of the smoking section. Most of the customers in that area were adults, and the partitions kept the younger patrons' hubbub at a distance.

Yukino could just barely taste the faint voices from far away on her tongue, but they weren't too bad.

It was thanks to Chiharu choosing such a good spot. Yukino smiled softly with happiness and nodded.

“Yes.”\
“Actually, you know what? It'd be better if I could cover your ears when you need me to. Come sit beside me.”

They were at a four-person table, so naturally the two of them had been sitting opposite each other. But now Chiharu gestured towards the chair beside her.

“Huh?” Yukino let out a silly little sound as she stared at the chair. A moment later, the meaning of Chiharu's words sank in, and she faltered too. Her wide eyes trembled, and her voice shook.

“No, I... I can't ask you to go that far...”\
“It's not going that far. I took all of this into account when I invited you. But if you don't want to, that's fine.”\
“It's not that I don't want to... I just... I don't want to be a nuisance...”

Yukino continued to struggle with her words, unable to resolve her internal conflict. Chiharu finally lost patience and sighed.

Then, with an exasperated smile, she tapped the chair, leaving Yukino no room to argue.

Yukino nodded timidly.

After moving their plates and other dishes to the seats on that side, Yukino murmured, “E-excuse me,” and sat down beside Chiharu.

A faint scent of deodorant drifted over. Yukino's heart began to pound noisily.

Oh, right. I like this person...

The thought made it impossible to meet Chiharu's eyes.

“Let's practise.”

As she spoke, Chiharu covered both of Yukino's ears with her hands. Through the contact, Yukino could feel Chiharu's body heat.

Feeling the warmth radiating from Yukino's ears, which had turned bright red, Chiharu looked a little surprised. Then she grinned.

“You're shy, aren't you?”\
“Yes...”

Unable to deny it, Yukino answered in a barely audible voice. Chiharu's shoulders shook with amusement.

After that, in preparation for any emergency, Chiharu began eating while turned slightly towards Yukino. Yukino did the same.

Since they were both angled a little towards each other, their knees occasionally brushed. Each time they touched, their eyes met briefly. Yukino would grow embarrassed and look down, while Chiharu, by contrast, would say something odd like, “I'm keeping count.”

They continued eating for a while before Chiharu suddenly looked up, as though she'd realised something.

“Oh, right. You don't really go to family restaurants much, do you, Yukino?”

“Huh? Oh, yes, that's right. As the name suggests, they're family restaurants, after all.”

The Hasekura family, whose relationships were strained, to put it mildly, would never come to a place like this.

Yukino smiled a little sadly. And yet, that was precisely why Chiharu's invitation meant so much to her.

A tiny glimmer of happiness bloomed in Yukino's clouded expression, like a lotus flower rising from the mud. Seeing it, Chiharu's face softened with relief. Then she nudged her own plate a little closer to Yukino.

“Then I hope this becomes a happy memory for you, even if only a little. Here, have my carrot.”\
“...Um, it's the only thing on your plate you haven't touched. You're offering it out of goodwill, right?”\
“If you feel bad about getting something for free, I'll trade you for some of your hamburger steak.”\
“It's cheating to refuse to answer yes or no. You're taking advantage of the fact that you know about my condition.”

Even Yukino's tongue couldn't make sense of Chiharu's incoherent remarks. They were essentially flavourless and scentless, utterly incomprehensible.

But Chiharu clearly seemed to hate carrots, so Yukino would have to be firm for her own good.

“Y-you shouldn't be a picky eater! Though I'm hardly one to talk.”

Yukino gave her a gentle scolding. Chiharu responded by directing a sulky, half-lidded gaze into empty space.

Then, pouting, she hooked the tip of the carrot with her fork and brought it to her mouth. Her expression grew progressively more displeased, her eyes narrowing as she chewed.

“Glaring at me won't help,” Yukino retorted.

After nearly thirty seconds of silent chewing, Chiharu washed down the remnants of the carrot with tea.

Then she proudly showed Yukino the carrot, now reduced by one piece out of three.

“I ate one! Well? Aren't I good?”

Yukino drew in a long breath and closed her eyes in troubled resignation.

“Oh, honestly...”

Fully aware of the weakness that came with being in love, she ate the remaining two pieces of carrot for Chiharu.

Chiharu watched her from right beside her, her beautiful eyes narrowing with delight.

Her gaze was as lovely as the night sky visible through the distant window, deep enough to draw someone in. Her soft, pale-pink lips curved upwards in amusement, occasionally moving in a faintly alluring way as she struggled to suppress a laugh. It lent an approachable warmth to her otherwise refined features.

Just being watched, or stealing a glance at her, was enough to set Yukino's heart racing.

She was afraid Chiharu might hear the sound of her pounding heart.

That was the only thing she was worried about now.

Before long, they had finished their meals, and both sets of dishes were empty.

“Ahh, that was delicious. If you're free afterwards, shall we go somewhere?”

Chiharu spoke contentedly, rubbing her stomach, which hadn't grown at all. Yukino, by contrast, pressed a hand to her painfully full belly and thought for a moment, bringing her other hand to her cheek.

“W-well, I suppose so...”

Her schedule was, of course, completely free. And what she wanted most was to spend more time with Chiharu.

Unfortunately, she wasn't exactly flush with cash, so it would depend on where they went. Yukino was wondering how best to explain that when Chiharu leaned in, as though she'd seen right through her.

“【I'm not exactly rolling in money either, so】 we won't spend anything. We could take a walk through the shopping district, or...”

The taste of vomit sprinkled with sugar. The taste of a sweet lie.

But there wasn't much intention to conceal anything behind it.

It was a faint flavour that slipped out naturally. Yet it hit Yukino hard, especially with her stomach so full.

The contents of her tightly packed stomach began to churn all at once. Sweat immediately beaded on her skin, and her face went pale as she clapped a hand over her mouth.

“Ah.”

Realising she'd lied without meaning to, Chiharu covered her own mouth. Then, far too late to do any good, she covered both of Yukino's ears.

Several seconds later, Yukino managed to hold it back by the narrowest of margins. She let out a deep sigh, and Chiharu's shoulders relaxed as she gave an embarrassed smile.

“I'm sorry...! I did it completely unconsciously! I was trying too hard to be considerate!”\
“N-no, no... I'm the one who should apologise for making you feel you have to be considerate of me...”

Yukino managed a smile despite her ragged breathing. Chiharu, by contrast, closed her eyes with a look of genuine remorse.

But Yukino really didn't mind.

She had tasted this flavour countless times throughout her life, more than a decade of them, as lie after lie had assailed her like a relentless shower of rain. Compared with those experiences, Chiharu's lies were gentle, infrequent, and came with the added benefit that she would be careful after making a single mistake.

As far as Yukino was concerned, asking for any more would be downright greedy.

Yukino took a small breath and looked at Chiharu, whose brows were still drawn down apologetically.

“Um... i-if possible, could we go somewhere that doesn't cost money? I'd like to stay with you a little longer.”

Chiharu stared at Yukino as she rephrased her request. The guilt in her expression gradually gave way to happiness and gratitude.

Then, smiling with genuine affection, she nodded before letting her smile turn playful.

“We've eaten too much, so let's walk a little. There's a lively street nearby.”

At 6:45 p.m., Yukino dashed out of her house wearing a shirt and a pair of easy-to-move-in shorts, and made her way to the festival grounds. It was hardly an elegant outfit, but her desire to see Chiharu even one second sooner outweighed her wish to look pretty for her. Given the circumstances, it was unavoidable.

As she reached the area in front of the station, she found it just as crowded as she had expected.

Chiharu had been right. If they had arranged to meet here, finding each other would have been difficult.

Now, they were due to meet at 6:50. The meeting point was a space partway down the main street, where she would turn left. Chiharu had said there were hardly any people there, so they should be able to find each other as soon as Yukino arrived.

She wanted to see her. She wanted to see Chiharu as soon as possible and tell her how she felt.

Yukino hurried forward, her trainers flapping noisily with every step, intent on reaching the meeting place as quickly as she could. But then, suddenly, she stopped.

Something unusual had caught the edge of her vision. She focused on it, trying to work out what it was.

There were bus stops and a taxi rank in front of the station, and beside them stood a small, solitary smoking area.

Unhealthy plumes of smoke rose from it, the very embodiment of ill health. But just outside its partition, in the corner formed by a guardrail and the barrier, sat a little girl with her knees drawn up to her chest.

Her black hair fell down her back, and she looked no older than a child in the lower years of primary school. Very young indeed. She seemed entirely out of place in a smoking area, and Yukino stood there for several seconds, oblivious to the inconvenience she might be causing passers-by.

Then she suddenly came to her senses. She hurried on, checking the time as she went, torn between the need to avoid being late for her meeting and concern over whether the little girl was all right. Most likely, she was simply waiting for her parent, who was inside smoking. Besides, even if that were not the case, there were plenty of adults around. There was no need for Yukino to go out of her way to approach her.

That was what she decided.

*

“Um… are you all right?”

*

That was what she had decided, but before she knew it, Yukino was looking the little girl in the eye.

The girl gazed up at her with a puzzled expression. There was no sign of wariness in her eyes, only curiosity as she looked at Yukino’s concerned face.

“Are your mummy and daddy with you?”

Yukino immediately tried to establish the situation, and her fears proved justified. The girl shook her head vigorously.

“I came with Mummy, but we got separated at the festival. Mummy smokes, so…”\
“Oh, I see. So you might meet her again if you wait here?”

The girl nodded solemnly.

It seemed she really had become separated from her mother, just as Yukino had feared. But the girl had apparently thought through how to reunite with her and acted accordingly. Yukino folded her arms and groaned, “Hmmmm,” sinking into deep thought.

If she looked at it optimistically, things would probably work themselves out somehow even if she left the girl as she was.

But at a glance, the girl still seemed to be well under ten years old.

She must be frightened. In that case, Yukino wanted to do whatever she could to help. She turned the problem over in her mind.

“Um, er… did you get separated around here? Or somewhere along the main street?”\
“…I don't know.”\
“Then… well, do you remember when you got separated? Was it just a little while ago?”\
“…I don't know.”\
“Um, well… did you and your mummy agree that you should wait here?”\
“I don't know.”

She didn't know anything! Surely that couldn't be true. But she had no awareness that she was lying, either, so Yukino could detect no taste of concealment in her voice. There was no way to distinguish the truth.

Yukino's condition could expose other people's lies and wrongdoings, but at a time like this, it was completely useless.

With cold sweat breaking out, Yukino grimaced and groaned. She stared into space, looked up at the sky, then checked her phone.

The option of simply leaving the girl there never even crossed her mind.

After thinking carefully for about ten seconds, she sent Chiharu a quick message.

I'm sorry, I'll be a little late.

Once she had sent it, she hurried away from the smoking area and looked around.

It would be difficult, and hardly sensible, to try to solve this on her own. She would do things properly.

As expected, an event that drew this many people had staff on hand. A little way off stood an older woman who appeared to be one of the organisers, wearing a fluorescent vest. Her hair was completely white, yet she moved briskly, managing the flow of people without the slightest hint of frailty. Her back was perfectly straight, too.

“Excuse me…”

Yukino approached somewhat nervously. The older woman turned her sleepy-looking eyes towards her.

“Hmm? What's up?”\
“There's a little girl, about primary-school age, sitting near the smoking area. I spoke to her, and apparently she's become separated from the mother she came with. Her mother smokes, so she seems to be waiting near the smoking area…”

Yukino pointed towards the girl, apologetically, as though aware she was interrupting the woman's work.

The situation was a little ambiguous as to whether it warranted intervention, but despite her sleepy expression, the woman was not the sort to brush off a troublesome matter. Her face immediately grew serious. Resting a hand on her chin, she pictured the area in her mind.

“Ah… There's a lost children's centre. It might be best to take her there.”\
“That's what I thought, too. I just wondered whether they might have agreed to meet at the place where they got separated, or something.”

When Yukino explained her concern, the woman folded her arms and pursed her lips. A few seconds later, she said:

“Hmm. Wait here a moment.”

She pressed a hand to the earpiece hidden beneath her short white hair and gave a brief report over her radio. Then she glanced at her watch, finished the transmission and turned back to Yukino.

“All right. There's a lost children's centre a little way from here, so we'll take her there together. I'll call another member of staff to this area and explain the situation. If a parent comes looking for a child at the smoking area, they'll know what to do. That should cover it, shouldn't it?”

Admiring the woman's swift and efficient handling of the situation, Yukino bowed her head in gratitude.

“Y-yes!”

The woman gave her a fearless smile and a thumbs-up.

And so, the possibility of Yukino being suspected of abducting a child had been eliminated. She returned with the woman to where the girl was sitting. The girl had given the same monotonous answer, “I don't know,” when asked whether her mother knew she was waiting there. Yukino tried phrasing the question in several different ways until she finally managed to get a clear answer. With that settled, the three of them set off towards the lost children's centre, located a little before the midpoint of the main street.

The girl looked calm, her expression perfectly serious. But once they started walking, she reached out and took Yukino's hand.

Considering how she had insisted on answering even simple questions with “I don't know”, she must have been terribly anxious inside, even if she was doing her best not to show it. Yukino smiled and squeezed her hand firmly in return.

When it came to people unaffected by her condition, Hasekura Yukino really was just an ordinary person, with no special skills at all. A frail, introverted, rather hopeless woman, capable of little more than anyone else.

But that did not matter.

A special ability was a means, not a motive. The motive had always been there, deep in her heart.

“Your mummy is waiting for you, so let's hurry and go and find her, all right?”

The girl nodded and quickened her pace a little.

The older woman, walking on the other side of the girl, slipped a finger into the pocket of her denim jeans. She watched the exchange with an affectionate smile, then turned an appraising gaze towards Yukino.

“Do you know this child?”\
“Ah, n-no. This is the first time I've met her. I noticed her while I was on my way to meet someone.”

The woman looked surprised. “What about the time?”

Yukino gave an ambiguous smile and looked off into space.

“I've sent them a message.”

The woman shrugged as if to say, Well, well, then glanced at the hands of Yukino and the girl, clasped together.

“You're a real soft touch… I'll give you some festival vouchers later.”\
“N-no, please don't! I couldn't accept them, really!”

There was nothing wrong with receiving fair compensation for one's efforts. Yukino had no intention of rejecting that principle.

But having offered her hand to help the girl, she felt somehow uncomfortable accepting a reward for it in front of her. It was foolish, perhaps, but strangely enough, this was one kind of foolishness she could not bring herself to dislike.

As they continued talking, Yukino felt the little hand in hers begin to lag behind.

She looked down and saw that the girl was walking half a step behind, her head bowed. Yukino hurriedly slowed her pace without drawing attention to it. But the girl slowed down more and more, until eventually all three of them came to a halt in the middle of the street.

“D-did something happen?”\
“Did you forget something? Auntie can go and get it for you.”

When the two of them asked, the girl murmured in a dejected voice:

“My feet hurt.”

Yukino and the woman exchanged bewildered looks, then looked back at the girl.

A dozen or so seconds later, Yukino was trudging along with the girl on her back, sweat pouring down her face.

“Gnnnngh…”

She clenched her teeth, eyes wide, and forced her trembling, feeble body onwards.

The girl probably weighed less than thirty kilos. By a strange coincidence, Yukino herself weighed less than forty.

With her body chronically malnourished from her unbalanced diet and frequent vomiting, this was an ordeal that pushed her to the very limits of her strength. Sweat broke out all over her in an instant. Strip away her synaesthesia, the ability to read other people's emotions, and what remained was simply a frail, introverted, utterly helpless woman.

“Big sister, are you okay?”\
“I-I-I-I'm fine. I'm perfectly fine…!”\
“You've got some grit, I'll give you that…”

The woman walked briskly alongside them, looking impressed. At first, she had offered to carry the girl herself, but she appeared to be around sixty. Yukino couldn't possibly ask her to do something so strenuous. It might have been unreasonable for Yukino to carry the girl, too, but that was beside the point. It couldn't be helped. They couldn't make the little girl walk.

Breathing in ragged, irregular gasps, Yukino stared straight ahead with a distant look in her eyes and spoke to the woman.

“I… I don't have any grit. I really don't.”

The woman listened to the exhausted confession of a girl whose name she did not even know.

“I'm no good at anything, and I think I'm a useless person myself… Whenever something difficult comes along, I give up on myself straight away. I did it recently, too, and ended up hurting someone precious to me.”

Yukino spoke as though expelling the stagnant feelings from deep inside her chest, then gave a self-mocking laugh.

“I wish I had some grit.”

The woman gave a soft chuckle and lightly nudged Yukino's shoulder, taking care not to jostle the girl on her back.

“If you can feel that way, it'll come to you in time.”\
“…Hehe. I hope so.”

In reality, becoming a high-school detective is difficult, with various laws standing in the way. But becoming a university-student detective is comparatively easy, at least from a legal standpoint.

Three and a half years had passed since that summer day when they confessed their feelings to one another against a backdrop of fireworks.

It was the winter of her second year at university. Hasekura Yukino was sitting on a guardrail beside a utility pole, along a two-lane road in the city.

It was early December, bitterly cold. The time was five in the evening, and the sky had already taken on a gradient of deep purplish blue.

In front of her stood a Chinese restaurant, from which delicious aromas were wafting. Her stomach gave a rumble.

But she had to resist. Resist!

The woman, now considerably healthier and better nourished than she had been three and a half years earlier, grimaced as though fighting off her hunger and shook her head vigorously from side to side.

Compared with three and a half years ago, her complexion was much healthier, and her hair had become glossy. Her dark brown hair was still the same semi-long style, with braids woven into it. She had grown a little taller, but still hadn't caught up with her girlfriend.

Her face had become slightly more mature, and an untrustworthy-looking smile played across it.

She wore a long coat to withstand the bitter winter cold, with a cardigan underneath and denim jeans below.

After a while, an adult came walking towards her from the far end of the pavement, emerging through the school gates.

Hasekura Yukino naturally rose from the guardrail. She bowed towards the restaurant as though she had just finished eating there, then began walking towards the approaching adult.

When she reached the woman, she raised a hand to stop her as she tried to walk past.

“Good evening. Sorry to bother you when you're probably tired. Might I have a moment of your time?”

The woman regarded her suspiciously. She appeared to be a young teacher in her late twenties, dressed in a business suit.

“Y-yes… What is it?”

She clearly wanted to get home as soon as possible.

Feeling a little sorry for her, Yukino nevertheless took a business card from inside her coat.

“My name is Hasekura. I'm a private detective. I've recently received a consultation from the parent of one of your school's students concerning bullying. I realise this is a rather presumptuous request, but might I ask you a few questions?”

The woman accepted the card and read it.

“Convenience Detective… Kona-chan?”

She frowned at the name of the agency, which sounded like a joke. Her wariness became immediately apparent.

Yukino had suggested that they give the agency a little more thought before settling on a name. Still, she smiled wryly. In this case, having people be wary of her was actually convenient.

Normally, revealing that she was a detective would put her at a disadvantage. But Yukino was an exception.

“I'm afraid I can't tell you anything. I'm sorry, but matters like this should be handled through the school…”\
“However, the student in question was quite distressed. They told me they'd already consulted the school, but nothing had been resolved.”

Yukino gave the woman a sad look and pressed her a little, just as she was about to dismiss her outright.

The woman furrowed her brow. Yukino didn't intend to cause her any trouble, but there was one thing she wanted to ask.

“Is there anything in particular that comes to mind in your class, sensei?”

The female teacher's face twisted guiltily.

Then, as though searching her memory, she stared vacantly into the distance with an almost exaggerated expression of thought. After a light cough, she answered in a dismissive tone.

“[No.] Could you please refrain from making such rude remarks?”\
“Wouldn't students have come to you with concerns about something like this, though?”\
“[No.] Could you please stop this now?”

Emotions were conveyed to Yukino's tongue as tastes through her synaesthesia.

The underlying flavour was sour, indicating guilt. Perhaps she felt she couldn't handle the situation herself, or perhaps someone higher up had given her instructions.

But the words of denial were unmistakably lies, intended to conceal something. They tasted like foul vomit: lukewarm, sour and viscous.

Having vomited so many times over the years, Yukino could recall that sensation with terrible clarity. Inevitably, nausea rose in her throat.

“Guh…”

She suddenly retched, startling the woman.

But Yukino thumped her chest repeatedly, like a drummer beating a rhythm, then coughed.

“Cough… I'm all right.”

She raised her head with a smile and looked at the suspicious woman, waving a hand.

“Sorry about that. Please excuse me.”\
“N-no… Well, can we leave it there?”\
“Oh, sorry. Just one last thing.”

Yukino raised a finger to stop the woman as she turned to leave, receiving a look of outright contempt in return.

She smiled wryly at that, but also acknowledged the guilt she had tasted, and tried to reach out to the woman in her own way.

“Um… I think it must be terribly difficult to resolve something this serious all by yourself. But I'm sure it'll be all right. It really will. We'll find a way to sort this out.”

Yukino looked her straight in the eyes as she spoke.

The female teacher's eyes wavered with uncertainty.

But when she saw the unwavering conviction in Yukino's gaze, her face twisted. She merely gave a small bow and walked away.

Yukino sensed no malice in her retreating figure. All she saw was the pitiful back of a homeroom teacher who, in the end, had become complicit in covering up the situation.

Well, that was the job done.

It had been quite a demanding assignment, especially after a full day of university lectures.

Yukino took out her smartphone and began walking towards the station by a roundabout route, making a phone call as she went.

The recipient was saved as Kona-chan.

She held the phone to her ear. After a few rings, the call connected.

“Hey, hello. How'd the investigation go, Yuki?”

A woman's voice.

Yukino felt reassured by the familiar voice of her boss and began explaining.

“I'll submit the written report later, but…”\
“Uegh!”

Remembering the taste made Yukino feel as though she was about to throw up again. She crouched down on the spot and retched several times.

Hearing the sounds and her voice through the phone, the woman burst into laughter.

“Brings back memories, doesn't it? Come to think of it, you were doing exactly the same thing when we first met.”

Kona-chan.

That was Sayama Konatsu, the name of Yukino's best friend, who had opened her own detective and general services business while attending university, on the very day she turned nineteen.

It was essentially a student start-up.

Her sole employee was Hasekura Yukino.

After they ended up in the same class in their third year of high school, the two had discussed their futures and decided to attend the same university. Konatsu had told her, “You can put that condition of yours to good use,” and invited her to join the business on something like a part-time basis.

As a result, Yukino now helped with both the general services and detective work.

Yukino stood up, covering her mouth, and glared half-heartedly at her laughing best friend.

“Honestly… You started this business relying on my condition, didn't you?”\
“That's what you say, but I've actually solved more cases than you have.”\
“That's because you pad the numbers with the general services jobs. Take that sticker-removal job the other day, for example. Counting that as four solved cases on the website is practically fraud, isn't it?”\
“Ah, yeah, yeah. All right, all right. Shut up already. Anyway, what did you find?”

Returning to the subject, Yukino continued walking towards the station and reported her findings.

“It's confirmed. The homeroom teacher knows about the bullying, and reports have been made to her. On top of that, it looks like the whole thing is being covered up. The teacher is quite young, and she seemed to feel guilty about it. So I suspect someone higher up has ordered her to keep quiet.”\
“Huh… Got it, got it. Well, if the teacher knows about it, that means it's happening inside the school. And the teachers can't be relied upon. Right, let's stick with the original plan and have the client's child carry a voice recorder. We'll also arrange a way for them to contact us directly, in case something happens outside school.”

The boss made her decision without hesitation.

“Understood,” her employee replied.

They exchanged a few more details, and the initial report was complete. Yukino would prepare the full written report later.

At one point, Konatsu sighed. Yukino could hear the sound of her leaning back in her chair through the phone.

“Honestly… bullying, huh? Bad people crop up in every environment.”

Yukino remembered the incident three years earlier, when Chiharu's notebook had been hidden, and gazed into the distance with a melancholy expression.

She walked a little faster along the quiet streets at night, smiling faintly.

“That's precisely why someone has to do something about it. Don't you think?”\
“Well, when you consider the principles our business is founded on, I suppose so. But there's no end to it.”\
“Shall we change careers, then? Just general services from now on?”\
“I'd love to, if I could. Unfortunately, the calls for help come from all over the country… well, the Kanto region, mainly Tokyo.”\
“Taking jobs from Hokkaido might be a little difficult for us, at least. Though I'd still do it.”

Human malice was surprisingly inexhaustible. Put an end to it in one place, and it would spring up somewhere else.

There was no way to cure it at its roots. All they could do was repeatedly treat the symptoms.

If someone hid something, they would find it and hold them accountable. If someone bullied another person, they would expose it and make sure someone dealt with it.

Over and over again.

“It's a little strange for me to ask, considering I'm the one who invited you, but Yuki, how come you've stuck with this for so long?”

Konatsu sounded genuinely impressed by Yukino's declaration that she would even take on jobs in Hokkaido.

Of course, that assumed the travel expenses would be covered. But perhaps she was also asking why Yukino hadn't refused when Konatsu first invited her to join the business.

“You were pretty forceful when you invited me. Though I suppose you're only asking that now.”

Yukino smiled wryly and gently breathed out a cloud of white vapour.

A passing car tore through it with its slipstream.

I live as a kind person, so that I can face the person I'll be tomorrow with my head held high.

Three and a half years earlier, Miyazono Chiharu had spoken those words to Yukino when explaining how she chose to live.

Her purpose wasn't to make other people happy. Rather, she made other people happy in order to make herself happy. It was a form of self-satisfaction.

Those words were still engraved in Yukino's heart, and so, lost in memories, she murmured:

“My condition makes people unhappy. So, in return, I'll make other people happy myself.”

That was the way of life Yukino had promised Chiharu she would follow at the summer festival three and a half years ago.

“But if I can use my condition to make someone happy, then there's nothing more I could ask for.”

As unusual as her condition was, it could be used for something rather like an unfairly powerful form of psychological profiling.

If she could tell when someone was lying, she could get to the heart of an incident and help resolve problems.

Investigating whether someone was having an affair. Finding the culprit behind a stalking incident. Looking into bullying.

It wasn't much use for finding lost cats, though.

“That's why you invited me, too, isn't it, Kona-chan?”

Yukino gave her a knowing grin through the phone.

From the other end came an amused laugh.

“Ha! Who knows? Maybe I just wanted to steal a little of your time with Chiharu.”\
“It's not like that sort of self-deprecating act is going to fool me anymore. Come on, be honest.”

Yukino heard a sigh of embarrassment through the phone and giggled to herself.

Just then, a gust of winter wind slipped through the gaps in her clothes.

A shiver ran through her, and a breath escaped her lips.

“It's coooold. Boss, it's freezing out here. Your employee has been working hard in this weather.”\
“Yeah, yeah. Unfortunately, we don't offer extreme-cold-weather allowances. Still, good work today. Are you coming by the office afterwards? I could at least treat you to dinner.”

Yukino looked apologetic as she quickened her pace and declined the offer.

“Ah, sorry. It's a special occasion today, so I'm going to have dinner at home with my girlfriend.”

Three and a half years had passed. Konatsu was no longer some defeated romantic heroine who would be thrown into turmoil by a remark like that.

She gave a single, frustrated growl.

Then, suddenly, her voice turned curious.

“Special occasion? You got together in the summer, didn't you? What's happening today?”\
“My birthday, obviously! My birthday! Mine! Did you forget? It's today.”

Yukino chuckled into the phone.

Her best friend, who somehow managed to forget Yukino's birthday almost every year, cried out, “Ah!” Then she laughed at herself for having forgotten again.

“Happy birthday, happy birthday.”\
“Thanks, thanks.”

Yukino laughed, thinking that last year had probably gone much the same way.

“Anyway, I'm heading straight home today. I'll submit the report later.”\
“Got it. Be happy together.”

It was an offhand expression of goodwill.

But for a moment, those words transported Yukino's heart back to that summer three years ago.

Happiness.

The definition of the word was still vague, but lately, she had found herself thinking about happiness less and less.

That wasn't because she was unhappy now. Quite the opposite.

People who are happy take it for granted. Just as those who live in a world of darkness don't think of the world as dark, those who live surrounded by happiness naturally come to regard it as the ordinary state of things.

Remembering how she used to mock herself for believing she could never be happy, Yukino felt a little embarrassed.

But then she reconsidered. The answer she had reached through all that suffering must surely have meant something, too.

She smiled.

“Yes. You too.”

*

She had moved house when she entered university. Surprisingly, her mother had agreed to act as her guarantor.

Her mother still spent very little time at home, but she had kept her promise and stopped bringing men back with her.

In fact, she had even begun setting aside time to talk with Yukino at least once a month.

By that point, Yukino had gradually come to realise that her mother didn't particularly like her, but disliked the rest of humanity even more.

In other words, relatively speaking, Yukino was among the people her mother liked quite a bit.

Once she had realised that, Yukino made a request.

Her mother had taken some of the compensation money from her unfaithful father's divorce settlement, deposited it into an account, and told Yukino, “Use it if you ever need it.”

Since then, she had not interfered with Yukino's life at all.

As for the new home she had moved into, it was an apartment building with six floors.

It was a little way outside central Tokyo, but the rent was surprisingly affordable considering how new and well-maintained the building was. Yukino was grateful for that.

It was a five-minute walk from the station. Not quite as close as one might hope, but certainly not bad.

After getting off the train and leaving the station, Yukino watched the students and office workers gathered in front of it with an almost suspiciously warm expression.

She followed her way home, entertaining the sort of idealistic thought one might expect from a comic-book superhero: I hope everyone is happy today, too.

Then she spotted a middle-aged woman with a distinctly dubious appearance standing in the station plaza.

“Do you believe in God?”

Remembering an encounter from long ago, Yukino smiled wryly and tried to hurry past her.

But by sheer coincidence, the middle-aged woman stepped directly in front of her and produced a pamphlet.

“Good evening. Do you believe in God?”\
“G-God?”\
“Yes, God. If you believe in God, you too can become happy…”

At that point, the middle-aged woman stopped speaking.

Yukino, too, widened her eyes and fell silent.

Something about her…

She felt as though she recognised her.

And the woman seemed to feel the same way.

They blinked at one another.

Then the woman looked Yukino up and down, from head to toe, before quietly putting the pamphlet away.

“…You already look quite happy enough.”

Apparently, she had a good eye for these things.

Yukino returned her smile.

“Yes,” she replied, giving a small bow.

That'll make an amusing story to tell when I get home.

Thinking that, Yukino arrived at her apartment building.

She adjusted her hair slightly using the glass panel in the lift, then, unable to contain her excitement, rose onto her toes as soon as she arrived at her floor.

Waving enthusiastically, she made her way down the familiar corridor.

Happiness…

Its shape was still vague in Yukino's mind.

But she now believed that it was something that unquestionably existed within her.

Even so, if someone asked her what it actually consisted of, she might struggle to answer.

But if she had to give some examples…

For instance, even a mother who seemed much colder than those of other families would, when asked, ultimately indulge her selfish requests. Sometimes she even gave her pocket money.

For instance, having a best friend. Someone who worked alongside her and took her condition into consideration.

For instance, having made a few friends at university. When she had gradually begun explaining her circumstances to them, they had accepted her.

For instance, receiving grateful smiles and words of thanks from clients through her detective and general services work.

That, perhaps, was a kind of happiness that would give her a reason to keep going for the rest of her life.

And then there was…

On a pitch-black winter night, she walked to the end of the apartment corridor, illuminated by the cold light of fluorescent lamps.

With hands stiff and numb from the cold, she took a spare key from her bag and unlocked the securely locked door.

When she opened it, a warm-coloured light was shining in the entrance hall.

The air flowing out from inside was pleasantly warm.

She stepped inside and locked the door behind her.

Then, just as the door leading to the living room at the far end of the hall opened…

“Welcome home.”

Her beloved girlfriend appeared.

That, without a doubt, was happiness.

Over the past three and a half years, Chiharu had grown roughly as much as Yukino, but Yukino still hadn't caught up with her height.

Her casually styled black hair was still cut to almost exactly the same length, and she remained as beautiful as ever.

She was wearing comfortable clothes with an apron over them, making it clear that she had been cooking until just a moment ago.

Yukino had experienced this scene countless times by now. She was accustomed to it, yet the joy of it never grew old.

In the entrance hall, where their lives had become intertwined, there were shoes that Yukino would never wear, and an umbrella they shared.

Yukino returned Chiharu's smile.

“I'm home! Your beloved Yukino-chan has returned!”

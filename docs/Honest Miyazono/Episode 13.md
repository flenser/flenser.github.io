The sixth-period lesson ended. In the brief interval before the homeroom meeting began, Aida left the classroom.

She was apparently going to the toilet. Yukino watched her disappear through the classroom door, then glanced towards Chiharu. She was clutching her bag and checking the train timetable on her phone, since she needed to hurry home to retrieve her notebook. They had often walked home together lately, but that would be difficult today.

Hasekura Yukino was in love with Miyazono Chiharu. She was grateful to her, from the bottom of her heart.

And that was precisely why she would deal with this matter alone.

Yukino quietly rose from her seat and left the classroom.

She spotted Aida walking leisurely towards the toilets, just as she had announced.

Yukino silently followed her from behind. Then, just as they reached a particular classroom, a girl stepped out through the door, her gaze sharp.

Yukino was taken aback.

The girl, too, widened her eyes when she saw Yukino.

It was Sayama Konatsu. Judging by their interactions at the sports festival committee meetings and earlier, over the textbook, she seemed to be an old friend of Chiharu's.

Konatsu, meanwhile, recognised Yukino and recalled what Chiharu had said on the evening of the sports festival gathering. The girl who had gone out to eat with Chiharu, leaving her own classmates behind. Her name was Hasekura Yukino, if she remembered correctly.

The two exchanged a glance, then continued after Aida without a word, quickening their pace.

After walking alongside one another for a while, they both looked at each other, wondering if perhaps...

Their eyes met squarely, and they could feel their suspicions growing stronger.

“Don't tell me... you've noticed it too?”

Konatsu asked, giving Yukino a sidelong glance. Yukino's eyes widened slightly.

“Could it be that you have a similar condition to mine?”\
“Huh? A condition?”

Konatsu gave her a look that plainly said, What on earth are you talking about? Yukino realised she'd jumped to conclusions.

They both slowed their pace a little, taking a moment to establish where the other stood before proceeding.

Yukino accepted that her assumption had been mistaken and tried to brush off her slip of the tongue.

“Forget I said that. But... Aida-san has Chiharu-san's notebook...”

Konatsu seemed rather deflated.

“Oh, so you know about that. Well, I don't have proof, but it's obviously suspicious.”\
“Yes. But how did you know?”

Asking while conveniently overlooking her own circumstances, Yukino received a dubious look from Konatsu. She covered her mouth and narrowed her eyes, then answered in a weary voice, concealing an unpleasant smile.

“Because I'm a liar. I can tell when someone else is lying. What about you?”

There was no taste of a lie in her words. So Konatsu probably didn't have a condition like Yukino's. Rather, she was simply good at spotting lies because she was a liar herself. She was a type of person Yukino had never encountered before.

Yukino swallowed her surprise and answered the question.

“Various circumstances. More importantly, may I assume you're also here to confront Aida-san?”\
“Of course. I don't have proof, though. I'm going to try and bluff her into confessing.”\
“Then would you mind leaving that part to me? I'm rather good at it.”

Yukino spoke as she gazed coolly at the approaching girls' toilets. Konatsu gave a dubious “Hmm,” then seemed satisfied for the moment.

“There's something I've wanted to tell you for a while, actually. Is that all right?”

Konatsu slowed her pace further, her voice taking on a dangerous edge. Yukino looked at her with a startled, “Y-yes?”

Konatsu's expression betrayed a hint of hesitation, but she was driven by complicated emotions as she spoke.

“...You're not good enough for Chiharu.”

Yukino's expression stiffened and her eyes widened. Konatsu stared at her sharply.

But a few seconds later, Yukino quietly narrowed her eyes, let her gaze drift into the distance, and gave a slight nod.

“I think so too.”

Konatsu's eyebrow twitched. She stared at Yukino in silence, waiting to hear what she meant.

It had clearly been a hostile warning, but Yukino's response was no lie. Konatsu genuinely thought they were mismatched. Unfortunately, Yukino agreed with her completely.

“Chiharu-san is an extraordinary person. She's clever, athletic, and capable. But that's not what matters most. She's hardworking, principled, kind, and sociable. She's not perfect, but she's someone who strives to do what's right. She saved me. I could never imagine that we're on equal footing.”

Konatsu's expression had initially been almost a glare, but as Yukino continued, the hostility gradually faded. Perhaps she, too, was someone who had been drawn to Chiharu in much the same way.

“...You care about Chiharu-san too, don't you?”

Konatsu's expression softened a little. Then she stopped walking. Before they knew it, they had reached their destination.

The two stood side by side at the entrance to the girls' toilets, where Aida had disappeared, and their eyes met.

“You make it sound as though you care about her yourself.”\
“Yes. She's someone very dear to me.”

And that was precisely why Yukino was prepared to keep her romantic feelings under lock and key. Her own existence must never be allowed to influence the course of Chiharu's life.

Yukino nodded without hesitation. Konatsu turned her distant gaze towards the empty space ahead and murmured.

“I see... I love Chiharu so much that I can't even put it into words like that. Romantically, I mean.”

The words were saturated with pure, unadulterated romantic affection. Yukino's eyes widened as she tasted them.

“But I don't particularly feel like fighting with you. There are other enemies to deal with.”

Konatsu watched Yukino, who was still dumbfounded, then pointed towards the toilets. She didn't seem interested in prolonging the conversation.

Yukino somehow managed to collect herself. Still struggling with what to do with the feelings she'd locked away after hearing Chiharu's words, she decided that, for now, she would simply speak to the person who was causing trouble for someone dear to her.

“I wouldn't go so far as to call them enemies, but... I agree. Shall we?”

They entered the girls' toilets together. Only one cubicle was occupied.

And almost simultaneously, just as Yukino and Konatsu exchanged glances, they heard the sound of a toilet flushing.

A few seconds later, Aida emerged from the cubicle, adjusting her fringe. When she saw the two of them, her shoulders jerked and she stumbled backwards.

“W-what? Wh-what is it?!”

She looked from one to the other, unable to conceal her bewilderment.

Konatsu glanced at Yukino, leaving the opportunity to her. Grateful for the gesture, Yukino met the eyes of Aida, who was still visibly startled and confused.

“Aida-san. I'd like to ask you a few questions about the whereabouts of Chiharu-san's notebook.”

Aida furrowed her brow suspiciously.

“Me? W-what? 【I don't know anything about it,】 so what's this all of a sudden?”

A faint sheen of sweat appeared on her forehead. She glared at Yukino as though trying to intimidate her.

But the lie tasted so horribly rancid that Yukino's face turned pale. She covered her mouth with one hand, as though struggling not to vomit, and pressed on with her questioning in a tone that was unnaturally calm.

“...Aida-san was the first person to return to the classroom after fifth period.”

That was no lie. Thinking back, Aida really had been the first to leave the classroom after the fifth-period lesson.

Of course, she might have stopped somewhere along the way, but Yukino deliberately made no mention of that possibility.

As expected, Aida didn't deny the uncertain part of Yukino's statement. Instead, her voice grew even sharper.

“S-so what?! Are you trying to say I stole it?! That's offensive!”

Aida snapped at her, making no attempt to hide her irritation. Yukino, however, pretended to think it over.

Konatsu, who shouldn't have known the details of when the notebook had been stolen, interjected with a shamelessly innocent question.

“That's strange. Why would you jump to that conclusion? 【I was going to ask whether, since you were the first one back in the classroom, you'd seen the moment it was stolen.】 Why are you getting so angry?”

It was such a blatantly innocent question that it was almost amusing, given what Konatsu was really like.

The lie tasted bitter, a foul mixture of disgust and resentment. It was like sewage laced with a large quantity of bitter medicine. The taste stirred Yukino's nausea, her stomach churning as though it were boiling. But she bit down on her lip and endured it, continuing the interrogation.

Aida realised she'd let something slip. The colour drained from her face.

“Uh... I mean...”

After mumbling incoherently for a moment, she forced a strained smile and softened her tone a little.

“...Oh, that's what you meant. You should've said so earlier. I didn't see anything.”\
“Is that so? But could you try to remember a little more? That can't be right. Because 【I saw the notebook there myself just before fifth period began.】”

Yukino delivered the lie with a face so pale that she could hardly bear to look at herself.

The theft had taken place in the interval between fifth and sixth periods. Aida, who had returned to the classroom first, had committed it.

Perhaps the gamble of making such a specific accusation had paid off. Aida's eyes darted conspicuously from side to side.

“I-is that so...? But 【I don't know anything about it】...”

If Aida stubbornly shut herself away behind a wall of denials, Yukino would be the one in trouble.

So Yukino offered her a false way out. Aida, sweating nervously, seized it with a smile.

“Ah! That's right! Sorry, I wasn't in the classroom!”

That was the truth. In other words, she had returned from the chemistry lab first, then immediately left the classroom again.

Hypothesis: If she was the one who had stolen the notebook, and had done so in the interval between fifth and sixth periods...

Conclusion: The place she had gone to must necessarily be where the notebook was hidden. That was what they needed to investigate.

Yukino forced a smile onto her pale face and clasped her hands before her chest, searching for a way forward.

“I see! Well, I'm terribly sorry for misunderstanding. I shouldn't have made assumptions without knowing... By the way.”

After smiling as though accepting Aida's version of events, Yukino let her smile fade and lowered her eyes to the floor, pretending to think.

“【You might have seen the culprit,】 so I'd like to ask you a few questions.”

It would be unnatural for Aida to refuse. Yukino already knew what her answer would be.

“【S-sure.】”

Aida forced a stiff smile.

“Did you leave the classroom to use the toilets, or something like that?”\
“Ah, y-yes! 【I had a bit of a stomach ache, so I went to the toilet and came straight back.】”

If it was true that she'd gone to the toilet, then the notebook was likely hidden there.

“I see... But 【I also stopped at the toilets on my way back to the classroom, and I seem to remember that all the cubicles were empty.】 Oh, could it... cough, cough, urgh... cough... perhaps... cough... have... been... another floor...?”

A large amount of saliva flooded Yukino's mouth. Even as she gagged, she desperately forced the words out.

Aida and Konatsu stared at her in alarm, but Yukino wasn't about to let them interrupt with concern.

She fixed Aida with a fierce gaze, waiting for her to answer the question. Overwhelmed, Aida replied.

“W-well, yes, but... What does that have to do with anything?”

It was true. In other words, the notebook wasn't hidden in the toilets on this floor.

If Yukino pointed out that it would be natural to use the toilets on the same floor after returning to the classroom, she would only corner Aida and make her clam up. For now, she would leave that suspicious detail unchallenged and continue without drawing attention to it.

The second-year classrooms were on the third floor. The chemistry lab was on the fourth. That meant the notebook was most likely hidden on the first or second floor.

The second floor had the first-year classrooms. Both sets of toilets there would see a reasonable amount of use.

But the first floor had no classrooms for students. Naturally, not many students would use its toilets between lessons. In particular, the eastern end had the open space where Yukino and Chiharu ate lunch. From there, Yukino had been able to see that very few people used the facilities.

What if the notebook was hidden in the toilets?

With an exhausted, half-lidded gaze, Yukino surveyed the third-floor toilets, whose layout was identical to those on the other floors.

Hiding it inside a cubicle would be risky. But what other hiding places were there...?

Then she noticed a narrow, narrow door. She opened it. Inside were cleaning supplies, haphazardly piled together.

*

“...Do you think the notebook might be hidden in the cleaning cupboard in the girls' toilets at the eastern end of the first floor?”

*

Aida and Konatsu were dumbfounded by Yukino's deduction.

She had skipped an astonishing number of steps in the investigation and jumped straight to the conclusion. Konatsu could only gape and let out a foolish little “Huh?”

But Aida, who had initially been stunned, soon broke out in goosebumps along the back of her neck. The colour drained from her face. Her lips trembled as her eyes darted about, and she frantically wiped her sweaty palms against her skirt.

“[N-no... I don't know? I mean, I don't know, but...]”

Seeing Aida's extremely obvious reaction, Konatsu was convinced that Yukino's deduction was correct. She stared in astonishment, her mouth hanging open.

And of course, Yukino understood that her guess was right too. Aida's reply was made entirely of lies.

There was no reason to continue speaking with her.

“Thank you for your cooperation.”

Yukino turned on her heel, her face so pale that she looked ready to vomit at any moment.

“W-wait!”

Aida reached out towards her.

But Konatsu caught her by the wrist, stopping her, then gave her a sharp glare before following Yukino.

As the two of them descended the stairs to the first floor together, Konatsu fixed Yukino with a questioning gaze.

“What was that? How did you know?”\
“Well... I don't think it's particularly important...”

Yukino gave an ambiguous smile, trying to brush the question aside. Konatsu responded with an unreservedly suspicious look.

But since whatever had happened had ultimately worked in their favour, it would be difficult to reproach her. Even so, the mystery of what had just taken place would probably keep Konatsu awake at night, or at least make her curious enough to think about it. She narrowed her eyes, lost in thought, then finally voiced the word that had been caught in her throat.

“Your condition.”

Yukino glanced at her.

“When I first suspected that girl was lying, you mentioned having a condition. You also said you were good at interrogations. A condition that lets you tell when people are lying? And then there's the fact that you've looked terribly unwell this whole time. I've seen it before. The first time we spoke, you were about to throw up in the first-floor toilets. And you said Chiharu had saved you. There's a rumour that Chiharu was instrumental in dealing with the Shinohara voyeurism incident, and lately you two have been spending a lot of time together. That gathering after the sports festival, too. I wondered whether you must have a particularly special relationship to arrange to go out together, just the two of you, rather than with your classmates. But that's not it. No... Perhaps it is, on top of everything else. If you can tell when people are lying, and the price is that you suffer from nausea, and Chiharu is willing to help you now that she knows your secret, then everything fits.”

Her powers of deduction were astonishing. Yukino regretted giving her so much material to work with.

She returned a quiet sideways glance, her expression an ambiguous smile of regret.

“You've been reading too much manga.”\
“Would it be better if I left it at that?”\
“...I'm surprised. I thought you'd press me harder.”\
“If it's being used in a way that benefits Chiharu, I have no intention of condemning it. I just find it uncomfortable to leave something I don't understand unexplained. I want to know the truth, that's all.”\
“I see. Then... yes. Please leave it as a case of having read too much manga.”

There was no point in trying to evade her any further.

Yukino asked her to let the matter rest, and Konatsu thoughtfully pinched her chin. Yukino tensed, wondering whether she was about to propose some sort of bargain.

Instead, Konatsu pointed at herself.

“Sayama Konatsu. That's my name.”

Yukino stared at her, mouth hanging open in surprise. A moment later, she remembered that they hadn't actually introduced themselves.

“H-Hasekura Yukino. Pleased to meet you, Sayama-san.”

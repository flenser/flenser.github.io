The two of them descended to the first floor and made their way to the girls’ toilets at the eastern end of the building.

After entering and confirming that all the cubicles were empty, they opened the cleaning cupboard.

Inside were a tap for filling buckets, several buckets stacked haphazardly nearby, and the mops and brooms that had been put away. Naturally, at first glance, there seemed to be nothing hidden there. But…

“I’ll do it.”

Until now, Konatsu had left the interrogation entirely to Yukino. Now she stepped in front of her and took charge of the search instead. She methodically exposed one blind spot after another, rummaging through the cupboard, until she found a notebook behind a bucket.

“So it really was here.”

Mathematics. And the name Miyazono Chiharu. After confirming both, the two of them let out a sigh.

“Why would anyone do something this stupid?”

Konatsu shrugged, looking exasperated, and handed the notebook to Yukino, leaving its return to her.

Yukino accepted it, her eyes lowered sadly, and offered her own theory as someone who had observed the various events unfold.

“I think she didn’t want Chiharu-san to come to karaoke.”

The answer was so unexpected that Konatsu was left gaping. Not only was the explanation surprising, but so was the fact that Yukino had been able to arrive at it. After several seconds, she knitted her brows and exclaimed in a voice that had gone rather shrill.

“H-huh? What’s that supposed to mean?”\
“Today, Chiharu-san had made plans to go out after school with a group of people she’s usually quite close to. I’ll spare you the details, out of consideration for Aida-san’s reputation, but… Chiharu-san is a wonderful person. One of the boys in that group might have feelings for her. Or perhaps, if he already does, spending time together like that might bring them closer. If Aida-san thought…”

There would be nothing particularly strange about this situation. Yukino spoke with a bitter expression, and Konatsu picked up where she left off.

“…Then she could hide the notebook that was due today, make Chiharu go back to retrieve it after school, and keep her from joining them at karaoke. Afterwards, she could quietly put the notebook back on her desk. If Chiharu couldn’t find it at home, she might come back to school to look for it. But…”

Konatsu paused.

“That only works if nothing comes of it, right? If it turned into a big incident, she’d be in trouble too.”\
“So you think she had some other motive?”\
“Otherwise, it’s just too stupid. Like I said, it’s a fool’s plan.”\
“I see. So you don’t think she would have carried out a plan with so many obvious holes in it.”

Yukino responded with an uncertain smile, almost as though she were merely echoing Konatsu’s words. Konatsu seemed to understand what she was getting at. Her face twisted into a strained smile.

“She’s an idiot,” she muttered, pressing a hand to her forehead before letting out a deep sigh.

Of course, everything Yukino had said was only speculation. But it was speculation based on having heard the conversation between Aida, Tanabe and Chiharu in the classroom, and on Yukino’s ability to recognise lies.

“In any case, thank you for your help, Sayama-san.”\
“I didn’t do anything. Honestly, you would’ve managed just fine without me.”\
“No, that’s…”

Yukino firmly disagreed with a smile. Konatsu tilted her head, looking at her suspiciously.

“When I discover that someone is hiding something like this, I’ve always tried to do something about it. I’m not very good at this sort of thing, though, so most of the time I can’t get anywhere on my own. But if someone else is there, perhaps things might go a little more smoothly. Just having someone with me makes me feel better.”

Yukino recalled, gazing into the distance, how Chiharu had reached out to her when she had tried to shoulder the entire burden of the hidden-camera incident involving Shinohara-sensei.

Seeing Yukino’s expression, Konatsu seemed to sense that something like that had happened.

“Hm.” Her lips curled into a faint smile, tinged with just a little regret, but also warmth. “Well, I’ll accept your thanks, then. The notebook’s up to you.”

“Of course. I’ll make sure it’s returned safely.”\
“What about that girl? We don’t have any physical evidence that she stole it, but…”\
“I intend to keep it from Chiharu-san. She probably won’t do it again.”

Yukino proposed letting Aida off without consequence.

Naturally, she was willing to discuss it if Konatsu objected, but Konatsu seemed prepared to hear her out. With a puzzled expression, she asked rather brusquely:

“Why?”

Yukino had just parted her lips to answer when a trembling cry rang out from the entrance.

*

“There! There you are! Look, you’ve got the notebook! [I knew you were suspicious!]”

*

Yukino and Konatsu were both taken aback. Standing at the entrance were Aida and Yamazaki.

And finally, Miyazono Chiharu, the person whose notebook had been stolen in the first place, stood behind them, looking astonished.

“The notebook…? Are you serious? You’re serious. Hasekura, and you’re Sayama, right?”\
“Yukino… and Konatsu. What are the two of you doing here?”

Chiharu showed no sign of suspecting either of them. She simply seemed unable to comprehend the sight of two friends of hers, who were close to each other in entirely separate circles, standing together and chatting amicably while holding her notebook. Her eyes widened, and her gaze wavered with confusion as she asked her question in a bewildered voice.

But then she looked carefully at the two of them, narrowing her eyes as she seemed to realise something. She glanced at Aida and put a hand to her forehead.

“Ahhh…”

Apparently, Chiharu had grasped the whole situation. So much for the plan, Konatsu seemed to say with her half-lidded glance at Yukino. Yukino, meanwhile, turned an awkward smile towards empty space, her momentum thoroughly broken.

Aida, sweating profusely and with no room to spare for the unspoken exchange between the others, desperately tried to shift all the blame onto them with a stiffly pasted-on smile.

“[Y-you know, they were acting all secretive together! So I thought they were suspicious!]”

And so, the accuser had suddenly become a suspect.

Faced with such an inept attempt, Konatsu let out a dry, exasperated laugh.

“Haha. I see. So Hasekura really was right. You’re a genuine idiot.”\
“P-please don’t say that! I never said anything like that…”

Yukino’s eyes widened in protest. It was almost as though she herself had directly called Aida an idiot.

“Hm?” Konatsu gave her a teasing, innocent look.

Chiharu, watching the exchange, said just one thing.

“…You two seem awfully close.”

It was a look of jealousy, the first Yukino had ever seen on Chiharu’s face. She had puffed out her cheeks a little, looking almost sulky.

But this was hardly a situation in which they could simply stand around chatting. Yamazaki, who had seen the notebook stolen from her friend, frowned and questioned Yukino.

“Wait, wait. Why are you two chatting away so happily? Hasekura, did you really hide the notebook?”

The person who tried to object first, before anyone else, was Chiharu.

But Konatsu stepped in front of Yukino, hooked her thumb into the pocket of her skirt, and cut her off.

“Let me ask you something instead. Have you considered the possibility that we found the notebook where it had been hidden?”\
“You’re being roundabout. I want you to answer the question.”\
“‘We didn’t hide it.’ There, I answered your question. Now we know who the culprit is.”\
“N-no, I mean, then why are you here?”

Konatsu’s hostility came from her irritation at having to indulge such a ridiculous charade.

Yamazaki, on the other hand, was trying to maintain a sense of fairness. Yet, consciously or otherwise, she was already operating on the assumption that Aida was telling the truth, directing suspicious looks at the others. Under those circumstances, simply denying the accusation would accomplish nothing. What was needed now was not emotion, but reason.

“See? It’s pointless asking questions if you’re not going to listen to the answers. If you’re not just there for decoration, try using your own head. I’ll ask again. Have you considered the possibility that we found the notebook where it had been hidden?”

As the conversation circled back to the original question, Aida realised that staying silent would make things worse. Sweat poured from her so heavily that it soaked through her blouse. She forced a smile and raised her voice, which cracked as she spoke.

“W-wait! Just wait…! Um, does the situation even matter?! What matters is that Miyazono-san’s notebook was hidden, and that those two have it!”

Konatsu twisted her mouth into a smile and looked down at Aida with contempt.

“Shut up, idiot. I pity the oxygen wasted on you.”\
“Could you stop calling my friend an idiot?”

Yamazaki was defending Aida. But presumably without realising it, she made no attempt to address the sheer absurdity of Aida’s argument.

“Two idiots, then. Is that really all you have to say after hearing that ridiculous nonsense?”\
“P-please, both of you, calm down. Sayama-san, you’re going too far.”

Yukino intervened, admonishing Konatsu, who was clearly getting carried away. At the same time, Chiharu tried to calm Yamazaki.

Once the argument had settled down, Yukino and Chiharu exchanged glances.

From Yukino’s perspective, Chiharu had already worked out the truth. Yet she was leaving it to Yukino to decide how to reveal it. Why? Yukino wondered, but decided to assume it was an expression of trust.

As expected, Chiharu had already reached the correct conclusion.

Aida had stolen the notebook. Yukino had her unusual condition, while Konatsu had her powers of observation, and the two of them had independently uncovered the same thing before joining forces. After that, they had confronted Aida and discovered where she had hidden the notebook. In a panic, Aida had resorted to what was known as stealing thunder: volunteering information that would otherwise work against her in order to make herself seem more trustworthy.

And that was how they had arrived at the present situation. But now that her scheme had been seen through, it was worse than useless. She had only dug herself deeper.

However, Chiharu had sensed from their exchange that Yukino and Konatsu were trying to accomplish something beyond merely defending themselves. She therefore judged that it would be better not to interfere. Instead, she chose to let Yukino write the final chapter of this little drama.

Their acquaintance was still relatively new, and yet they seemed to understand each other’s thoughts simply by meeting each other’s eyes. Chiharu’s expression softened ever so slightly. Yukino, her face still pale, returned a faintly embarrassed smile and nodded.

“Hey, Yukino. [I don’t know the truth], so I just want to ask you one thing.”

Chiharu mixed in a lie, accompanied by a twinge of guilt. Even so, Yukino’s expression softened for an instant, looking almost apologetic.

“Did you hide my notebook?”

Chiharu was leaving the decision entirely to Yukino, allowing her to determine how she wanted this situation to end.

While Aida bit her lip and struggled to catch her breath, Yukino placed a hand against her chest and took several shallow, measured breaths. Then she glanced at Konatsu, an apology in her eyes, and answered.

*

“[Yes. I hid Chiharu-san’s textbook.]”

*

It was a bolt from the blue. Aida and Yamazaki were both left speechless.

Aida’s mouth opened and closed repeatedly, as though she had no idea what had just happened. Yamazaki frowned, unable to shake her suspicions, and searched Yukino’s face for the truth.

Konatsu, Sayama Konatsu, was plainly unconvinced, but she did not challenge the decision of the person who had done the real work. Chiharu, too, accepted it without expression, having more or less anticipated that Yukino might choose this course.

But behind that blank expression, Chiharu was considering Yukino’s intentions. She had a suspicion of what lay behind them.

Why would Yukino protect Aida here? What could she possibly gain from it?

There was nothing to gain. At least, not for Yukino.

But there was something in it for Chiharu. It meant she would be spared the emotional burden of having to condemn someone who was, at least nominally, her friend. Her impression of Aida would not change; she would still know what sort of person she was. But having to confront her and risk making their relationship awkward would be painful and troublesome.

And so Yukino had taken that bitterness upon herself. Half of it, perhaps. No, closer to seventy per cent.

The rest she had, without even realising it, left to Konatsu, who had somehow become close to her as well.

What a pair of foolish friends.

Chiharu muttered the words silently to herself. And yet, at the same time, she felt that these friends of hers were more precious than anything. She closed her eyes, as though savouring the feeling, and let the corners of her mouth curl upwards behind the hand concealing her cheek.

To honour the kindness they had shown her, she decided to accept it and keep quiet.

*

“I see. [You’re the worst.]”

*

It was a sentiment that would be properly conveyed precisely because it was directed at Yukino, who could recognise lies.

Chiharu’s words of condemnation were delivered with a gentle, softened expression. Yukino smiled, lowering the corners of her eyebrows as though admitting defeat. Konatsu looked away with an air of resignation, while Yamazaki remained silent, her expression full of unspoken questions.

At last, Yukino handed the notebook to Chiharu, who accepted it without hesitation.

“[Thank you for telling me, Aida-san.]”

I suppose it was for one of the boys in the group, Chiharu thought, as she put on a smile.

“Ah, u-um… yeah! It’s fine! Really! Don’t worry about it…!”

Aida, utterly flustered, could barely get the words out. Yamazaki, too, finally seemed to understand what was happening.

“[That’s how it is, then, Goldilocks.]”

Konatsu spoke with a frivolous expression after seeing Yamazaki’s face. Yamazaki nodded, looking troubled.

“[Right, right. I see how it is.] Come on, Aida. Let’s go.”

“You really are an idiot. This is what they mean by digging your own grave…”

Five seconds after the trio had left the toilets, Kohatsu began, sighing in exasperation.

But almost simultaneously, Yukino dashed into a cubicle. The instant she was inside—

“Ghk, cough… Ugh… Uuugh!”

The sound of her lunch, mixed with stomach acid, splattering into the water in the toilet bowl echoed through the room. Kohatsu’s face twisted in dismay. With an expression of concern, she scratched her head and stood there, unsure what to do.

“...Are you all right? Is that, what, a side effect of your condition?”\
“H-ha… Ugh, ugh… y-yes… Ghk, cough! Ugh…”

Yukino tried to answer immediately, even though she should have waited until she had settled down, and promptly retched again.

A few seconds later, there was another splattering sound, like the last drops after a long stream.

“Haa… haa… Th-that’s… ugh… cough… yes…”\
“D-don’t force yourself to talk. I can wait a little.”

Kohatsu leaned against the washbasin, folded her arms, and waited several dozen seconds, her expression still somewhat strained. At last, Yukino managed to compose herself.

After a brief silence, Yukino spoke quietly.

“I’m sorry. I made you look like the bad guy too, Sayama-san.”

Hearing her voice from inside the cubicle, Kohatsu gave her a dubious look, gazed off into space and shrugged.

“It's fine. The only person who believes that ending is the idiot herself. What I’m more curious about is what you hoped to achieve with such an obvious lie. Why did you take the blame?”\
“...Even though everyone there knew the truth, Aida-san was officially cleared of any wrongdoing.”\
“And?”\
“That way, they can still be friends.”

The toilet flushed. After that brief, perfectly legitimate silence, Kohatsu swallowed her exasperation.

“It’s a sham. From that point on, their friendship will be nothing but a worthless facade.”\
“Yes. But I doubt Aida-san will ever do anything like this again, now that she’s been given a fright. Besides, there’s no decisive physical evidence against her. And… even if their friendship looks worthless to us, it might still mean a great deal to Chiharu-san.”

Yukino gazed down at the now-clean water in the toilet bowl, smiling as she reminisced.

“Chiharu-san has given me so much. I wanted to repay her, even if only a little.”

A sigh of exasperation seeped into the silence of the toilets.

“You really are a strange one.”\
“Haha. I know. I doubt I’ll ever be able to live a happy life.”\
“Happiness, huh… Well, being able to tell when people are lying must be miserable enough. And with a personality like yours on top of that…”

The cubicle door opened, and Yukino emerged, her face pale and her steps unsteady.

She looked intently at Kohatsu, who was standing by the washbasin with her arms folded. Kohatsu furrowed her brow suspiciously.

“What? Is something wrong?”\
“...No. It just reminded me of how Chiharu-san brought me a bottle of water.”

Remembering the past, Yukino made the remark with a slightly playful expression. Kohatsu gave her a half-lidded smile.

“Haha. You're a demanding woman, aren't you? Rinse your mouth with the tap water. The chlorine will disinfect it.”\
“Hehe. I rather like that. It means I don't have to hold back with you.”

Yukino went to the washbasin, cupped some water in her hands and rinsed her mouth, as though recalling that earlier occasion.

Having learnt that Chiharu had once gone so far as to buy Yukino a bottle of water, Kohatsu gazed into the distance.

“Chiharu's basically strict about people letting her down, but she's soft-hearted in the end. That girl.”\
“Yes, I agree. I think she understood what I was trying to do this time.”\
“Probably. Otherwise, she'd have made sure Aida properly regretted it. She's surprisingly unforgiving when she wants to be.”

The two of them chatted about their mutual friend.

Another ten seconds of silence passed. Yukino dried her hands with a paper towel.

Watching her from the side, Kohatsu gazed into space for a long while, apparently choosing her words carefully, before finally speaking.

“Hey, Hasekura. Do you like Chiharu? As in, as a girl?”

Yukino stopped drying her hands and stared at Kohatsu's back through the mirror, her eyes wide.

Then, after glancing sideways to check her expression, she slowly closed her eyes and nodded.

“...Yes.”

She opened her eyes again, thinking of the moon hidden from view, then folded the damp paper towel and threw it away.

“But she doesn't want to compromise the course of her own life. So I'll keep my feelings to myself.”\
“That's for the best. Most people aren't worthy of her. They'd only hold her back.”

Yukino rested her hands on the washbasin and watched the droplets falling from the automatic tap.

A drop fell and dissolved against the artificial marble. After watching it, Yukino asked:

“Is that why you haven't confessed to Chiharu-san?”

It was a question that brought them a little closer, but Kohatsu didn't reject it.

Surprisingly, her tone remained unchanged. Still with her arms folded, she nodded matter-of-factly.

“Something like that. We wouldn't be a good match, and I don't think she's the sort of person who'd want a romantic relationship.”

Then she casually added:

“But if, by some miracle, there were even the slightest chance… I'd confess.”

It was a sweet declaration, steeped in genuine romantic feeling, without the slightest hint of a lie.

The girl in the mirror opened her eyes wide in astonishment, then narrowed them with a mixture of admiration, envy and jealousy.

If Chiharu ever did become interested in romance, would Yukino be able to bring herself to confess?

“Then, Hasekura. If that day ever comes, I won't hold back, and I won't show you any mercy. I'll oppose you with everything I've got and do whatever it takes to beat you.”

Without paying any heed to Yukino's hesitation, Kohatsu made her declaration of war.

If Chiharu were someone who could fall in love with another woman, surely she would be far happier with Kohatsu than with Yukino. Yukino turned her faint self-loathing into a smile, and was just about to nod darkly—

“So you should, too. When that time comes, fight tooth and nail to win Chiharu over.”

She stopped.

Startled, she turned her entire face towards Kohatsu's profile. Kohatsu glanced back at her from the corner of her eye.

After several seconds of silence and exchanged glances, Yukino, still unable to shake her surprise, managed a flippant remark.

“I-I'm astonished. I thought you'd left your sense of sportsmanship in your mother's womb.”\
“I'm astonished myself. But, well, you're not quite as bad a person as I'd thought.”

Kohatsu gave a faint, self-deprecating laugh, folded her arms and gazed into the distance as though bemused by her own behaviour.

Yukino had the impression that Sayama Kohatsu was not necessarily a good person. Of course, she possessed a proper sense of legality and ethics, but if being good meant showing kindness and consideration towards others, then Kohatsu did not seem to fit the description. Yukino had assumed that, if it meant achieving her goal of winning Chiharu's love, Kohatsu would have no hesitation about crushing Yukino underfoot.

That was precisely why Yukino couldn't conceal her surprise at what she'd just heard.

But gradually, her astonishment gave way to a faint smile. Eventually, she looked away and murmured.

Looking ahead, the girl in the mirror seemed happy. For the first time, she had found someone with whom she could exchange words on equal terms, without having to hold back.

“Perhaps… we could become friends.”\
“Ugh, gross. Sorry, I'm allergic to youth and all its nonsense.”

Kohatsu hugged herself, pretending to shudder as she spoke in a half-laughing voice. Yukino closed her eyes and chuckled.

“I suffer from a severe deficiency of youth. You'll have to put up with me.”\
“Friends, huh? That's not the sort of relationship you need to go out of your way to declare. It's embarrassing.”\
“I'm making Chiharu-san worry. I don't have many friends, you see.”\
“So, if I become your friend, there's a chance I can prise you away from Chiharu?”

That was probably what she genuinely thought, though it might also have been a way of hiding her embarrassment. She needed an excuse, because saying something as straightforward as ‘friends’ was too embarrassing.

Kohatsu gave Yukino a mischievous, half-lidded look from the corner of her eye. Yukino tilted her head and smiled back.

“That's right… much to my regret.”\
“Let's talk about our primary school days, then, my dearest best friend.”\
“We went to different schools. That conversation would end rather quickly.”

The usual open space at the eastern end of the first-floor corridor. The two of them had spread their lunch boxes out on the bench.

Yukino glanced at Chiharu, who was sitting beside her with her head bowed pensively, and lowered her own head, feeling just as dejected.

But then, at last, Chiharu forced out a few words, heavy with self-loathing.

“I'm sorry about earlier. For showing you that side of me. Half of it was me taking my frustration out on him.”

Chiharu bit her lip, knitting her brows. Seeing this, Yukino raised her own brows and replied with unexpected force.

“No, not at all! I… wasn't feeling particularly good about it either. I was glad you said something. Even if half of it was taking your frustration out on him, the other half was kindness. And I was half glad, too, so that makes us… even!”

She raised a finger, though by this point she had no idea what she was saying. At last, Chiharu's expression softened into an amused smile.

“What was that supposed to be?”

Yukino smiled back, embarrassed. “I have no idea.”

They put the matter behind them and began eating.

As usual, Chiharu had made their lunch herself. There was plenty of chicken and root vegetables simmered together, with the remaining space filled by a colourful assortment of side dishes, chosen with an eye to the nutrients and colours that were otherwise lacking.

While Yukino ate her white rice sprinkled with sesame and salt, Chiharu stopped moving her chopsticks and gazed into the distance, lost in thought.

Something was still wrong. Yukino knew Chiharu was troubled by Konatsu's confession, but there was something peculiar about the anger she had shown earlier, something that suggested more than mere worry. Of course, Miyazono Chiharu might be a paragon of virtue, but she wasn't a machine. She could get angry when someone rubbed her the wrong way. Even so, she would normally handle it a little more gracefully.

“Has something happened…?”

After several seconds of opening and closing her mouth in indecision, Yukino finally summoned the courage to ask.

Chiharu's shoulders twitched as she came back to herself. She looked at Yukino with slightly widened eyes, then narrowed them and turned away.

Yukino had intended not to press if Chiharu didn't want to talk about it. But Chiharu squeezed her eyes shut. Her inner conflict was plain to see. Yukino didn't know what secret she was concealing, or why, but she could tell that Chiharu was struggling with whether she ought to share it. Even so, Chiharu finally steeled herself and spoke.

“Konatsu said something similar to me, too. She asked whether this relationship of ours was really just sympathy.”

Yukino's eyes wavered. She pressed her lips together and, after several seconds of silence, asked in a trembling voice:

“Was that… when she… confessed to you?”\
“Yeah. That's why it made me even angrier. He doesn't know anything about us.”

It was true that Ota knew nothing about their relationship, and had casually chosen his words simply to praise Chiharu.

But what about Konatsu?

The moment Yukino considered that, a dark foreboding settled over her face.

Konatsu knew. She knew what Chiharu and Yukino were to one another, and she knew Yukino's secret. If, knowing all that, she had still raised the possibility that Chiharu's feelings were merely sympathy, then it was an entirely different matter from Ota's remark. Even allowing for the possibility that she had made the accusation as a ploy to further her own romantic hopes, it was still persuasive enough to deserve consideration.

“But Sayama-san knows about us, doesn't she? And what did you say to her? What… do you think about it?”

Worried, Yukino wiped her sweaty palm against her skirt and asked, the corners of her brows drooping.

Looking at Yukino's face, Chiharu put on a gentle smile, shrugged and turned her gaze away before answering.

*

“[There's nothing to agonise over. I simply wanted to be as sincere as I could, as Konatsu's friend—]”

*

Her voice gradually faded as she reached the end of the sentence, then broke off altogether, as though the words had disintegrated in mid-air.

Yukino's eyes had widened little by little as she listened. Her expression twisted. The taste of an unconscious lie flooded her mouth, and she nearly brought up the mouthful of simmered vegetables she had just swallowed.

Seeing Yukino's reaction, and recognising for herself the falseness of what she had just said, Chiharu covered her mouth.

Five seconds of silence, their eyes fixed on each other.

Another five seconds. Chiharu tried to say something, but no words came. Silence again.

Then, for a final five seconds, Yukino tried desperately to force a smile in an attempt to ease the tension. But her expression only stiffened. Chiharu realised beyond doubt that her words had been a lie, and the two of them fell silent together.

Yukino's face wavered between a crooked smile and a blank expression. She looked away and tried to think.

So, can you make Chiharu happy yourself?

Someone who knows about your condition might refrain from lying to spare your feelings.

Konatsu's words from the day she had declared herself Yukino's rival echoed in her mind, striking her straight in the heart.

She could feel the friction. A conversation that should have gone smoothly, even if only on the surface, as though nothing were wrong, had ground to a halt. It was like stepping on a surface that suddenly lost its grip. The friction that had vanished so abruptly gave rise to heat, to discord. Yukino's face twisted. She regretted not pretending she hadn't noticed.

She was experiencing the worst aspect of her condition: knowing when someone was lying.

Chiharu Miyazono is troubled. She's wondering whether the feelings she has for me are merely sympathy.

As far as Yukino was concerned, it wouldn't matter if Chiharu's feelings were sympathy. Of course, that would be different if Chiharu herself wanted to avoid such a relationship. But even if her feelings were false, Yukino hoped they could cultivate something genuine together from that point onwards.

Yet now she had caught sight of a gathering darkness: the possibility that this very future might make Chiharu unhappy.

No.

Yukino grimaced and bit her lip. No. She didn't want to admit it. Wasn't it a trivial matter? Lies, of all things. People ought to live honestly. If they didn't, then surely the fault lay with the person telling the lie…

She kept falling back on such pathetic attempts to shift the blame, thoughts she had never entertained before. Then, with clouded eyes and mounting desperation, she spoke.

“Um, well… Oh! How about I make lunch from now on?”

The gears slipped. They no longer meshed. In her desperation, Yukino was searching for even the smallest way she might make Chiharu happy. But to Chiharu, the suggestion sounded as though Yukino had defined their relationship as one founded on sympathy.

“No, wait, Yukino. You don't have to do that. We…”

Chiharu's expression twisted as though she had been hurt. Her voice, unusually flustered, was desperate as she tried to stop her.

“[No, I…! I don't think it's right for me to be the only one receiving things from you.]”

They weren't connecting. The gears had slipped out of alignment under the friction. The mechanism was gradually grinding to a halt, pressure building as it did.

The heat of their discord continued to accumulate. This was the sort of misunderstanding, the sort of incident, that could always arise in a relationship where nothing could be kept hidden, where the truth lay exposed and unprotected.

*There's nothing to agonise over. I simply wanted to be sincere with Konatsu.*

If Chiharu had told that lie, then continued to consider the matter privately, everything should have been resolved. It should have been that simple. How had things come to this?

Chiharu tried to analyse the cause with her thoughts running hot from panic, only for them to arrive immediately at Yukino Hasekura's condition. Her face twisted as she desperately tried to turn away from it.

Seeing Chiharu's anguished expression, Yukino could feel her own heart pounding faster and faster.

So, can you make Chiharu happy yourself?

At the very least, the person before her did not look happy. And when Yukino asked herself why Chiharu wore such an expression, only one answer presented itself.

A relationship without lies as its lubricant would always be subject to friction.

Yukino had understood this intellectually. And yet, for some reason, the romantic feelings she had developed had deliberately turned her eyes away from it. Now Konatsu Sayama had forced her to look directly at it. Konatsu was merely the catalyst. The true cause lay entirely within Hasekura Yukino herself, and the distortion was growing ever larger.

As though abruptly tearing through the long, long silence, Chiharu finally forced out a few words, her eyes unsteady and her composure gone.

“Anyway, [there's really nothing to worry about with Konatsu—]”

Her voice dwindled as she struggled to finish the sentence. Then her expression twisted more violently than before.

It was an excuse, offered to keep the other person from worrying. But beneath it lurked the feeling that she did not want that person to know what she truly thought. The desire to conceal something. And among the complex emotions that usually blurred together, one stood out with particular clarity: an acrid taste, suggestive of fear and anguish.

Perhaps Chiharu had realised for herself that she was lying. Her face twisted as she tried to explain.

“That's not what I…”

But Yukino interrupted her with an “Um…”, as though pretending she hadn't heard a thing.

“I'm s-sorry… I mean, um, this must be difficult for you to talk about.”

At Yukino's stiff, strained and awkward smile, Chiharu bit her lip, unable to find any words of explanation.

“[No, it's not—]”

Chiharu's voice grew hoarse. Her face crumpled, and she covered it with her hand.

Yukino's eyes widened. Then she gave a smile, as though something tightly stretched between them had finally snapped.

The taste of a lie was awful. Like swallowing a whole lump of sticky bitterness and acidity.

But that alone could not explain the overwhelming nausea now stirring in Yukino's stomach.

She fought desperately to hold back her vomit. Saliva flooded her mouth, and she kept her lips firmly shut to contain it. Pressing a hand to her chest, she tried to suppress the nausea, her mouth still closed as she gave repeated, cough-like retches.

It was an entirely ordinary form of communication. And yet, stripped of the lubricant of lies, the mechanism generated friction. Friction meant heat and wear, both of which could be called discord.

Pain was reflected in the eyes with which they looked at one another.

Yukino swallowed down the stomach acid rising in her throat, then forced a smile that made her cheeks twitch.

“Um… Would it be all right if we took a little time to think things over? Both of us?”

Chiharu's expression crumpled, anguish showing through. It was the first time Yukino had seen her look as though she might cry.

Like a child throwing a tantrum when nothing went right, Chiharu's eyes grew slightly moist. She bit her lip, swallowing her frustration and helplessness. Then, looking utterly miserable, she gave a shallow nod.

She quickly closed her lunch box, which she had barely opened, and left with a single “I'm going.”

Chiharu's back looked even more slender than usual as she walked away, as though dragging her heavy feet behind her.

Once, Yukino would have hurried after that retreating figure and taken her hand. But now, she couldn't even do that.

So, can you make Chiharu happy yourself?

Konatsu Sayama's words repeated in her mind like a curse.

Yukino still didn't know the answer to that question. But there was one thing she had realised.

Konatsu Sayama might be able to make Miyazono Chiharu happier than Hasekura Yukino could.

*

Chiharu cried at school for the first time in her life. It happened while she was eating her lunch in secret in the toilets.

She carefully soaked up her tears with a handkerchief, then dampened it and wiped her face once she had stopped crying. She had done everything she could to prevent her eyes from swelling, and by the time she left the toilets, her face should have been back to its usual Miyazono Chiharu.

She was climbing the stairs to the third floor at a sluggish pace, on her way back to the classroom, when she encountered someone.

The lunch break was already about eighty per cent over. Here and there, students hurried along the corridor.

Leaning against the wall beside the windows stood Konatsu Sayama. Her silky golden hair, gleaming beautifully in the light of the early afternoon, swayed in time with her nodding head. She was standing with her arms folded, looking sleepy and vaguely distracted. Chiharu had often seen her like this in their first year. Konatsu frequently spent her lunch breaks napping.

Wondering why she was dozing here, Chiharu approached. Konatsu's amber eyes opened.

When she noticed Chiharu and turned her gaze towards her, her eyelids seemed to widen just a little.

“What's wrong, Konatsu? What are you doing here?”

Chiharu didn't think her voice had trembled. She had wiped away her tears properly, and Konatsu didn't seem to have noticed. Without bringing it up, Konatsu scratched her head awkwardly.

“I was wondering if you were all right.”

Chiharu's mouth fell open in surprise. Then she slowly sighed and gave a wry smile.

“…Honestly. And whose fault do you think this is?”\
“The fact that you don't call me the cause says a lot about your character.”\
“It's not as though you actually are the cause. You're just the catalyst.”

If Konatsu had kept interfering, then it would have been fair to call her the cause.

But she had raised the question in order to interfere, then stepped back. All she had done was strip away the deception that had been covering something up. Whatever her intentions might have been, the underlying cause was that deception itself.

Seeing Chiharu accept this without shifting the blame, Konatsu gave a faint smile and clasped her hands behind her back.

“I wasn't trying to make you suffer.”

It was an excuse. Even so, Chiharu had no intention of hating or resenting her.

“I know. I don't think you're in the wrong. Everyone has the right to express their feelings. Or, at least, I think they do. All you did was raise a question about my feelings in the process. The problem is me. Even I can't clearly distinguish between sympathy and romantic love, and I've been aware of that while deliberately looking the other way. I thought that was all right.”\
“I don't think that's necessarily… wrong.”\
“Coming from you?”

Chiharu retorted with a half-lidded stare. Konatsu looked away with a complicated expression, evidently aware of the irony.

Chiharu couldn't help giving a little laugh.

She understood. Konatsu had voiced her doubts about a love that had not yet come to fruition, all in the service of her own romantic feelings. In effect, she was saying: If it really is only sympathy, then surely you could put your own feelings aside and give me a chance.

And she was leaving the choice to Chiharu.

It was an emotional argument, but one that reflected Konatsu's earnest nature: someone who could not simply disregard a reasonable point, even when it was inconvenient.

“Give me a little more time. I'll come to a proper answer.”

Chiharu narrowed her eyes at the sky beyond the corridor, bright and clear enough to inspire envy.

*

And Hasekura Yukino, who had been secretly watching Chiharu and Konatsu's exchange from a distance, spent fifth and sixth periods in a daze.

After Chiharu had said, “Coming from you?”, she had laughed brightly.

She had looked happy. It was a smile she hadn't once shown while eating with Yukino.

Was it jealousy? Or anguish? Frustration? Her chest throbbed with a sharp, prickling pain.

When you love someone, you want to make them happy. But if the best way to achieve that is to leave them, is there really anyone who can continue wishing for their happiness all the same?

Sixth period ended, and the end-of-day homeroom was kept brief.

As soon as the dismissal call was given, Yukino found it too painful to look in Chiharu's direction. She fled the classroom.

She walked a little way down the corridor, reached the stairs, then let out a small breath and slowed her pace.

Someone came running up behind her, panting. For an instant, hope and anxiety swelled within her. But the voice that called out was not the one she had been hoping to hear.

“Hasekura.”

It was Konatsu Sayama. Perhaps she had spotted Yukino walking along the corridor and run after her. Her breathing was ragged.

Yukino managed to form a faint smile on her gloomy face.

“Sayama-san.”

She relaxed her shoulders.

There was no doubt that Konatsu was the one who had set everything in motion, and Yukino was jealous of her. But that didn't mean she had any reason to hate her. In fact, right now, Konatsu was somewhat easier to face than Chiharu.

Konatsu studied Yukino's face with a faintly concerned expression, then quietly looked away.

“I do feel bad about it, but I'm not going to back down.”

The expression on her face held the guilt of someone who had tried to tear apart a pair of people who loved each other, simply to get what she wanted.

And yet she was determined not to let that guilt defeat her. She would stand her ground to the very end.

Yukino was impressed to discover that Konatsu had a surprisingly ordinary, human side to her. At the same time, she felt a certain admiration. She wasn't sure she herself could have followed through so resolutely in the same position.

She narrowed her eyes with that admiration, then turned to face forwards, as though looking away from Konatsu beside her.

“I don't mind…”

Konatsu's brow twitched at the reply, but before she could say anything, Yukino continued.

“…or at least, I thought I was the sort of person who could say that.”

Yukino gave a self-deprecating laugh, then looked back at Konatsu, who was watching her.

“I hate how petty I am, that I can't bring myself to say it honestly.”

Konatsu narrowed her eyes as though weighing those words, then faced forwards and gave a low murmur.

“Yeah.”

Then, with a feeling that seemed to well up from deep inside her, she added:

“I think I understand a little.”\
“Ah, so Sayama-san does understand how other people feel.”

Yukino's mouth twisted slightly, revealing the tip of a canine tooth. Konatsu returned a smile of much the same degree.

“Haha. That's harsh. Well, I'll accept the criticism. I'm the villain here, after all.”

Every word she said was entirely genuine. It proved that her guilt was real.

But if their positions had been reversed, Yukino would surely have gone so far as to express her own feelings, too.

And if simply confessing left her with no chance of winning, then doing so while knowing that would be nothing more than self-indulgence, a way of making peace with her own feelings. If she truly wanted to be in a relationship, she would look for even the smallest opening. Konatsu might be a villain for choosing that path, but there was a good chance Hasekura Yukino would have become one just the same.

“But I'm a little relieved.”

Konatsu suddenly closed her eyes in a gesture of relief. Yukino raised her brows and glanced at her profile.

“Because you seem to really love Chiharu, more than I thought you did.”

Yukino's eyes widened. Konatsu added, with a faintly troubled smile:

“Even if, by some tiny chance, she doesn't choose me, well… If you love her that much, then perhaps you could make her happy, even if not quite as much as I could.”

Yukino could only stare at Konatsu's profile, unable to say a word.

So, can you make Chiharu happy yourself?

Konatsu had asked Yukino that question. Yukino hadn't been able to answer, and was still struggling with it now.

But now Konatsu herself, the very person who had raised the question, was acknowledging that Yukino might be able to make Chiharu happy, at least to some extent.

Did that mean Yukino could believe she was capable of it, too? Could she hold her head high?

She didn't know.

There was just one thing she was certain of.

Konatsu genuinely wished for Chiharu's happiness.

Yukino could feel her own mouth twisting. She couldn't manage a proper smile.

There was no difference between Konatsu and Yukino in the one thing that mattered: Chiharu was precious to them both. They both wanted her to be happy just as much as the other did. And Konatsu had shown relief at the thought that, even if her own wish went unfulfilled, Chiharu might still find happiness.

She had meant it.

But what about Yukino?

Could she accept that Chiharu's happiness mattered more than the fulfilment of her own feelings?

Yukino asked herself that question. Then she felt the crooked smile on her lips spreading across her face. Her cheeks twitched, unable to form a proper smile, and her mouth grew dry with tension.

And then, in the next instant—

*I'm s-sorry… I mean, um, this must be difficult for you to talk about.*

*[No, it's not—]*

The words she had exchanged with Chiharu during lunch flashed through her mind.

Resignation appeared in Yukino's expression.

Her shoulders relaxed. She looked into empty space with a weak but natural smile, then closed her eyes in acceptance and took a shallow breath.

“I… I feel the same way. Chiharu-san's happiness comes first for me, too.”

Yukino murmured the words out of nowhere.

Having said them aloud, and knowing that they weren't a lie, she felt genuine relief.

Konatsu raised her brows in surprise and looked at Yukino.

Then she tilted her head in puzzlement at the smile on Yukino's face, which seemed to contain both exhaustion and resignation.

But Yukino's expression crumpled as though she might cry. She took a deep breath, then steeled herself.

“Could I borrow your name for a little while, Sayama-san?”

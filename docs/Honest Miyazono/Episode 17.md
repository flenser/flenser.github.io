Episode 17

The following morning, the familiar route to school seemed just a little brighter than usual.

Yukino's steps were light, and the scent of midsummer, now beginning to make itself known, felt more pleasant than ever.

With every step forward, she remembered the hand they'd held yesterday, and her heart fluttered. Then, whenever her foot touched the ground, she remembered Chiharu's words about not wanting someone else to determine the course of her life, and her feelings became tangled once more.

Her spirits rose, then sank. But with every repetition, her expectations swelled, like air being pumped into a balloon.

Yukino gradually lifted her head and narrowed her eyes at the blue morning sky. Then came the tap-tap of leather shoes behind her.

“Gooood morning!”

The familiar voice sounded more buoyant than Yukino had ever heard it. Her expectations burst into life.

Yukino's eyes widened as she turned around. There, her eyes glittering in the morning sunlight, stood Chiharu Miyazono. Her beautiful, refined features retained their usual composed expression, but a delighted smile was beginning to seep through.

Yukino's own cheeks softened in response. She dipped her chin slightly and returned the greeting.

“Good morning.”
“Oh? What's this? You look awfully happy.”
“Really…? You're asking me that, Chiharu-san?”

Yukino smiled and glanced up at her. Chiharu touched her own cheek with the palm of her hand.

Then, as her embarrassingly slack expression took on the faintest blush, she gave a playful laugh.

“Well, well, well. What's the harm in that? Hehe.”

Yukino couldn't know for certain why Chiharu was in such a good mood, but she had her suspicions. Of course, those suspicions rested largely on wishful thinking. So Yukino pretended not to notice, and simply kept her quiet happiness to herself.

Chiharu then sidled up beside her, rubbing her shoulder against Yukino's like a cat that had taken a liking to someone. She peered into Yukino's face with eyes like jewels the colour of the night sky.

That captivating gaze always made Yukino's heart pound like an alarm bell. She took a deep breath, trying to suppress the heat rising through her body, and returned Chiharu's look.

“So, Yukino. Are you free today?”

Yukino's eyes widened, pretending to be surprised by the question she'd been eagerly awaiting. She then tried on several expressions of deliberation, one after another, before finally squeezing out an answer, unable to hide her excitement.

“Y-yes.”

Chiharu lightly bit her lip and closed her eyes, as though savouring the moment. Then her expression softened.

“Then let's go out today, for real this time. I'll pay.”

Yukino was delighted, but she couldn't bring herself to agree to that last part. Her neck practically creaked as she shook her head.

“I-I can't accept that. I'll pay for my own share.”
“But, you know… I understand your circumstances, and I'm the one dragging you out with me…”

She meant Yukino's home situation. The fact that her mother wasn't exactly fulfilling the role of a mother. But—

“If money is going to be a problem, then I won't go. Besides…”

Yukino firmly rejected Chiharu's offer. Seeing her look displeased, she explained what she wanted from their relationship.

“…I don't want us to end up with a relationship built on favours and debts.”

Whether it was because one person gained financially, or because the relationship offered some kind of social status…

Whether they remained friends or their relationship eventually became something else, Yukino didn't want anything other than their feelings to enter into the equation. She felt that, otherwise, she wouldn't be able to hold her head high and take pride in their relationship.

Chiharu seemed to understand how Yukino felt. She reconsidered, resting a finger against her chin.

Then she smiled in acceptance, narrowed her eyes, and gave a small nod.

“I see.”
“Then, if things really are difficult, don't hide it from me. Just tell me.”

It sounded almost as though she were asking Yukino to rely on her. That was probably the greatest concession Chiharu was willing to make. Yukino hesitated, rocking her head back and forth, before finally giving in with an embarrassed smile.

“Yes.”

*

After school that day, the two of them entered a small karaoke room together.

The walls were covered in Western-style wallpaper, and a black leather sofa ran along them.

A glass-topped table sat squarely in the centre of the room. The monitor had been placed at an angle that made it slightly difficult to see from the sofa, with an electronic song catalogue in front of it. There were also microphones, thoroughly disinfected as a precaution against infectious diseases and even wrapped in protective film. The air conditioner smelled faintly of dust.

“So this is… karaoke!”

Yukino was the first to find their room, carrying the receipt and entering with an excited look in her eyes. Behind her, Chiharu smiled fondly and switched on the lights with a click. She took Yukino's bag from her and placed both bags on the sofa where the monitor was harder to see from. Then she sat down on the sofa directly opposite it.

“So this really is your first time. You've never been before?”
“Never! It's amazing. A whole room like this, for that price!”
“Are you some kind of karaoke shop salesperson? Well, actually, if you go for an all-day package, it can work out cheaper than some coworking spaces. Apparently people who've missed their chance to get a hotel room use them, too.”

Yukino nodded repeatedly, deeply impressed by Chiharu's explanation. She examined the equipment for adjusting the microphone settings, installed beneath the television, then happily fiddled with the dimmable lights. Suddenly, as though remembering herself, she looked up. Her cheeks faintly pink, she started to take a seat—

“Sit here.”

Chiharu, showing the tip of one canine tooth, gestured towards the space beside her on the somewhat roomy two-seater sofa.

Yukino hid her mouth behind her hands, which she'd pressed together in front of her face, and obediently replied,

“Y-yes.”

She sat down beside Chiharu, her palms resting on her knees, her back straight, and a faint sheen of sweat on her skin.

Chiharu, meanwhile, stretched both arms along the back of the sofa and leaned forwards slightly to peer at Yukino's face. In the dim, soundproof room, Yukino worried that Chiharu might be able to hear her heart pounding. Her palms were growing damp.

“Um…?”

Unable to bear it any longer, Yukino asked in a trembling voice.

Chiharu broke into a delighted grin.

“You looked like you were having so much fun.”

Yukino puffed out her cheeks, her face turning bright red.

“P-please forget about that. It was my first time, so I got a little carried away.”
“Oh? You got carried away because it was your first time…”
“Could you please stop repeating that part?!”
“I've taken Yukino's first time…”
“Hey! I feel like you're implying something!”

Yukino gave Chiharu a suspicious look as she continued making these rather suggestive jokes with a half-smile. Chiharu giggled, then lifted herself from the sofa and picked up the electronic song catalogue.

“Right, enough joking around. Let's sing. I want to hear you sing, Yukino.”

She held out the catalogue, and Yukino nervously began searching for a song.

The two of them took turns singing, playing around with the scoring function, switching it on and off as they went.

Yukino's choices were mostly older songs or love songs. Some were songs she vaguely remembered from long ago, from when she was very young, played in the car while her mother was driving. Others were love songs she'd heard in short videos on the streaming service she sometimes browsed on the smartphone she'd been given, ostensibly for her own use.

Chiharu's choices, by contrast, covered an astonishing range. J-pop above all, but also Western music, anime songs, Vocaloid, video game music, K-pop, and even enka. Yukino didn't recognise most of them, but Chiharu's overwhelming vocal ability and confident expressiveness were so impressive that, halfway through, Yukino began earnestly practising her tambourine technique.

By the time they'd been singing for about an hour, Yukino had grown accustomed to the place, and her nervousness had begun to fade.

“You tend to pick songs from a little while ago, don't you, Yukino? Do you like them?”

A brief lull had opened up, and Chiharu began chatting as she tapped through the catalogue in search of her next song.

Yukino sipped her green tea noisily through a straw and gave an uncertain little hum.

“Hmm… It's not that I particularly like them. It's just that I don't listen to music very much in the first place.”
“Why not? Not really your thing?”
“When I listen to a song, I can taste the singer's emotions from when they recorded it.”

Chiharu stopped tapping and stared at her in astonishment. Then she seemed to understand.

“I see.”

“That's right.” Yukino smiled awkwardly, bringing all five fingers together in front of her chest.

“Of course, it doesn't have that absolutely awful taste peculiar to people concealing something, but it makes it difficult to concentrate on the music. And having the flavour constantly changing and swirling around isn't particularly pleasant, either. That's why I can enjoy instrumental bands, for example. And, well, synthesised voices like Vocaloid are also flavourless and odourless.”
“Ahh… So that means you can taste something from my singing, too?”
“Yes. But it's been sweet the whole time… I didn't mind it at all. In fact, I could listen to it forever.”

Yukino confessed her feelings, growing embarrassed as she spoke. They were entirely honest, but somehow more difficult to say aloud than usual.

Naturally, it wasn't easy for the person receiving such a straightforward compliment to know how to respond. Chiharu began fiddling with her fringe, looking bashful.

“I see…”

She murmured the words softly, almost mumbling them.

“Then… how come you know those older songs?”

Yukino had almost forgotten the original question. Chiharu brought her back to it, and a troubled smile appeared on Yukino's face.

“I remember riding in the car when I was younger, with my mother driving. Those were the songs she used to play.”

Chiharu's previously relaxed expression softened. She smiled gently as the ice in her glass clinked against the sides while she drank her tea.

“So they're songs that bring back memories.”
“They're also some of my few memories of my mother. Ah, as you know, it's not that she's passed away or anything. We hardly speak these days, though, and my mother just finds me creepy.”

Yukino gazed down into empty space with a strangely detached expression. Chiharu closed her eyes, the corners of her own eyes drooping with sympathy.

“Your condition, huh… But you don't hate your mother, do you?”

Yukino gave an uncertain smile and spent several seconds searching for words. Eventually, she began with a long, drawn-out sigh.

Then she nodded awkwardly.

“Well, I suppose not.”
“…If she were truly an irredeemably awful person, she wouldn't have taken me to the hospital. She doesn't show much interest in me at all, but she does pay for things like my phone and school expenses.”

Chiharu let out a breath, seeming a little relieved. She made a conscious effort to keep her voice gentle.

“I don't want to be careless about someone else's family, but… have you ever talked things through with her?”
“There are hardly ever any opportunities. And my mother probably doesn't want to talk to me, either.”
“Well, still… This is just the opinion of someone on the outside, so ignore me if you think I'm wrong. But there are things you can't know unless you try talking to her at least once.”

If Yukino was living in a difficult situation, the last thing she needed was some privileged person lecturing her from their own comfortable position. Chiharu didn't try to justify herself or insist that she knew best. She knew she might make Yukino uncomfortable, but even so, she wanted to offer her an option that might make her a little happier.

Yukino accepted her words.

“…I'll give it some thought. I might ask you for advice about it sometime.”

Chiharu took out her own phone and gave it a casual wave.

“Anytime. Morning or night, call me. I'll answer.”

Yukino nodded, gazing intently at the phone her mother had bought for her.

She wasn't ready to feel that way just yet. But perhaps someday. With that thought in mind, she put her phone back in her pocket and noisily sucked down the green tea, now chilled by the ice.

“Shall we sing something together?”

Chiharu suddenly threw out the suggestion with a mischievous grin. Yukino nearly spat out her tea.

A duet. Also known as a two-part vocal performance. A duet.

Yukino sipped her green tea to regain her composure, though the ice had almost entirely melted, leaving it tasting more like water than anything else.

Singing a duet was surely an ordinary thing for friends to do. And yet, when she had special feelings for someone, it somehow seemed almost like an indecent, sexual act.

Could she even sing? And did she know any songs suitable for a duet?

With her face flushed, Yukino's eyes spun in their sockets. She pulled her mouth away from the straw and murmured,

“I-I'd like a love song.”

Hasekura Yukino was, in most respects, a shameless woman.

But Miyazono Chiharu was just as magnanimous.

And so the two of them pored over the electronic catalogue, searching the digital sea for a song they both knew. After about three minutes of deliberation, they settled on a relatively recent ballad. It had been written as the theme song for a popular romance film, and had become an internet sensation almost overnight.

Chiharu entered the song. The intro began to play, and the two of them sat side by side, each holding a microphone.

Yukino's heart pounded. Her stomach was doing something unusual, twisting itself into knots for an entirely different reason than usual. She took a deep breath to calm her nerves, but apparently the microphone was on, and the sound came out enormously amplified. Yukino jumped, hurriedly switching it off. Chiharu watched her with a cheerful smile, then began singing in her beautiful voice.

Diaphragmatic breathing. A wide-open mouth. Careful use of mixed voice, pitch, and rhythm. Expressiveness.

She's so good, Yukino thought absently as she listened, lost in the performance.

Then it was her turn.

Shallow chest breathing. A small mouth. A trembling voice, pitches missed here and there, and a rhythm that dragged in places.

The difference in their singing ability was embarrassing. But Chiharu never made fun of her. Instead, she watched Yukino and swayed along to the rhythm with her body.

So Yukino watched her and sang.

The two singers sang lyrics about a deeply twisted kind of love.

One was serious and sincere. Precisely because she was so steadfast, she needed proof that the romantic feelings she held were genuine, for the sake of the person she loved. She never used words like love or romance, but showed her feelings through her actions alone.

The other was a coward. So timid that she couldn't even bring herself to do anything underhanded. She had no courage to love anyone wholeheartedly, yet she used pretty words about love and devotion in the hope that someone would love her.

Chiharu gazed at Yukino as she sang in her gentle voice. Yukino, whose memory of the lyrics was hazy, stumbled through the song, looking alternately at Chiharu as she kept the rhythm and at the words displayed on the monitor.

Then, quite suddenly, Yukino reached one hand towards her skirt to wipe away the sweat on her palm.

The backs of their hands touched.

Chiharu and Yukino both jerked their heads up as though struck by a spark. Their eyes met.

For the first time, Chiharu's otherwise fluent, beautiful voice faltered. Her ears turned red, and her gaze wandered as she hurriedly tried to recover the rhythm.

Yukino couldn't look at either the monitor or Chiharu. Unable to forget the fleeting sensation of their hands touching, she lowered her head. Every last scrap of the lyrics she'd half-remembered vanished from her mind.

But at the edge of her lowered gaze, she saw Chiharu's hand resting on the sofa again.

This time, her palm was facing upwards, as though waiting for something.

Yukino swallowed.

Then, narrowing her eyes, she cautiously placed her hand over Chiharu's. Chiharu's palm closed around hers, just as carefully.

Her hand was warm. It seemed almost damp with sweat.

Their hands were joined.

Their fingers intertwined. Yukino's index finger felt Chiharu's middle finger, and Chiharu's middle finger felt Yukino's.

The warmth of their hands connected their hearts. And so, at last, they were able to look at the monitor and sing.

For Hasekura Yukino, happiness was something of immense importance, and yet it was also terribly vague. She had no concrete definition for it. It was merely something she observed relatively, through the reactions of her own heart.

But if someone asked her what happiness was, and demanded a concrete answer…

Surely, she would think of this day. Of the four minutes and thirty-one seconds of that duet, in the middle of summer.

As she listened quietly to the instrumental outro, Yukino let her thoughts wander with the warmth coming from her right hand. Chiharu, who stole a sideways glance at her, was wishing that this outro could go on forever.

*

After singing for another two hours or so, the two of them finally stepped outside together. The shop had grown dark around them.

The sky was already filled with stars, and the moon bathed the world in pale blue light. Under the watchful glow of the streetlamps, which shone in warm contrast, they took a little breath of the outside air, smiled at each other, and set off together towards the nearest station.

“That was fun. Let's go again sometime.”
“Next time, I'll expand my repertoire a little!”
“Haha. Well, there's nothing wrong with singing the same song three times in a row.”
“Singing a song because you love it is different from singing it because you've run out of ideas. It's disrespectful to the song.”
“Every time you sing at karaoke, the rights holders get royalties. Wouldn't that make it the opposite of disrespectful?”
“S-so they're better off if people sing lots of songs, even if they have to make up an excuse…?!”

They chatted animatedly as they reached the nearest station to their high school.

The concourse ran in a straight line between the eastern and western entrances, connected by escalators. Two different sets of ticket gates stood on the northern side, while stained glass and a noticeboard adorned the southern end.

The two of them would be using different ticket gates.

As Yukino searched for a topic that might keep Chiharu with her a little longer, her gaze caught on the noticeboard at the edge of her vision.

More precisely, on a poster displayed there.

The words Yōka Festival.

Yukino slowed her pace. Apparently Chiharu had noticed the same thing at the same time, because she came to a complete stop.

The silence was born of hesitation. Yukino wondered whether she should speak, while Chiharu carefully chose her words.

But each quickly understood the meaning of the other's silence. Before long, Chiharu was fiddling with her fringe, looking away with an expression of studied nonchalance. Then, in a light, casual tone, she said,

“Hey, about the festival… W-would you like to go together?”

Yukino had completely forgotten that crowds were difficult for her, because she could hear so many conversations at once.

She was on the verge of accepting without a second thought, but hurriedly swallowed the words that had risen to her throat.

“I-I… Well, I don't mind at all, but what about your other friends? Would they be all right with it?”

She knew how disingenuous it was to ask such a question at this point. But Hasekura Yukino was that much of a coward.

Chiharu knew this, too. She narrowed her eyes and smiled with just her mouth.

“Why are you asking that now? Of course they'd be fine with it… Ah, no. Well…”

Just when Yukino thought she could relax, Chiharu groaned with her eyes closed, saying something that made her anxious all over again.

Yukino waited, frightened, a sheen of nervous sweat gathering on her skin. Chiharu covered her mouth with one hand. Her face faintly red, she answered in a tone that sounded almost petulant.

“It's not about whether they'd be all right with it… I want to go with you, Yukino.”

It was as though someone had grabbed Yukino by the head and shaken her. Her face turned bright red. She opened and closed her mouth, making little sounds like “Ah…” and “Uwah…”, rubbed her hands together in front of her chest, and rolled her eyes as she muttered something incoherent.

Her reaction was more eloquent than words, but Chiharu still waited, apparently wanting to hear her answer out loud.

So Yukino hid her ears, which felt hot enough to steam, and gave a barely audible reply.

“Yes.”

Chiharu let out a breath of relief, then asked in a more relaxed tone,

“What about you, though? Are you sure you're happy with me? Isn't there someone else you'd rather go with?”

Yukino's red face stiffened. She pursed her lips and groaned, thinking hard.

She was conflicted. How much of her feelings should she reveal?

Unless she was being conceited, there was no longer any need for them to confirm what they felt for one another. And yet…

Chiharu had once described romance as an act of bending the course of one's life, and had seemed to dislike the idea.

Was it all right for Yukino to keep walking down this path?

She hesitated, swallowed, and remembered Konatsu Sayama's words.

Chiharu was an attractive woman. And compared to her, what was Yukino?

That was precisely why she had to take a step forwards.

With sweat beading all over her skin, Yukino spoke.

“Recently… I've realised something. I like women.”

Chiharu's eyes darted away in plain view. Her face tightened with alarm, and her throat moved as she swallowed.

“And, well… there's a woman I like. So… I'm fine.”

If one assumed that the conjunctions in her sentence were doing their job, then this was, for all practical purposes, a confession.

Chiharu made a soundless movement with her throat, drawing in and letting out a little air.

“O-oh… Ah…”

Her eyes widened and wandered about, and a strange sheen of sweat appeared on her skin as she let out a drawn-out, incoherent groan.

“S-so… can I look forward to that day?”

Chiharu asked timidly.

After a trembling deep breath, Yukino nodded carefully.

Chiharu's eyes widened. She took a deep breath.

“I see.”

Then, unable to hide her bewilderment, she let her gaze wander and fidgeted restlessly. After a moment, she left Yukino with a rather brusque,

“Well, then…”

And began walking unsteadily towards the ticket gates.

Yukino, gradually emerging from the tension that had made it difficult even to see, leaned her back against the wall of the concourse. She pressed her hands together in front of her face and took a deep breath, as though in prayer.

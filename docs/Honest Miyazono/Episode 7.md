The fifth lesson that day was civics. Shinohara, whom they had seen earlier, stood at the front of the classroom.

High school students who had just finished lunch tended to nod off as their blood sugar spiked, but only when they thought they could get away with it. Shinohara was the sort of man who rarely reprimanded anyone directly for sleeping in class, instead silently deducting points from their conduct grades. As a result, around twenty per cent of the class were gently drifting in and out of the dream world.

Yukino was paying proper attention from her seat at the back of the classroom, right in the middle. Then, quite unexpectedly, she glanced towards Chiharu, who was sitting near the front, slightly to the left.

Chiharu turned around, though surely she hadn't noticed Yukino looking at her. Their eyes met with a sharp click, and Yukino flinched. Chiharu raised an eyebrow, tapping her notebook with her pen and giving her a mischievous smile that seemed to say, Pay attention. Yukino pursed her lips in a sulk and pointed towards the front, as if to say, You should be looking that way.

“...And so, groping and false accusations of groping are fairly common on Japan's railways, particularly on local lines and trains other than limited express services. In recent years, false accusations have also begun to receive more attention as a social issue. People say, ‘When in doubt, favour the accused,’ but that's the way judges are supposed to think. You could even call it an idealistic principle. Once prosecutors bring a case to trial, they fight tooth and nail for a guilty verdict. That can lead to problems such as what's known as ‘hostage justice’, which has drawn plenty of criticism. And that's precisely why we need defence lawyers. They help counteract that bias.”

Looking rather weary, Shinohara spoke as he wrote only the bare minimum on the board.

Whether it was fact or not, guilt and punishment, lies and truth, were issues that ran deep for Yukino, too.

For instance, if she ever went into a profession like that, she could uncover the truth of a case simply by talking to people. Such information might not be of any use in court, of course, but it would be another matter during an investigation. She could pursue the case on the assumption that she knew who the real culprit was. That seemed as though it could be useful.

“Railways, false accusations... Which reminds me, there's another issue. Voyeuristic photography is a problem, too.”

For a moment, an odd taste settled on her tongue.

It wasn't a lie. But neither was it the truth.

It was a taste that couldn't be measured along the simple axes of truth and falsehood, something else altogether. She rarely experienced it, and had never quite put it into words, but if she had to describe it, impatience came close. It was the flavour that arose when someone was driven by some emotion and forcefully steered a conversation in a direction of their choosing.

While everyone else listened sleepily, Yukino tensed.

Her brow furrowed, and she started to raise a hand to cover her ears. Chiharu's face came to mind. But...

She lowered her hand.

It was instinct. The instinct of someone who had lived with this condition for more than a decade. When she tasted something so strongly, there was usually a powerful emotion behind it. And powerful emotions sometimes gave rise to powerful lies.

Like the incident involving Takada, who had stolen Iida Riko's handkerchief.

The kind of lie that could give birth to a crime like that.

Genzaburou Shinohara. Twenty-nine years old, a young male civics teacher. He had a languid air about him, but was handsome enough to be popular with the girls. In fact, during lunch, a female student had clung to his arm. Yukino had heard few bad rumours about him. If anything, he seemed diligent, arriving at school early every morning to patrol the grounds.

Now he gazed into empty space, rubbing the chalk between his fingers as he spoke.

“Voyeuristic photography covers a wide range of behaviours. For example, it might be done to satisfy sexual urges, or to gain attention by posting the images on social media. Like many other crimes, it can arise from a specific desire. Of course, none of these acts should ever be tolerated, but speaking frankly as a teacher...”

A sharp, fermented-food flavour stung the back of Yukino's nose. The unpleasant taste of impatience.

I don't want to hear this.

But I need to hear it.

Caught between those contradictory impulses, Yukino froze completely as Shinohara continued.

“[I want you to understand that the people who get photographed without their consent are partly to blame, too.]”

The taste of a lie.

A complex flavour of putrid, lukewarm vomit, once solid but now reduced to a thick, flowing sludge.

Even when she called it the taste of a lie, the flavour varied enormously. Her tongue picked up a mixture of emotions. A little lie told in the course of everyday life had a faint, simple taste, like ordinary vomit. But a lie woven out of powerful feelings, whether to deceive or hurt someone, or for some other reason entirely, tasted incomparably worse.

Shinohara's lie was the foulest she had tasted in months.

At its core was the sourness of fear. Lemon juice formed the base, infused with the complex fermented flavour of desperation. The spiciness of anger grew viscous, and everything simmered together in a pot, cooked up with the taste of vomit. It was the worst lie imaginable.

The instant she heard it, her stomach seemed to contract with nausea. Yukino's face turned deathly pale, sweat soaking her skin as she pressed a hand over her mouth. Before her, Shinohara continued speaking in an utterly matter-of-fact tone.

“For instance, if you dressed modestly and lived quietly, you wouldn't attract the attention of the idiots who want to post pictures of you on social media, would you? [It's the same principle with things like not rolling up your skirt, or choosing trousers instead of a skirt in the first place. There are precautions you can take, yet people who refuse to take them make a fuss about being photographed. Of course, the perpetrator is in the wrong. But speaking as a teacher, I'd say the victim bears some responsibility, too. If you don't want it to happen, you should take appropriate precautions first.]”

Yukino repeatedly swallowed back the vomit rising into her throat. Her skin was drenched in sweat, her body temperature so strange that she couldn't tell whether she was hot or cold. She stared at Shinohara with trembling eyes.

A few students looked at him sceptically at such an extreme statement, but the class as a whole remained relaxed. Most simply let his words wash over them, taking little notice.

Shinohara gazed into empty space with an expression of exasperation, then spat out his final words.

*

“[Everyone, please be careful. I'm worried about all of you.]”

*

In the sudden silence, the sound of a chair scraping against the floor rang out.

Shinohara fell silent. The students stopped writing.

They all turned towards the source of the noise, at the back of the classroom.

Yukino Hasekura was standing, her face ashen and her skin glistening with sweat. She moved her lips as though trying to say something, then fled the classroom without another word, looking as if she were in agony.

“H-Hasekura?” Shinohara watched her go in astonishment as murmurs spread through the room.

Another chair scraped against the floor, drawing everyone's attention to a different girl.

It was Chiharu Miyazono.

With an unusually serious expression, Chiharu stood and headed for the door.

“Yu... Hasekura-san hasn't been feeling well since this afternoon! I'm just going to check on her!”

Chiharu had built up enough trust that her explanation was accepted without question. “Ah, right,” Shinohara and the other students replied.

If Yukino was unwell, there was no helping it. Besides, her face really had been pale, so it wasn't as though Chiharu were lying.

Chiharu hurried out of the classroom, forcing herself not to break into a run, and made her way swiftly to the toilets.

There was no time to go looking somewhere farther away, so she headed for the nearest toilets on the same floor.

The moment she reached the entrance, she heard someone retching. Then came the sound of vomit falling into the toilet bowl.

“Ugh... cough... urgh...!”

From the nearest cubicle came the unmistakable sound of stomach contents being violently expelled, splattering as they came up.

Chiharu stood frozen. A faint sheen of sweat gathered on her skin as she stared at the floor, wondering what she should do.

It must be awful to have a friend walk in while you were throwing up. Having someone hear those sounds would be even worse.

Perhaps she should wait outside. She knew she ought to let Yukino approach her in her own time, but even knowing that, she found it painful to stand by while her friend suffered.

So Chiharu hurried away, bought a bottle of water from a vending machine on the first floor, then rushed back to the toilets.

By the time she returned, the retching had stopped. She worried that Yukino might have gone back to class, but when she stepped inside, she found that the toilet cubicle was locked.

Should she wait, or go in? After hesitating for a moment, she decided to call out, just in case something was wrong.

“...Yukino?”

After knocking twice, she called Yukino's name. A faint, hoarse voice answered.

“...Yes.”

Relieved, Chiharu heard the toilet flush. Then the lock clicked, and the cubicle door slowly opened.

Inside, a girl with dark brown hair sat helplessly on the toilet floor, breathing shallowly and painfully. Her features were beautiful, but her skin was unnaturally pale, and she was painfully thin. When she raised her frail gaze towards Chiharu, Chiharu carefully handed her the bottle of water, as gently as if she were handling a delicate sugar sculpture.

“Here. Rinse your mouth out with this.”\
“I'm so... sorry. Thank you.”

Yukino tried to unscrew the bottle with trembling hands, but she couldn't get the cap off.

Chiharu opened it for her. After another hoarse “Thank you”, Yukino rinsed her mouth several times, screwed the cap back on, then leaned against the wall of the cubicle, cradling the bottle in her arms.

Chiharu watched silently. Torn over what to do, she looked into empty space before finally venturing a little further.

“I know it's not much, but... are you all right?”

When she looked into Yukino's eyes, Yukino gave her a sad smile.

“Please... don't put yourself down like that. Thank you for coming to find me. And the water... I'm truly, very grateful. Thank you. I'll pay you back for it later.”\
“You don't need to worry about that now. More importantly, do you want to go to the nurse's office?”

Yukino met Chiharu's concerned gaze, then looked away with a weak smile.

And then, as though resolving to do something she needed to do, she changed that smile into a bright, artificial one.

“[I'm fine! I'll be back soon.] If you could just let me rest here for a little while...”

It was fair to say that Yukino and Chiharu hadn't known each other for very long.

Even so, Chiharu was almost certain that the smile was forced.

She felt the distance between them. Yukino sat on the floor, while Chiharu crouched beside her. They were close enough to touch if either reached out, yet a gulf remained between their hearts. Chiharu suspected that, out of all the people Yukino had ever known, she was probably the person who had come closest to her. And yet she was still far from the very core of Yukino's heart.

Faced with Yukino's forced smile, Chiharu hesitated.

She fell silent and lowered her gaze, gripping her knee tightly with the hand that had reached out as if trying to grasp something.

If the other person wanted to keep their distance, the sensible thing was to respect that and step back.

It was the wise way to live, and Chiharu believed it was the right thing to do. But...

They had grown a little too close for her to swallow that righteousness without a struggle. Yukino had helped her when she was in trouble. She had eaten Chiharu's homemade lunch with such obvious delight. They had opened up to one another, if only a little. Those small things had come to mean an extraordinary amount to Chiharu.

So she pressed her lips together and closed her eyes. After a shallow breath, she opened them and spoke to Yukino.

“I believe the most important thing in communication and relationships is respecting each other's wishes. So I should respect your decision not to tell me anything, and the right thing to do would be to back off now.”

At Chiharu's words, Yukino lowered her head, the corners of her eyebrows drooping apologetically.

“But I'm sorry. Even if it's not the right thing to do, I want to take a step closer.”

Yukino slowly raised her head, hesitantly. In her fragile eyes, she saw Chiharu's serious expression reflected back at her.

Light filtered through the frosted glass in the toilets. Without the lights on, the cubicle and the space beyond it were divided between shadow and illumination. To Yukino, looking out from the darkness in surprise and uncertainty, Chiharu's unwavering gaze was almost blinding.

“I want to know your secret. What kind of condition you have, why you're hiding it, what happened to you, and what you're trying to do now... I want you to rely on me.”

There was no confidence on Chiharu's face as she said this. No composure, either. It was a resolve she had struggled to find, and had done her best to put into words.

All that lay behind it was a desire to do something for her friend. There was no lie in her words.

The flavour Yukino sensed in them was a gentle sweetness, like fruit. It was the taste of affection, something Yukino rarely got to experience. Before she knew it, her voice was trembling.

“I... I... Just by coming here, you've already shown me how kind you are. Anything more than that...”\
“If you're feeling guilty, then I feel guilty, too. And if I turn back now, I know that feeling won't go away.”

Yukino's face slowly crumpled, like water soaking into paper. She was struggling so hard that she looked ready to cry.

Chiharu had already begun to realise just how unusual and deeply rooted Yukino's problems were. There were countless clues: her uncanny intuition, the rare moments when she behaved in ways completely at odds with her usual personality, her sudden bouts of vomiting, her difficulty with relationships despite seeming perfectly capable of socialising, her home life, her eating habits.

There was a secret that connected all these things. Yukino was hiding it.

Chiharu knew that even if she learned the secret, there might be very little she could do. But she wanted to do as much as she could. That was why she wanted to know. She wanted to understand, so she could help Yukino.

This wasn't about being a kind person who wanted to make everyone happy.

This was about Chiharu Miyazono's own way of living: wanting to do what she could for Yukino Hasekura, her friend.

“If there's a way for us to stop feeling guilty, then I think we'll only find it once we understand each other. Asking you to tell me your secret might be causing you pain. But I promise you, I won't let you regret it. I'll make you smile.”

Chiharu gently reached out and placed her hand over Yukino's trembling hand on her knee.

Yukino's face crumpled even further. She bit her lip and lowered her head, then finally forced the words out.

“There are... two reasons I didn't want to tell you.”

Chiharu nodded at Yukino's strained confession, tightening her hand over Yukino's.

Feeling the warmth of Chiharu's hand against the back of her own, Yukino continued, clinging to the last remnants of her reluctance.

“One reason is... I thought you might find me creepy if you knew.”

As if I ever would.

Chiharu swallowed the words. Instead, she understood that Yukino was afraid of something that could provoke such a reaction, and prioritised empathising with her feelings. She nodded, silently encouraging her to continue.

“...But I don't think you'd say something like that, Miyazono-san. That's precisely why I'm worried about the other thing. Kind people might push themselves too far because of it, and our relationship might become distorted.”

Yukino forced the words out through her trembling voice, laying bare all her fears.

“My secret... destroys relationships between people.”

Because I don't think I'm the kind of person who can be happy.

Remembering Yukino's words, Chiharu tugged at the hand resting on her knee.

Then she enclosed it between both her hands and looked straight at Yukino, an unshakeable steel resolve in her eyes.

“It won't break. I won't let it break. Even if you cry, I'm not going to fall out with you.”

The warmth of Chiharu's hands spread from Yukino's left hand. It was like holding a warm mug in the depths of a bitterly freezing winter, or having cold water poured into your hands in a scorching desert when your throat was parched.

At those words, Yukino narrowed her eyes. She blinked several times, trying to hold back the tears.

And then, wanting to repay Chiharu's affection, she steeled herself as though preparing to cut open her own belly, and revealed her secret.

*

“I can tell when people are lying.”

*

Half astonishment, half understanding. Chiharu's eyes widened slightly, then softened again almost at once.

“Okay,” she said, accepting Yukino's words without a trace of doubt.

Surprised by that, Yukino found that once the dam had broken, her words flowed like water. She began quietly explaining the whole of her secret.

“To be precise, the emotions carried in someone's voice stimulate my sense of taste. Anger tastes spicy, friendship and affection taste sweet, and lies... taste like truly horrible vomit. I was diagnosed with synaesthesia when I was little.”\
“Synaesthesia,” Chiharu murmured, searching her memory. Wasn't that a condition in which one kind of stimulus produced a different kind of sensory experience? Seeing colours in numbers, for instance, or colours in letters.

If Yukino tasted emotions, and if lies, the feeling of wanting to deceive someone, had an overwhelmingly unpleasant flavour, then everything about Yukino's behaviour suddenly made sense.

“So... I end up knowing all about the things people are hiding, along with that horrible taste.”

Yukino's face was deathly pale, her expression filled with fear that Chiharu might reject her.

It was certainly the sort of secret that could cause considerable trouble for anyone who heard it. Everyone had a thing or two to hide. Yukino's ability might vary in precision and degree, but if it stripped away the lies that served as social lubricant, all that would remain was friction.

That was why she had said she could never be happy.

She had only ever had two choices: continue suffering herself, or create friction in her relationships with others.

But even after understanding all of that, Chiharu shrugged as though she had come full circle around every one of Yukino's concerns.

“Then there's absolutely no problem with telling me! Honestly, I was worrying far too much.”

She gave Yukino an amused, exasperated smile, speaking playfully. Yukino's frightened eyes widened.

Looking at her with a laugh, Chiharu made a point of keeping her tone light and casual.

“I'm an honest person, after all. If you can tell when I'm lying, that won't be a problem at all. Right?”

Silence. Stillness.

A few seconds later, Chiharu noticed tears welling in the corners of Yukino's eyes, and pretended not to see them.

Yukino bit her lip and bowed her head, repeatedly wiping at the corners of her eyes with the back of her hand. She struggled to suppress her sobs, then spoke in a trembling voice.

“...You don't doubt me.”

There was something almost happy in the way she said it, so Chiharu answered brightly.

“...On the day I met my brother's future wife, you knew I had plans. When you offered me the two bottles of drink, you left me the one I wanted. Thinking back, whenever you looked like you were in pain, someone had always been lying. More precisely, their words were things I realised were lies when I thought back on them. At first, I wondered if you'd been studying psychology or something, but if what you've told me about your condition is true, everything fits. So I have no reason to doubt you.”

Every word was sincere. Chiharu had apparently come much closer to understanding Yukino's secret than Yukino had expected.

“I'm sorry. I wish I could believe you simply because it was you saying it, but... I've judged that your words are true based on reason. And having done that, I can say without reservation that it's no problem at all.”

Chiharu's playful expression softened with a little more tenderness, and she smiled.

“You can tell, can't you? That I'm not lying. Our relationship won't change.”

Who could possibly laugh at the sight of a tear finally slipping from Yukino's eye, despite all her efforts to hold it back?

Yukino sniffled softly and lifted her slender right hand, doing her best to wipe away her tears.

“...Yes...”

Hearing that tiny, almost inaudible response of acceptance, Chiharu finally felt the weight lift from her shoulders.

She gave a rueful smile and took a handkerchief from her pocket. Keeping hold of Yukino's left hand with her right, she used her other hand to wipe away Yukino's tears. Yukino sniffled and let her, gazing at Chiharu with eyes that had grown slightly red and swollen. Chiharu squeezed their joined hands and smiled back at her gently.

For a dozen or so seconds, they remained silent, savouring the moment and the connection between them.

Eventually, Yukino managed to hold back her tears, and Chiharu, having seen that she was all right, met her gaze.

The gentle atmosphere grew a little more serious as Chiharu brought the conversation back to the matter at hand.

“So... the fact that you became this sick means Shinohara-sensei lied about something, right?”\
“...Yes. He did.”\
“And that lie probably wasn't an ordinary one. It was the sort of lie that made you decide you had to send me back to the classroom and deal with something on your own. Am I right?”

Chiharu gave her a sly, almost teasing smile. Yukino nodded with a weary smile, thinking that she really couldn't hide anything from her.

“‘The people who get photographed are partly to blame.’ ‘They should take precautions.’ ‘I'm worried about all of you.’ Those were the lies.”\
“...Lies? So he doesn't really believe any of those things? That the person doing the photographing is at fault, and that it's not a matter of taking precautions... Or perhaps he doesn't actually care about the students at all?”\
“No. The flavours are a little more complicated than that, but the change of subject was rather abrupt.”\
“True. Groping leads naturally enough from railways, and voyeuristic photography is connected to groping, but he really went off on a tangent.”\
“Yes. So, based on experience, I think he was telling those lies to himself.”

Yukino honestly explained her reasoning. To her surprise, Chiharu reached the same conclusion within seconds.

“In other words, they're things he wishes were true. ‘The people who get photographed are partly to blame.’ ‘It's the fault of those who don't take precautions.’ ‘I'm worried about everyone, so I'm a good person.’ That's what he's telling himself.”\
“...Yes. In other words, cognitive dissonance. He understands that something is wrong, yet he does it himself. To relieve the excessive stress caused by that contradiction, he adds false beliefs to his own thinking. If these are lies intended to deceive himself... then the fact that he doesn't push away students who get unusually close to him, and that he comes to school earlier than anyone else every morning to patrol the grounds, could point to the possibility that...”

He wanted the people who were photographed to be partly to blame.

He wanted it to be true that people who failed to protect themselves were at fault.

He wanted to believe he was a good person because he worried about everyone.

There was only one kind of wrongdoing that came to mind when someone tried to comfort himself with lies like these.

*

“...Voyeuristic photography inside the school.”

*

Chiharu murmured the words, and Yukino nodded quietly.

“I was going to look for cameras...”

Chiharu watched her in silence. Then, as Yukino tilted her head with an embarrassed expression, Chiharu spoke.

“You've been carrying things like this all by yourself for so long.”

Being able to tell when people were lying meant that, more often than not, Yukino could uncover the crimes people were hiding.

Thinking of the recent incident involving the suspected theft of a handkerchief, Yukino nodded with a lonely smile.

“But I'm used to it.”

She tried to make light of it, but Chiharu's eyebrows shot up in a reproachful frown.

“Don't get used to it.”

Chiharu gave her a mild scolding, then pulled her by the hand and stood up.

“Let's look for them together. Right now.”\
“...You skipped class to come here, didn't you? So did I, for that matter.”\
“We'll say I was feeling unwell. This is where having a good reputation comes in handy.”\
“It's only a possibility. We might not find anything.”\
“I hope that's the case. But we should look, so we can be sure.”

Chiharu didn't doubt Yukino's condition or her words.

Yukino could tell that clearly, and so she accepted Chiharu's help and got to her feet.

“Your lunch... You went to all that trouble to make it, and I'm sorry.”

Yukino lowered her head, her face twisted with guilt. Chiharu caught her by the chin and lifted her face.

Yukino flinched when she was forced to meet Chiharu's gaze, but Chiharu simply returned a carefree smile.

“I'll make it again, so don't look like that. We'll eat together again.”

Hearing those words only made Yukino more conscious of how her face was crumpling.

Her eyes grew hot.

But when Chiharu saw this, she moved her hand from Yukino's chin to her cheek and forced a smile onto her face.

“Being able to tell when someone is lying must be far more painful than I can imagine. So I understand why you think you might never be happy. But...”

Chiharu tried to understand Yukino's suffering. She didn't claim that she could fully understand it.

Instead, she acknowledged Yukino's pain without denying it, then chose to offer her a smile.

“Even if you can't find happiness on your own, I'll make you happy. I want my friends to smile.”

Chiharu said it with an unguarded smile. Yukino received her words with a fragile expression, the corners of her eyebrows drooping.

Her cheeks wanted to soften with happiness, while her heart seemed ready to melt into tears. Her breath came out hot. The warmth of her heart travelled through their joined fingers. Her heart pounded, and she could hear the rush of blood deep in her ears. Her face turned bright red, as though someone had emptied a bucket of paint over it, and her body temperature rose until she almost imagined that Chiharu's hand had become lukewarm.

At her fingertips, Yukino felt the happiness she had given up on, the happiness she had turned away from. She found something special in it.

Whatever interpretation Chiharu put on Yukino's bright-red face, she gave a little chuckle and peered at her with a teasing smile.

“Getting embarrassed, are you?”

Then she gave their joined hands a gentle tug.

“Come on, Yukino.”

And, as though drawing her out of the shadows, Chiharu began to walk.

Light filtered through the frosted glass in the toilets. Yukino stepped out of the dim cubicle, where the lights were still off, and narrowed her eyes at the brightness.

Then she looked at someone even brighter.

“Yes, Chiharu-san.”

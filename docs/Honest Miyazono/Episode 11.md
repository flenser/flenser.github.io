A lively street that never slept, its neon signs mingling with one another. Just one narrow alley over from it.

The two of them walked along a dark, narrow lane where even the voices of passers-by were indistinct. It felt as though only the moonlight watched over them, yet it was also the kind of place where, if they called out, someone would come to their aid.

“I'm really sorry about earlier. Are you feeling all right now?”

It was late June, already the height of early summer. After eight in the evening, the entertainment district was still warm and humid, the air faintly balmy.

A breeze brought a little relief from the heat as the two walked side by side, their shadows cast by the streetlights mingling on the ground.

Chiharu seemed to genuinely regard her earlier remark as a serious mistake.

Since she brought it up again with such an apologetic expression, Yukino responded with a gentle smile.

“Please don't worry so much. Little lies aren't all that painful.”\
“No, I'm going to worry about it. Let me at least buy you a drink or something. I won't feel right otherwise.”\
“Are you sure? If I accept, I'll start charging you every time you lie from now on, you know?”

If Yukino tried to be overly considerate, Chiharu would never let the matter drop. This called for a more assertive approach.

When Yukino spoke with a slightly teasing air, Chiharu understood what she was getting at, and her momentum faltered.

“...But I still won't feel right doing nothing. You looked like you were suffering.”\
“It's my own fault for eating too much. Besides, I didn't actually throw up in the end.”

Even after hearing that, Chiharu still wasn't satisfied. She folded her arms and pondered for several seconds.

Eventually, she stopped walking, took a notepad and pen from her pocket, and scribbled something down by the light of the streetlamp and the moon. Then she tore off the page without ceremony and held it out to Yukino.

“Is this a cheque?”\
“That's right. If you ever run short of money, just use this... Wait, what am I saying?”

After exchanging a clumsy joke and an equally clumsy retort, Yukino accepted the paper.

She held it up to the moonlight and read the words.

“‘One-Time Wish Ticket’.”\
“If I ever tell a particularly serious lie that gets you into trouble again, I'll issue you one of these as an apology.”\
“Won't these be worthless by next year...? A-are you sure about this?”\
“It's only for lies that cause you real trouble, so I'll issue at most one a month. ...Probably.”

Chiharu didn't sound entirely confident. She added that last word with a faintly uncertain expression.

The right to make a selfish request. Yukino studied the ticket with a troubled look, and Chiharu continued as she put her notepad away.

“The full name is a bit long, so let's call it the ‘Wish Ticket’ for short.”\
“Wouldn't it have been easier to call it that from the start...?”\
“Then its official name shall be the ‘Wish Ticket’. Its meaning is ‘A Ticket That Allows Hasekura Yukino to Have Her Wishes Granted’.”\
“I see. Now I understand the importance of an official name. As an apology, please hit me.”\
“You're taking this way too seriously.”

Setting the jokes aside, Chiharu raised a finger and explained what the ticket entitled its holder to.

“For example, you could say, ‘Please put this in tomorrow's bento,’ or ‘If you're free today, please walk home with me.’ It's a right that might get you little things like that.”\
“S-so you won't guarantee that you'll do it?”\
“It's only the right to make a selfish request, after all. What do you think?”

It was almost too generous a reward for a lie that had slipped out by accident. And precisely because of that, Yukino felt she couldn't accept it.

Yet somehow, she felt that each ticket would strengthen the bond between her and Chiharu. Telling herself not to use them for anything too unreasonable, she hugged the little slip of paper to her chest.

“Hehe. I'll treasure it.”

A rare, childlike smile appeared on Yukino's face, and Chiharu's cheeks softened with satisfaction.

Just then, a warm breeze swept past, brushing through their hair.

Yukino gently smoothed her hair and murmured, “The nights are still cool, aren't they?”

Chiharu let out a breath, glanced briefly at the sky, and shrugged with a weary expression.

“I dread the thought of how hot it's going to get from here on.”

As Yukino slipped the Wish Ticket into her purse, she decided to pursue the small talk a little further.

“Chiharu-san, which do you prefer, summer or winter?”\
“Winter, without question. And I'd still say winter if you asked me in winter, of course. Tokyo is overwhelmingly better in winter. No insects, no sweating. You can deal with the cold by putting on more clothes, but you can't take them off just because it's hot when you're outside. If aliens came down and told us they'd turn Tokyo into a place where it was either summer or winter all year round, and made us vote on it, I'd choose winter so overwhelmingly that the blood of the summer supporters would turn Shibuya Scramble Crossing red.”\
“I can tell you really prefer winter if you're willing to go that far in your imagination to make your point.”

Yukino giggled, thoroughly convinced by Chiharu's emphatic speech, complete with a raised finger.

“Well, that said...”

Chiharu continued, gazing at the stars in the distance.

“If we're talking about which I like more, it's winter. But I don't dislike summer either. The smell of the humid air, the sun setting late, the swaying wind chimes and glowing paper lanterns. And the season when a breeze feels pleasant rather than biting is summer.”

As if affirming her words, a breeze blew through the still-not-quite-sweltering air of early summer.

It was soft and warm, though Yukino knew that in winter, the same wind would surely sting their skin.

As Yukino savoured Chiharu's beautiful words, Chiharu's cheerful gaze settled on her.

“Which do you prefer, Yukino? Choose your answer carefully. If the aliens come, I don't want to have to kill you.”\
“Ehh...? P-please don't kill me. I think... it might be summer.”

Yukino's answer came after a moment of apparent deliberation, and there was something suggestive about it.

When Chiharu asked why, Yukino's cheeks took on a faint blush, barely visible in the moonlight, and she murmured softly.

“When I'm grown up and look back on the happy memories of my youth... I think I'll remember this time.”

Summer. The sports festival organising committee. The season that had brought Chiharu and Yukino together.

As she gradually understood what Yukino meant, Chiharu's face slowly brightened into a smile.

“...I don't intend to change my answer, whether it's summer or winter.”

Yukino tilted her head at Chiharu's seemingly unprompted remark.

“But by winter, your answer might have changed to winter, too. If we're following that logic.”

Unable to grasp the meaning behind Chiharu's confident smile, Yukino tilted her head and thought it over.

Chiharu didn't elaborate any further.

Yukino had said that when she grew up and looked back on her happy memories, she would remember the present. That was why she chose summer. So if her answer became winter by the time winter arrived, it meant that they intended to keep making memories together.

Once she understood, Yukino decided that asking Chiharu to confirm it would only spoil the moment.

“Perhaps,” she replied with a radiant smile.

Understanding from that answer that her feelings had been conveyed, Chiharu smiled back. Then she turned her expression playful, stretched her arms high above her head, and sighed.

“But then again, I'm popular, you know. Everyone might be fighting over my time by then.”

Chiharu smirked as she boasted, but Yukino couldn't exactly deny it. She closed her eyes for a moment.

“Perhaps.”\
“Oh? You're agreeing with me?”\
“I can't deny it. I think you're a truly wonderful person, Chiharu-san.”

Miyazono Chiharu was an exceptional person. Not merely in terms of ability, but in her character, too.

The more Yukino came to know her, the more she felt herself sinking into Chiharu's charm, as though drowning in it.

And so Yukino said it plainly. Chiharu looked embarrassed, lightly brushing her faintly reddened cheeks as if to hide them. Then, apparently trying to cover up her embarrassment, she glanced exaggeratedly into empty space and turned the conversation around.

“But you're not a bad person yourself. I wouldn't recommend telling just anyone about your condition, but why not make a friend you can tell something like, ‘Listening to people's voices makes me feel sick’? I'm sure you could find friends who'd try to make you happy, too.”

It was an abrupt change of subject, but the substance of what she said was entirely serious.

Yukino's smile faded a little as she gazed at the moon in the distance.

“...Friends, huh...? Do you think someone like me could make any?”\
“Well, it doesn't have to be a friend. A romantic partner would be fine, too.”

Romantic partner.

Repeating the phrase silently to herself, Yukino found herself staring at Chiharu.

Unaware of the feelings behind Yukino's gaze, Chiharu looked back at her, apparently enjoying the prospect of chatting about romance with a friend.

“...Is there no one? Someone you're interested in, I mean?”

For an instant, Yukino wondered what would happen if she said Miyazono Chiharu's name right then.

But she couldn't bring herself to say it. Her romantic feelings for Chiharu were genuine, but so was her own cowardice. If admitting them risked destroying their relationship, Yukino would rather not let it progress any further.

Yet if there were no risk of breaking what they had, she wanted to move forward.

That was the sort of despicable, timid person she was.

Yukino gazed vaguely at the moon and thought for a moment.

There was a phrase, The moon is beautiful, isn't it?, which was sometimes said to be a declaration of love. There were various theories about its origins, but apparently it came from Natsume Sōseki. According to the story, when translating I love you into Japanese, he supposedly suggested that The moon is beautiful, isn't it? was more appropriate than the direct I love you.

Yukino had heard that this reflected the idea that Japanese people weren't inclined to confess their love so directly. Now she understood why the argument had some merit.

She was glad it was night.

With an awkward, faint smile, Yukino let the dim expression melt into the darkness.

“...I'm not very knowledgeable about that sort of thing. What about you, Chiharu-san?”

It wasn't a lie. Although she'd steered the conversation away from its original subject, the answer she received might still make a difference.

Yukino watched Chiharu with a tentative smile. Chiharu pursed her lips and thought for a while.

“Me? Hmm... I haven't really thought much about that sort of thing either. I'm not interested in anyone at the moment.”

Yukino felt the tension drain out of her body, and immediately realised that what she'd released had been a mixture of nervousness and hope.

Chiharu saw Yukino hide her disappointment behind a smile and raised one finger.

“This is just my personal theory, but I think life is a road.”\
“A road?”\
“That's right. We walk a road that's different for each of us from birth, and along the way, we come to forks where we make choices. I think friends are people whose paths happen to run near ours. We become curious about them, learn about them, and occasionally take little detours from our own paths. As we do, we influence one another.”

Intrigued by the idea, Yukino murmured, “I see,” and turned it over in her mind.

It made sense. They came to know people because they were walking along similar paths, and they could walk side by side because their destinations were close.

As Yukino nodded in admiration, Chiharu returned to her earlier point, looking into the distance.

“But a partner is different. I think a partner is someone who keeps taking forks in the road, as far as possible, towards the other's path, until eventually the two roads, which should never have met, merge into one.”

The difference between a friend and a partner, whether a romantic couple or a married pair.

Would they continue walking their own paths, occasionally taking a small detour? Or would they turn aside from their chosen route for the other person?

“That's an interesting way of looking at it. Taking a detour, or changing your path... is that the distinction?”\
“Exactly. In other words, I think a partner is someone for whom you need to change the path you're walking.”

Chiharu continued straight ahead, her steps steady and unwavering, along the road that branched away from the busy entertainment district.

“I'm satisfied with the life I'm living, so I don't want to change my path for someone else.”

Watching Chiharu's profile as she spoke, gazing straight ahead, Yukino quietly narrowed her eyes and softened her expression.

The beautiful moon was obscured by the fragments of clouds drifting across it, and the night grew a little darker.

“Besides, I have lots of friends. I think I'm better suited to cherishing those relationships.”

If Chiharu was determined never to change her path, then perhaps the best kind of relationship was one where they could meet along the way, taking little detours whenever they felt like it.

Yukino, too, couldn't bear the thought of someone changing the course of their life for her sake. And so, in the end, she could only reach the same conclusion as Chiharu.

“...Perhaps I really should make some friends after all.”

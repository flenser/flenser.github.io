And then, three days later. The weather was fine, and the festival apparently went ahead as planned.

The middle of the afternoon. With the air conditioning switched off, Chiharu Miyazono’s bedroom was hot enough to make her sweat beneath her clothes.

The six-and-a-half-tatami room was lit by sunlight filtering through lace curtains. Its pristine white walls enclosed an ordinary arrangement of furniture: a bed, a study desk and a wardrobe. Beside the wardrobe stood a full-length mirror.

Chiharu checked her reflection, now dressed to go out.

She had chosen practical clothes over anything cute: easy-to-move-in grey shorts, ankle socks, and a thick black shirt with three-quarter-length sleeves that concealed the contours of her body. Perhaps her choice was a gesture of consideration towards Yukino Hasekura, who had gone so far as to lie to her in an attempt to keep her at a distance. But Chiharu did not dwell on it.

Satisfied, she sat on the bed and checked her phone.

She and Konatsu had exchanged several messages confirming that they would be going to the festival as planned.

Then, without changing what she was doing, she opened her chat with Yukino.

Their last exchange consisted of a single pair of messages from Wednesday. Chiharu had asked, What shall we do about the festival? Yukino had replied, Wouldn't it be better if we didn't go this time?

Even if Yukino were to message her now, saying, Actually, shall we go after all?, Chiharu could hardly agree. It would be unfair to Konatsu.

And yet, her heart still found itself hoping for a message from Yukino.

Disgusted with herself, Chiharu turned her phone face down and let out a long, thin sigh, relaxing her shoulders.

I like Yukino Hasekura. I think I do.

But if someone asked whether those feelings were really just sympathy, she still had no answer. Perhaps wanting to be with Yukino came from a protective instinct born of pity. Perhaps her impulse to take Yukino’s hand had been an arrogant desire to possess her. Perhaps it was not romantic affection at all.

If that were her only concern, she thought she could gradually work through it together with Yukino.

But now that Konatsu had confessed to her as well, she did not want to cast her aside with some half-hearted excuse.

I have to make a decision.

Thinking this, Chiharu checked the time on her phone.

Then, telling herself she could not show such a face to a friend who had gone to the trouble of inviting her, she slapped her cheeks and stood up.

*

The Yōka Festival was a long-established favourite in Tokyo. The stretch of road from the nearest station to just short of the city hall was transformed into a pedestrian zone, extending so far that even an out-of-shape adult would be out of breath if they tried to run its length. Lanterns lined the road, their light stretching so far into the distance that it seemed they might reach the horizon, their glow drowning out the streetlights. Countless stalls crowded both sides of the street. The moment you laid eyes on it, the whole place was unmistakably bursting with life and excitement.

*

Escalators were dangerous, so people were supposed to stand still while using them. In other words, there was no reason to leave the right-hand side clear for people in a hurry. Even so, most people kept to the left to avoid trouble. Chiharu joined the crowd and descended the escalator leading down from the station.

Almost at the same moment, the deep, ground-shaking beat of drums struck her below the knees, urging her feet onwards.

It was 5:30 p.m. A faint orange tint was beginning to bleed into the sky, and the streets were already packed with people, like the tide at its fullest.

A solitary fountain stood in front of the station. Around it sat people waiting for someone, like the petals of a sunflower.

Among them was one petal that shone brighter than the rest. Her friend, with vivid blonde hair tied into a ponytail, wore a cool-looking collared shirt and shorts as she waited, gazing at her phone.

The slanting evening sun cast her face into shadow, making her expression difficult to discern.

But when Chiharu approached with a smile, the girl noticed her and looked up. Chiharu watched as her face was instantly transformed, tension giving way to delight. She was glad to see how happy her arrival had made Konatsu. At the same time, she felt disgusted with herself for being unable to return those feelings, and her smile faded a little.

“Chiharu. Y-you came… Though I suppose that's a strange thing to say. H-hey. Good to see you.”\
“Sorry to keep you waiting. Good grief, it's quite something today… It's hot, and there are so many people.”

Chiharu swept away the shadow that had fallen over her expression, raising a hand to her forehead as she gazed out over the sea of people, her eyes half-lidded with exhaustion.

“Yeah. It's my first time here, too, but I didn't expect it to be this crowded… I usually make a point of avoiding places like this around festival time.”\
“I used to enjoy this sort of thing, but these days we're practically drowning in entertainment.”

They could have gone on making small talk until the sun went down. Instead, Chiharu looked at Konatsu and gestured towards the crowds.

“Anyway, there's no point standing here talking. Shall we go?”

Konatsu tried to hide her delighted, softening expression by pursing her lips.

Then she gave a little, uncontrollable laugh.

“Hehe.”

And nodded.

Rather than swimming through the crowd, they surfed its waves. In other words, the two of them neither fought against the flow of people nor tried to push their way through. Instead, they joined the grand procession, moving at a painfully slow pace that seemed almost designed to waste their limited leisure time. They listened to the rumbling drums and the lively hubbub that seemed to embody the festival itself, along with the stallholders calling out to attract customers from every direction.

Red, black, white, blue, yellow. Stalls in vivid primary colours glowed beneath their warm lights, and together with the sinking sun, they made the whole world seem red.

Goldfish scooping, water-balloon fishing, shooting galleries, cotton candy. Food, food, and more food. The two of them took in the scene, the sort of sight that made you want to declare that this was what a festival ought to be, and enjoyed it together.

Then Chiharu looked at Konatsu.

“If there's anything you want to buy, just tell me. I'm the sort of person who's perfectly happy just looking.”\
“So, you're window-shopping.”\
“On a day this hot, that's quite appropriate. Though even just looking is still hot.”\
“But we've come all this way, so…”

Konatsu surveyed the countless signs and stalls lining both sides of the street.

Following her gaze, Chiharu felt a little of her gloomy mood lift beneath the orderly rows of lanterns. “That's true,” she said, allowing herself a faint smile, and decided to enjoy the festival.

She disliked carrying extra things, so they mostly bought food they could eat on the spot and throw away immediately afterwards. They bought cotton candy in different colours, whispering unpleasant things to each other about the cost price. Then they bought takoyaki and split it between them, both falling silent for a while because it was so hot.

Compared with the girls their age who were excitedly walking around in yukata, their way of enjoying the festival was rather subdued. Strangely, though, it felt perfectly comfortable.

One of the stalls was selling masks.

It was not one of those commonplace stalls selling dubious imitations, seemingly unaware of copyright. This was a proper stall run by a local shop specialising in traditional crafts. The masks were made of paper, with an assortment of Japanese-style shapes and painted designs.

“Masks.”\
“Proper ones, too.”\
“Yeah.”

Konatsu gave a brief, matter-of-fact response to Chiharu's remark, then walked past the stall as though uninterested.

But she slowed down, glancing back at it again and again. Perhaps she thought it was childish to show an interest.

Noticing this, Chiharu chuckled, stopped walking and smiled at her.

“Shall we have a look?”

Konatsu's cheeks coloured slightly, and she pursed her lips in embarrassment. Then, at last, she hurried over to the stall.

Chiharu followed at a leisurely pace, watching as Konatsu happily bought a proper fox mask. The shopkeeper, a kindly-looking man with a fearsome, craftsman-like face, saw them off as they left. Konatsu cheerfully placed the mask on her head, wearing it to one side rather than covering her face.

“Look at this! Isn't it cute?”

Konatsu tilted the mask towards her, her lips curling into a delighted smile. Chiharu thoughtfully touched her chin.

“It is cute. The paper's sturdy, and isn't that paintwork done by hand?”\
“The shop makes masks for festivals and religious ceremonies, so the quality's guaranteed. Didn't you want to buy one, Chiharu?”\
“I don't really like having more things to carry around. If I feel like looking at one, I'll just turn my head.”

Chiharu gave a little laugh, and Konatsu cheerfully showed off her mask.

As the stalls and lanterns gradually receded behind them, the orange in the sky began to deepen into indigo. The lanterns shone ever more brightly, illuminating the world.

They had been walking for about half an hour, but at their leisurely pace, the end of the street was still a long way off.

“Apparently, the fireworks start at seven. Let's stay and watch them.”

While they were walking, Chiharu had spotted a festival poster displayed on what looked like a noticeboard.

She had not been paying attention, but sure enough, the poster listed the time when the fireworks would begin.

The stalls continued even farther west from here, and the fireworks were to be launched over the river beyond them.

They had come all this way, so it would be a shame not to enjoy the whole thing.

“Sounds good,” Chiharu replied, nodding.

Still, the best viewing spots would already have been taken. She did not mind if they could not get a perfect view, as long as they could find somewhere quiet enough to watch in peace.

Thinking this, Chiharu looked around at the crowds.

Konatsu noticed and peered into her eyes with concern.

“Are you tired? Shall we take a break?”

She's worried about me.

Chiharu found herself thinking this with a strange sense of detachment.

Konatsu was in love with her. She wanted to take care of her, and she was worried about her now, too. And yet Chiharu had left her confession unanswered, continuing their friendship while she struggled to decide what to say.

The thought brought her abruptly back to reality, and she gave a faint smile.

“No… Actually, yes. Shall we take a little break?”

The two of them walked a little farther before turning into a narrow, quiet alley around the middle of the street.

There were only a handful of stalls here, and beyond them, after a little more walking, the darkness spread out ahead.

Apparently, this area was also part of the festival grounds. A rubbish bin bearing the festival's name had been placed inside the road closure, so they decided to take a short rest here.

As they considered whether there was anything they could eat or drink while resting, their gazes wandered almost in unison. They spotted a shaved-ice stall at almost exactly the same moment, then looked at each other.

Before they knew it, they were standing in front of the deserted stall, each ordering a shaved ice.

“Blue Hawai— No, strawberry, please.”\
“Ah, I'll have melon. I'd appreciate extra syrup.”

“Coming right up,” said the middle-aged stallholder, narrowing his eyes in amusement as he took Konatsu's order.

Well, it never hurts to ask, Chiharu thought, impressed. She took out her wallet, but stopped Konatsu with a hand.

“I'll pay here. I do work part-time now and then, and… I've been treating you rather badly, Konatsu.”

Konatsu looked as though she wanted to protest, asking what Chiharu meant. But the melon shaved ice arrived at just that moment, and she accepted it with both hands, her expression complicated.

Each serving cost 250 yen. Chiharu took a 500-yen coin from her wallet, placed it on the cash tray, and accepted her red shaved ice.

The two of them carried their desserts towards the rubbish bin in the corner. Along the way, Konatsu spoke in a dissatisfied tone.

“I'll pay you back. I have a part-time job, too.”

As she tried to take out her wallet with one hand, Chiharu stopped her with her own.

It was one thing to have a petty argument over such a small amount of money. But that was not the only reason.

“I know it's hard to accept. But… I've been thinking again about how badly I've treated you. I'd be glad if you'd let me pay. For the sake of my own guilt, too.”

After a few seconds of hesitation, Konatsu put her wallet away, frowning.

“Your guilt?”

Chiharu sat down on a tall kerb beside the rubbish bin and let out a deep breath. As she settled onto it, the heat rising from the ground seemed to brush against her cheeks. The moon looked beautiful overhead, but the damp air washed out the stars.

A distant lantern illuminated one side of her face in orange. Her arms, with her elbows resting on her knees, cast a shadow across the asphalt.

“I know how you feel about me… I know what you're hoping for. And I know myself that it's difficult for me to give you what you want. And yet here I am, spending the festival with you.”

Konatsu's eyes widened slightly. She looked at Chiharu's dimly lit profile, untouched by the lanterns and illuminated only by the faint reflection of moonlight. Then she sat on the kerb beside her, turning her mask around so that Chiharu could see her face, and so that she could see Chiharu's.

Konatsu's expression was tense and troubled. Chiharu's words had struck home, but there was also something else she wanted to say. She struggled to find a way to convey her feelings without leaving anything out or saying more than she meant.

Communicating the formless thing that was the heart to another person was extraordinarily difficult. Perhaps the heart wove words and moved the body, but whatever could be perceived through those means was ultimately only the surface. So long as the heart remained imprisoned within the body, these vague emotions could only ever reach another person in vague form.

Konatsu hoped that Chiharu would return her feelings.

And because Chiharu had accepted her invitation to the festival, that hope had grown.

But she also acknowledged that Chiharu's feelings remained firmly within the bounds of friendship.

Carefully disentangling the frustration and reason tangled together inside her, Konatsu allowed herself a brief, faint smile.

“Whatever you feel, Chiharu, I'm just happy that you accepted my invitation.”

But Chiharu did not seem satisfied. She narrowed her eyes, lightly biting her lip as she chose her words.

“…I feel as though I'm not being honest with you.”\
“That's mutual. I'm not being honest, either. In the first place, this is all my fault. I couldn't bring myself to accept things as they were. I thought that if you hadn't confessed yet, there was still a chance for me, so I got in the way of two people who liked each other. I said all those high-and-mighty things about whether it was sympathy, and whether you could make her happy… But I wasn't genuinely worried about any of that. I just found an opening and drove my attack straight through it.”

Konatsu grabbed her fringe and twisted it, her face contorting in self-mockery.

Apparently, she had said something to Yukino as well. Had that contributed to the way Yukino had been acting until recently? Chiharu was curious about what exactly she had said, but trusted that Konatsu had not done anything illegal. Besides, she knew that the current rift between them was not Konatsu's responsibility alone.

As Chiharu was thinking this, Konatsu continued in a voice so small it was almost inaudible.\
“And besides…”

*

“Actually, I already know. I know everything.”

*

Her voice was like a droplet filled with loneliness, and she did not specify what she meant. For a moment, Chiharu needed time to take in the meaning of those words.\
But gradually, as her eyes adjusted to the darkness, she made out Konatsu's face and understood.

She was smiling. There was a trace of resignation in that faint smile.

Her hopes were genuine. But at the same time, she understood.

It had already been a week since her confession, and Chiharu was still agonising over her feelings for Yukino. She was still agonising over them now. Konatsu understood that there might no longer be any room for her to intervene, and wondered whether she could really go on hoping.

She understood that her love would not be fulfilled.

The moment Chiharu realised this, her eyes wavered.

Before she knew it, her mouth had opened. Her emotions formed words.

“I…”

But Konatsu gave a smile that seemed to say she had had enough, and stuck her spoon into her shaved ice. The pleasant crunch interrupted Chiharu, as the artificial scent of melon mingled with the summer festival air.

“Don't apologise. I'm fine with things as they are.”

Beside Chiharu, who had been left speechless, Konatsu gazed at her shaved ice with lingering regret and took a bite.

“Are you not going to eat yours?”

She asked with quiet eyes, and Chiharu began eating her own, a flavourless red shaved ice.

The sky had been clear all day, and perhaps because of that, the air was hot and humid. The ice in her mouth seemed to cool her brain a little, overheated from all her thinking.

Eventually, Konatsu spoke matter-of-factly, gazing up at the starry sky.

“I'm not stupid, you know. After having my answer put on hold for a whole week, and spending all that time looking at the face of the person I confessed to, I can tell whether I have a chance or not. You're putting off answering me because you want to put your feelings for Hasekura into words and give me an honest answer. It's not because you're undecided. You accepted my invitation to the summer festival because you see me as a friend.”

It was as though an arrow had been fired straight into the centre of a target, hitting its mark without the slightest deviation.

That was exactly what it meant to hit the nail on the head. Chiharu's eyes wavered as though Konatsu had seen right through her thoughts. Sensing the grief behind Konatsu's words, she called her name.

“Konatsu…”

Konatsu laughed off her own feelings with an exaggerated show of bravado, then continued eating her shaved ice with theatrical gestures.

“When you think about it, it's only natural. I love you, too, and I'm sure that won't change no matter what anyone says.”

And that's why you understand that nothing I say from here on will make me fall in love with you.

Her eyes told Chiharu as much. Watching Konatsu's profile, Chiharu held her breath and closed her eyes.

Then she felt those words catch somewhere deep in her chest and gently pressed a hand against it. As though afraid to look at Chiharu, Konatsu searched the faded summer night sky, where no fireworks had yet appeared, for the Summer Triangle.

“So, you don't need to feel guilty about going to the summer festival with me and getting my hopes up. I understand everything. And even knowing all that…”

Konatsu hugged her knees tightly, smiled, and curled in on herself as she squeezed the words out from the depths of her heart.

“…I still want to dream for just a little longer. Until you understand how you feel about Hasekura.”

That was the final piece. The puzzle she had been working on for a week was now complete.

Are my feelings for Yukino Hasekura really romantic? Or are they just sympathy?

The answer to the question Konatsu had posed to her, Chiharu now understood through the words Konatsu herself had spoken.

“Ah… I see.”

She murmured just that, and her shoulders immediately relaxed.

Then, just for today, she tried to put on a smile, to hide what she had realised.

After all, Konatsu had worked up the courage to invite her. They had come somewhere fun, and they were going to watch the fireworks together.

But Konatsu had caught even the slightest shift in Chiharu's expression, so subtle that Chiharu herself had not noticed it. The look of understanding that appeared just before her smile was as though she had realised something she should never have noticed.

And so it was surely not an understanding of the feelings Konatsu had just expressed.

It was a realisation that would hurt Konatsu simply by being recognised.

And there could only be one thing.

Konatsu looked at Chiharu, who had tried to conceal something, with a smile tinged with resignation and regret, silently reproaching her.

When I think about it, that's what made me fall for her in the first place. The way she could act out of consideration for someone else.

Receiving Konatsu's gaze, full of those feelings, Chiharu looked away as though to hide her guilt and turned towards the main street.

“…We shouldn't keep resting here. Shall we finish our shaved ice and head back?”

Chiharu said this, casually pushing her spoon into the ice.

Konatsu could simply nod, enjoy the rest of the day without a care, and greet tomorrow.

That was the path she had wanted.

But as the person who had torn apart the time shared by two people who loved each other, she could not ask for anything more.

Ignoring the polite pretence, Konatsu asked directly.

“It's all right. You don't have to pretend… You've found your answer, haven't you?”

This time, Chiharu's face twisted with unmistakable self-loathing and guilt.

“No, don't worry about it. It's nothing.”\
“I know. I've been watching you all this time.”\
“Now isn't the time to tell you. I don't want to trample on the courage you showed, or ruin this time we've spent together.”\
“I tried to get what I wanted, even if it meant trampling on the precious relationship between you two.”

Konatsu understood the source of Chiharu's guilt.

Answering her confession now would ruin the day. It would inflict a wound that might never heal on Konatsu, who had found the courage to invite Chiharu to the festival.

“Let's watch the fireworks. We promised, didn't we?”

Chiharu murmured the words as though they were difficult to breathe out.

“Together, for the last time?” Konatsu asked.

Chiharu's expression crumpled further.

Unfortunately, Konatsu preferred sparklers to fireworks. The way they crackled quietly, scattering their flowers unseen. The scent of gunpowder drifting on the breeze, giving summer its distinctive character.

So she would become one of those, too. Leave the fireworks for the leading characters, and let this supporting character's love story come to an end here.

“I understand. You're kind, Chiharu. You don't want to end a day that I worked up the courage to invite you to by leaving things awkward between us. But it's all right. I'll treasure the mask I bought, and I won't forget the taste of the cotton candy, takoyaki and shaved ice we ate together. Not for decades, at least. Those memories won't disappear.”

As she spoke, self-loathing and self-mockery gradually crept into her voice. Her tightly wound emotions, the faint hopes she had allowed herself to entertain, and the cuteness she had been putting on all began to peel away, one by one.

Even so, she softened her cheeks into a smile and said, “So…”

It was as though she were trying to give Chiharu some oxygen, who was struggling to breathe.

“Let's end things here. You don't have to choose your words carefully. Tell me everything honestly.”

Chiharu bit her lip and lowered her head, letting out a frail sigh.

It took courage and resolve not only to be hurt, but to hurt someone else.

Chiharu straightened her back, looked up at the sky, and drew in a deep breath of the summer festival air. Then…

*

“I… feel sorry for you, Konatsu.”

*

The words were gently entrusted to the summer breeze. They were spoken with concern for Konatsu, and carried the painful warmth of friendship. And yet they struck her chest with the coldness of midwinter, tearing apart every feeling she had held for Chiharu and shaking the very foundations beneath them.

But Konatsu smiled softly.

Then what about your feelings for Hasekura? Are they really affection, and not pity?

The question she had once put to Chiharu had travelled full circle, and now it was Konatsu who was pierced by it.

Chiharu's face looked close to tears. Konatsu smiled in stark contrast. Their gazes met in the darkness.

“I see… Ah, I see. That makes sense. So that's your answer.”

After smiling as she said this, Konatsu felt her vision suddenly blur. Desperate to hold back her tears, she blinked rapidly, bit her lip, smiled, and ate another spoonful of shaved ice.

Chiharu thought that if this had conveyed everything, perhaps she should fall silent.

But Konatsu continued to look at her quietly.

She wanted Chiharu to sever everything, right to the very end.

Accepting that wish, Chiharu resolved herself to hurt her and decided to say everything.

“I have a friend who's been in love with me for a long time, and only now has she told me she likes me. I don't know whether it's because I now understand what it means to fall in love with someone, or because she's someone I'm particularly close to. But I wanted to face your feelings as sincerely as I could. And I think that, in itself, is sympathy.”

Konatsu, who had been quiet, gave a small laugh.

“Hehe.”

Perhaps it was the relief of finally letting go.

Chiharu, who had seemed to be struggling for breath, gradually drew in air. Only loneliness remained on her face.

“And I'll admit it. Just as you said, I pity Yukino, too. Her synaesthesia is a major obstacle in her relationships with other people, and it causes her so much suffering.”

“I suppose it is,” Konatsu murmured quietly.

Gazing down at the pool of strawberry-coloured liquid that had gathered at the bottom of her cup, Chiharu continued.

“So I… pity both Yukino and you, Konatsu.”

What goes around comes around.

It hurt to have those words turned against her, but she was the one who had brought them about. Konatsu gave a small, ironic laugh. Chiharu, by contrast, grimaced and pressed a hand against her aching chest.

“And even so… I've spent this past week looking for a reason why I could choose Yukino.”

Their eyes met. Konatsu had already made her decision, so there was no reason to stop now.

“I think that's the difference that gives me my answer. Now I can say it with confidence. Sympathy doesn't negate love. I've realised that the two feelings can exist together.”

Her feelings had originally been an indistinct form of romantic affection. She had been vaguely drawn to Yukino, and her heart had vaguely begun to race around her.

Perhaps that was enough for most romantic relationships. But when the person in question was Yukino Hasekura, those vague feelings might one day lose their boundary with sympathy. And if there was someone else who loved her, then she might end up hurting that person through nothing more than shallow thought.

That was why Chiharu Miyazono needed to find an answer. Her doubts had served a purpose.

Together, they looked away. They faced forward, watched the stars, and listened to the clamour of the people enjoying the festival around them.

*

“I pity Yukino, and I love her. That's no longer a lie.”

*

The feelings she had been unable to express to either Yukino or Konatsu, Chiharu had finally managed to put into words.

“At last… I've found the courage to make you cry.”

Chiharu Miyazono looked at Konatsu with a solemn expression, a firm resolve in her eyes.

Sayama Konatsu returned her gaze with a gentle smile, as though honouring that conviction.

“I'm sorry, Konatsu. I can't return your feelings.”

It had taken Chiharu a week to give her those words.

And thanks to that, Konatsu had been able to dream for a week.

Having finished speaking, Chiharu lightly bit her lip, a trace of loneliness and guilt crossing her expression.

Why are you the one who looks like you're about to cry?

Konatsu felt like making some flippant remark, but instead took a deep breath. She was sure she would remember the scent of this summer for a long, long time.

“Thank you. For facing me properly… for properly hurting me…”

Her eyes suddenly grew hot, and her voice caught. But she would not cry. She held herself back, knowing that she must not.

Her eyes were a little damp, but she kept smiling. Then, in order to put an end to what she herself had done, she apologised.

“I'm sorry. I did something awful to both of you.”\
“No. Even if you hadn't pointed it out, it was a wall we would have run into sooner or later. You were the catalyst, not the cause. I don't want to call the feelings you had for me something bad.”

For words offered to the loser, Chiharu's sincerity was almost unbearably warm.

That's why I fell for her.

Konatsu savoured the thought, then let out a breath as though something had finally been settled. In a somewhat joking tone, she left her feelings here, and let them go.

“Honestly… I sometimes thought about things like, what if Hasekura and I had been in different classes, or what if we'd joined the same club? What if we'd gone to the same middle school? Pathetic things like that.”

Chiharu looked serious as she prepared to respond to these pitiful wishes.

“I…”

“No, don't answer. I don't want to face reality… and Hasekura probably wouldn't like it, either.”

She had treated Yukino rather cruelly, too. She wanted to apologise to her properly, but that could wait until later.

Now, truth be told, Sayama Konatsu did not have many friends, either. Part of that was because she had never particularly sought them out herself. But if someone asked whether she disliked friendship, the answer would be no.

Which was to say, she had come to feel a certain fondness for Yukino Hasekura, who had called her a friend.

“I haven't known her for long, but she's a really good person. Take care of her, okay?”

It might have been stating the obvious, but Chiharu did not make light of it. She nodded deeply.

“…I promise.”

Konatsu nodded, satisfied. Her cup gave a faint scraping sound in her hands.

She casually brought the melting shaved ice to her mouth and drank down the remaining liquid, like juice.

Chiharu stared at her in surprise, wondering why she was eating so quickly, then began to eat her own. Meanwhile, Konatsu rose to her feet, tossed her empty cup into the rubbish bin beside Chiharu, and stood up properly.

“Well, I'll head home ahead of you.”

Chiharu's eyes widened. In a panic, she hurriedly began eating her shaved ice.

“Wait, I'll come with you—…………… No… Ah, right.”

Suddenly, she stopped.

“Actually, I'd better not.”

Now that she had fallen in love with someone, she understood that this was not the time for her to stay by Konatsu's side.

As expected, Konatsu smiled in the dim backlight of the countless lanterns, looking as though she were relieved.

“I'm glad you feel that way.”

For an instant, Konatsu's face twisted as though she were about to cry. Chiharu narrowed her eyes against the backlight and pretended not to notice.

Konatsu put on her fox mask, as though hiding her face behind a memory. Then she took a deep breath and waved.

“See you next week. As friends.”

She would not walk home with Chiharu, but she did not want to sit there and watch her leave, either.

Chiharu rose slowly, met her eyes, and expressed her gratitude.

“Thank you, Konatsu. I'm glad it was you who fell in love with me.”

She could not return Konatsu's romantic feelings, but Chiharu had come to the conclusion, after facing her own heart, that Konatsu was still a special friend to her.

That was why she thanked her.

For several seconds, the fox mask silently gazed at Chiharu. Eventually, its shoulders gave a slight shudder, and it turned away.

“Thank you, too, for cherishing my ‘love’. I'm glad you were my first love.”

Her voice was so muffled that the mask alone could not account for it.

A sparkler went out.

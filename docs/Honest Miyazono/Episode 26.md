“I think there are some things you can only understand by talking about them.”

Remembering Chiharu’s words, Yukino revised her thinking. She realised that Chiharu had been right.

She was always learning from her.

Back in her dimly lit bedroom, Yukino looked at the phone glowing on her bed. It had been left there when her mother came home, still displaying her message exchange with Chiharu.

The three messages she had checked earlier were still marked as read. She had left them unanswered.

That must have seemed rather rude. Sitting down beside the bed, Yukino began to type a reply, but then hesitated.

Her heart wanted to enjoy the summer festival with Chiharu. Her reason, however, held her back, worrying about the unhappiness of anyone who entered into a romantic relationship with her. And now, having taken her mother’s words to heart, she was thinking that perhaps she should let someone accept her own unhappiness, too.

Three conflicting impulses clashed in her mind, and she could not bring her thoughts together.

It was selfish. In the end, she would be choosing to make someone else unhappy simply because she wanted to be happy herself.

Yukino could not bring herself to believe that living as arrogantly as her mother was something that should be accepted. She rejected her own selfishness, and tried to resolve that, for Chiharu’s sake as well, she should not pursue a romantic relationship. Then she typed a lie she could utter quite calmly because it was not being spoken aloud: “I’ve already made other plans for today.”

She placed her finger over the send button, but could not press it. Instead, she put down her phone, buried her face in the sheets and groaned.

Her fingers scratched at the bedding. Hot, trembling breaths passed in and out of her throat.

She wanted to see her. She wanted to see Chiharu. To hold her hand. To eat together.

Thinking about it any longer threatened to drive her mad. Yukino slowly raised her head and glanced at the send button on her phone, but still did not press it. Instead, she tried to distract herself. Then came a faint rustle from the little pocket of her pyjama shorts.

She remembered the money her mother had given her.

She checked the four Fukuzawa Yukichi banknotes, an absurd amount to receive as casual pocket money.

Then she got up, opened the wallet on her desk and went to put the money away.

That was when she noticed a small piece of paper inside.

A torn-off page from a notepad.

She picked it up and looked at it.

The room was pitch-black. The only light came through the window, where the curtains had been left open. Moonlight.

*

One Ticket to Make a Selfish Request.

*

The moment she saw those words, they blurred until she could no longer read them.

The backs of her eyes and nose grew hot, and tears ran down her cheeks.

Yukino lightly bit her lip and wiped her eyes with the short sleeve of her pyjamas. But the tears would not stop.

A sob escaped her. She drew in a breath with all her might, trying to keep her voice down, and held the precious scrap of paper in both hands.

To fall in love with someone while possessing a form of synaesthesia that let her understand other people’s feelings, especially their secrets and lies, all the while knowing that this condition would make that person unhappy.

That was selfish.

Was she really allowed to make such a selfish request? Would someone accept it?

Just then, the phone she had left on the bed began to vibrate.

Brrr… brrr…

The drawn-out vibration was different from the pattern used for incoming messages. It meant someone was calling. With her vision blurred, Yukino made her way towards the bed, careful not to stumble, and checked the screen.

The caller was listed as Chiharu Miyazono.

As tears continued to spill down her cheeks, Yukino’s trembling finger hovered uncertainly between the answer and decline buttons.

Then, driven by nothing more than the desire to hear her voice, she tapped the green Answer button.

“Hello?”

After she answered, there was a silence lasting several seconds, so long that she almost wondered whether the call had failed to connect.

But she could hear the hubbub of the festival grounds through the phone. The call was definitely connected.

At last, Chiharu spoke, her voice tinged with a laugh that sounded slightly relieved.

“…Thank goodness. I thought you might have come to hate me. You weren’t replying.”

As though trying to embrace the sound of Chiharu’s voice, which she felt she had not heard in so long, Yukino curled up.

She bit her lip, searching for words. With tears still falling, she tried to explain why she had taken so long to reply.

“That was… because, just as you suggested, Chiharu-san, I had a proper conversation with my mother for the first time in ages.”\
“Oh, really?! How did it go? Did you make up?”

There must have been all sorts of things Chiharu wanted to talk about, but she skipped straight over them to ask that.

Yukino sniffed and rubbed her eyes roughly. Then, with the faintest hint of a smile, she answered in a muffled voice.

“It wasn’t as though we’d had a quarrel, but… I think we cleared up some misunderstandings.”\
“That’s wonderful! I knew it was worth talking. I’m glad if I was able to help.”

A proud little laugh, Hehe, came through the phone. Forgetting the awkwardness of recent days, Yukino remembered the time when they had been so close, spending their days together. She sniffed and wiped away her tears.

“…Are you crying?”

Chiharu must have noticed from the moment the call began, but she waited a little before asking in that gentle voice. Somehow, it sounded as though she were telling Yukino, It’s all right to cry.

The feelings she had carefully locked away deep inside began to rise up again.

A sob escaped her. She could not stop it. So, even as she struggled to speak, she forced herself to convey what she felt.

“Chiha… Chiharu-san…”\
“Mm-hm.”

Chiharu waited patiently, her voice gentle, as Yukino struggled to piece together her broken words.

Yukino looked at the scrap of paper in her hand. The words One Ticket to Make a Selfish Request were written on it. Her vision blurred in an instant, making them impossible to read, but she held the paper firmly and made her selfish request.

“…I want to see you, Chiharu-san.”

A laugh came through the phone. It sounded happy, but there was a slight catch of tears in it.

“Of course. Let’s meet. Let’s talk in person. I want us to have a proper conversation.”

A tear fell onto the memo paper with a soft plip. Yukino did not even notice. She rubbed her eyes and remembered all the insincere things she had said when they parted. Then she apologised.

“I’m sorry… I’m so… so sorry… I’m sorry for lying… The truth is…”\
“Mm-hm. I know. I know who you like, and I know what you’re thinking.”

Even without Yukino spelling everything out, even without the ability to detect lies, Chiharu could see straight through the lies Yukino told so transparently. In a soft voice, she continued.

“But this might just be my own assumption. And you might have your own assumptions, too. That’s why I want us to meet and talk properly. I want to see your face.”

Then, in a bright voice that betrayed no hesitation, Chiharu put the past to rest.

“I’m sorry, too. I’m sorry I made it difficult for you to talk to me back then.”\
“That was… well, my condition is the problem. It wasn’t your fault, Chiharu-san…”\
“Mm-hm. I’m not going to deny that.”

Yukino had honestly expected her to deny it, so the response caught her completely off guard.

But denial would have been disingenuous. A lie. After that day, Chiharu had come to understand how difficult it was to talk to Yukino. Pretending that difficulty did not exist and taking refuge in superficial kindness would surely get them nowhere.

Unlike the startled Yukino, Chiharu spoke with the clear resolve of someone who had made up her mind and overcome her doubts.

“I’m done with pretending otherwise. I understand perfectly well how difficult this will be. It’s true that there are hardships between us that wouldn’t exist if you chose someone else. I accept that. And even so…”

She paused.

“…I want to see you. I want to meet Yukino and talk.”

Yukino realised how shallow and naïve her own understanding had been.

She had thought that a relationship with someone who possessed synaesthesia that let her understand other people’s feelings would mean having their secrets exposed, and becoming unwell whenever they told her something they wanted to hide. One person would have to yield, and the other would suffer.

But that was only the starting point. Knowing full well how difficult things would be, they could still look for a way to face them together.

Someone precious to her had held on to a path that Yukino herself had given up on and cut away.

Yukino bit her lip and desperately wiped away her tears. She managed to stifle her sobs, then asked one final question.

“…Even if… Sayama-san could make you happier than I could?”

Would she respect Chiharu’s wishes, or prioritise Chiharu’s happiness?

Yukino was certain that Sayama Konatsu could make Chiharu happier than she ever could.

“Is that what you think, Yukino?”

Chiharu’s quiet, composed question seemed to probe that very thought.

Yukino took several shallow breaths, then faced reality and admitted it honestly.

“…Yes. I do.”

She wiped away her tears once more, then made her selfish request in a trembling voice.

“But… I want you to choose me.”

She was not a better person than Sayama Konatsu. She might not be able to make Chiharu happy.

Even so, she wanted Chiharu to choose her. She wanted to be with her.

That was the selfish request she made.

Chiharu let out a sigh and took a deep breath, sounding overcome with emotion. Then she replied in a happy voice, as though something had finally fallen into place.

“Mm-hm. Thank you. That’s what I wanted to hear.”

Then she said, “All right, let me answer your question,” and began to explain her own thoughts on the question of happiness Yukino had raised.

“Yukino. I… I don’t want you to make me happy. I wanted to make you happy. At least, that’s what I thought until just a little while ago. But… I realised I was wrong.”

Having decided to pursue her own feelings even if it meant hurting someone else, Chiharu Miyazono sat down on a kerb and looked out over the stone-paved street, illuminated by countless lanterns, where happy-looking people wandered past.

In the first place, Chiharu Miyazono was not someone who wanted other people to do things for her.

And she had wanted to make Hasekura Yukino happy because she felt sympathy for her condition.

But then there had been someone who wanted to make her happy. She had hurt that person. After going so far to follow through on her feelings, her own pride would not allow her to live a life in which she remained unhappy. And yet she did not want someone else to do things for her. Nor did she want to give up on making someone happy.

“I think the essence of being with someone is that a new subject comes into being: us.”

It was a complicated place, where duty and desire intersected.

“Rather than making each other happy, or having the other person make us happy… let’s be happy together.”

Even Hasekura Yukino, who could not be happy on her own, might find happiness alongside the honest Miyazono Chiharu.

Yukino took those words to heart and held them close.

Then, for the last time, she wiped away her tears.

And with a voice that had finally regained its composure, she answered, giving shape to the happiness that had until then remained so uncertain.

“Yes.”\
“Do you know where I am? Let’s meet there.”

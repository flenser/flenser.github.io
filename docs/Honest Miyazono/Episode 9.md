“On behalf of the school, I would like to offer my heartfelt apologies for causing our students such considerable anxiety.”

About a week later, the principal bowed deeply as he addressed the entire school at an extraordinary assembly held in the gymnasium.

Several teachers bowed their heads alongside him. Ordinarily, the restless chatter of the students would have drawn reprimands, but today it seemed no one had the heart to put a stop to it.

Around the time Shinohara, the civics teacher, had suddenly stopped coming to school, news outlets began reporting the name of the school, his name, and the crime he had committed: voyeuristic filming. Naturally, the facts spread among the students in no time at all. By the time reporters began gathering outside the school, the long-awaited assembly was finally convened.

From the back of the gymnasium gallery, Yukino and Chiharu quietly looked down at the noisy students.

“...The camera was installed recently. The purchase dates of the associated equipment confirm that. He didn't sell the recorded footage to anyone outside the school. He used it all for his own gratification...”

Chiharu murmured as she browsed an online news article on her phone.

“If he'd been desperate for money, that would be one thing, but if that wasn't the case, why couldn't he have stopped himself somewhere along the way...?”\
“Hmm. Everyone has desires, and those desires can be fulfilled. I think that's a fairly standard starting point for a lot of crimes. But most people don't commit them because their reason holds them back. So, if we're asking whether he could have stopped himself, the answer is that anyone could have. Shinohara simply chose not to.”\
“Perhaps there's no point in dwelling on it.”

“Mm.” Chiharu offered no objection to that conclusion and quietly slipped her phone back into her pocket.

“...So, how is it? We've managed to pull a few strings this time, so you can listen from the gallery.”

Chiharu looked into Yukino's eyes with a rather proud smile.

“It's much better,” Yukino replied, gazing down at the commotion.

The idea had been Chiharu's. There would surely be an uproar at today's assembly, and Yukino would be subjected to considerable psychological strain. So why not use the fact that she had reported the incident, along with the extent of her contribution, as leverage to negotiate permission to listen from the gallery in situations like this?

It was a clever solution that Yukino would never have thought of on her own, and one she probably could not have arranged even if she had.

But Chiharu had made use of all sorts of lies in Yukino's absence, along with the trust she had built up with others, and had secured their homeroom teacher's permission for this assembly, at least.

“At this distance, the jumbled din has no taste. It's a tremendous relief.”\
“Then I'm glad.”

Chiharu's face broke into a delighted smile at Yukino's gentle reply.

“Since meeting you, Chiharu-san, I've begun to... just a little... reconsider how I think about this condition of mine.”

Yukino murmured, closing her eyes to conceal the lingering uncertainty in them.

Chiharu rested a hand on the gallery railing, though she did not lean her weight against it, and gazed at the principal in the distance.

“...As you said, I think lies are a kind of lubricant that people need in order to communicate smoothly with one another. So you, who expose every one of them, will inevitably create friction and wear yourself down, whether you confide your secrets in someone or keep them to yourself. I won't deny that.”

Feeling a little sad, Yukino quietly closed her eyes.

Perhaps someone as clever as Chiharu could have denied it. Perhaps Yukino had been hoping she would. She had hoped Chiharu might find a way for her to live happily, as though nothing were wrong, despite having this condition.

But that hadn't happened. And that made her sad.

Still, if the facts could not be changed, then she ought to change how she faced them.

“But perhaps, once you've exposed a lie and torn away its veil of concealment, you might be able to protect someone, as you did this time. Of course, I can't simply call that a good thing when it comes at the cost of your own suffering.”

Yukino quietly repeated Chiharu's words, turning them over in her mind.

“Protect someone...?”

Amid the hubbub, the principal's words continued in a solemn cadence. A cacophony in which lies and truths mingled together.

Away from it all, in a gallery shared by just the two of them, they spoke softly to one another.

“That's right. In other words, I think you have a condition that could make someone happy. So, Yukino...”

I don't think I'm someone who can be happy.

Those words of Yukino's had stayed with Chiharu. She must have been thinking about them for a long time. And now, she proudly presented the counterargument she had arrived at in her own way.

“I think you should make happy the person who makes you happy.”

Sometimes, when people spoke to one another, their words entered the body by some route other than the accumulation of knowledge. The first kind of reception might be called remembering something in the brain. But what of the second?

As Yukino pondered this, she understood. So this was what it meant for something to sink in.

For several seconds, she stared at Chiharu in astonishment. Chiharu's deep eyes met her gaze and held it.

Happiness. Yukino had never defined it precisely.

She had simply been unable to imagine herself as happy whenever she thought about her future.

And yet, Chiharu's words had built a bridge towards the faint glimpse of happiness she had once seen in her fantasies, something she had believed would forever remain beyond her reach. It felt as though a film reel, which should have run out long ago, had somehow managed to turn the projector's wheels a little longer, revealing a little more after an interminably long stretch of darkness.

The happiness Yukino could imagine: making someone happy, and being made happy by someone in return.

And when she pictured such a happy future, the first face that came to mind was Miyazono Chiharu's.

At last, Yukino let out a small, breathy “Ah.”

Chiharu tilted her head at the indistinct murmur, but Yukino's eyes softened with affection. She had finally recognised her own feelings.

As she had grown closer to Chiharu, she had gradually become aware of a vague sense of danger. Now, at last, she understood its source.

For the first time in her life, she was becoming so absorbed in someone that she felt as though she were drowning in them. That feeling had a name.

*

She was in love with Miyazono Chiharu.

*

The moment she acknowledged it, her heart began to beat rapidly, as if confirming that she had found the right answer.

The rush of blood brought colour to her cheeks, red blooming across Yukino's snow-white skin, as pristine as freshly fallen snow.

Chiharu tilted her head, then gently reached out and touched Yukino's cheek, a faint smile playing on her lips.

“Your colour's a little better today, isn't it?”

She was treating Yukino like a sick person, but given how poor Yukino's diet really was, perhaps she had good reason to.

Feeling heat against her cheek as though she had touched a flame, and her heart pounding wildly, Yukino placed her own hand over Chiharu's, as if to catch it and keep it there.

Her fingers were beautiful, soft and slender.

“...【I wonder why. It's strange, isn't it?】”

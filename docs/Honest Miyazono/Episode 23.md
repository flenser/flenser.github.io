From then on, every morning when she woke up, Hasekura Yukino would play some random video on her phone.

Whenever the emotions in the performer's voice reached her as tastes, stimulating her synaesthesia, she would close her phone in despair and burrow back under the covers. The same thing, over and over. She would spend the day going through the motions, her mood dark and listless, all the while hoping that perhaps, by some mistake, her condition might have disappeared.

And so Monday of the following week arrived.

Yukino dragged her heavy feet to school, only to feel someone tap her on the shoulder near the entrance.

She flinched and drew her head in. Her eyes, still ringed with dark circles, widened as she turned around apprehensively.

There stood Miyazono Chiharu.

Unlike Yukino, Chiharu looked as beautiful and composed as ever. Seeing Yukino in an even worse state than usual, she furrowed her brows.

“Good morning. You look dreadful. Haven't you been sleeping?”

Yukino hadn't expected her to speak so casually. She stared in bewilderment for a moment, then cautiously nodded.

“G-good morning. I've [just] been a little… short on sleep. I'm fine.”

Chiharu narrowed her eyes.

“Then that's good.”

She took off her shoes.

Yukino followed suit, changed into her indoor shoes, and was about to set off when Chiharu said, “Wait a moment.” She began rummaging through her bag, eventually pulling out a lunch bag.

Yukino's eyes widened.

“Your lunch. I made it again today, so eat it. If you've bought any sweet buns, I'm confiscating them.”

With that, Chiharu thrust the bag into Yukino's hands in a manner that left no room for argument.

Until last week, Chiharu had made lunch for her as a matter of course, without even needing to ask. But Yukino had assumed there was no way she would do so today. She was still too surprised to take it in.

Then the other students, passing through the entrance with their usual morning lethargy, seemed to splash cold water over her astonishment, and little by little she came back to herself.

Her face twisted.

“I… I can't accept it.”

She had lied, knowing perfectly well that Chiharu would see through it, saying she loved Konatsu in an attempt to resolve their problem.

Yukino clasped her hands behind her back. Someone so insincere had no right to accept Chiharu's kindness.

“Just take it.”

Chiharu grabbed her arm and pulled. The difference in their strength made resistance futile, and she simply thrust the bag into Yukino's hands.

It was heavy. The weight of Chiharu's affection, and of the early morning she had spent preparing it, settled into Yukino's hands. Without warning, her eyes began to sting.

She felt warmth gathering behind them.

“Um…”

But she couldn't find the words to say.

Chiharu interrupted her, looking somewhat troubled, yet with her shoulders relaxed.

“I have absolutely no intention of believing that god-awful lie of yours, but… even if I did believe it, a friend is still a friend, isn't she? Besides, I don't think I'm entirely blameless either. Ah… How should I put this…”

She had started out trying to explain herself seriously, but apparently had gradually decided it was too much trouble.

Chiharu closed her eyes and scratched her head. A few seconds later, she held out her hand with an exasperated, half-lidded look.

“Here.”

Smack.

She gave Yukino a casual slap on the cheek, just hard enough to be felt, but not enough to hurt.

“Ah?”

Yukino let out a foolish little cry, her eyes wide as she clutched her cheek.

Chiharu gave her the gentle smile Yukino loved so much.

“There's fish in it. Fried white fish.”\
“R-right…”\
“Eating fish makes you clever. Seems just the thing for an idiot, doesn't it?”

Chiharu gave her a mischievous grin, as though taking her revenge.

Yukino bit her lip, the corners of her brows drooping.

Something hot welled up at the corners of her eyes, and she rubbed them vigorously with the short sleeve of her blouse.

Then, in a muffled voice, she said, “Yes,” and decided to accept it, foolish as she was.

Naturally, they ate lunch separately that day.

Chiharu was invited by Yamazaki and ate across from her at her desk. Yukino sat alone on the bench in the open space at the eastern end of the first-floor corridor, where nobody else was around.

Under the midsummer sunlight streaming in, she opened her lunch box. Inside, it was filled with all her favourite foods.

Which dish did you like best in today's lunch?

Chiharu always used to ask her that.

And today, she had packed nothing but Yukino's favourites.

Yukino had lied to her, hurting her in the process. And yet, despite being the one who had been hurt, Chiharu had taken the trouble to think of Yukino and cheer her up.

Realising that, tears began to fall, one after another, into the lunch box.

She hurriedly wiped them away with her sleeve, sniffled, and carried a piece of sweet, thick Japanese omelette to her mouth.

It tasted just a little salty.

*

Two days later, Sayama Konatsu stood motionless in the corridor.

It had come to her attention that the Yōka Festival would be held nearby on the second Saturday of July, in other words, three days from now.

The festival took place a little before the summer holidays. It was famous for attracting large numbers of visitors from outside the prefecture every year, sometimes even drawing the attention of the media. For Konatsu, it was a truly trivial event, little more than a minor detail in the grand scheme of her life, and usually just a reason to avoid taking the train at that time.

But it was Wednesday, three days before the event, and the person she loved had yet to answer her confession.

Having discovered the festival's existence on the noticeboard in her high school's corridor, she could no longer stand still.

“So, that's the situation. What do you think? Saturday. The festival.”

Konatsu had waited to catch Chiharu as she came out of the classroom after school. The response was hesitant.

“Hmm…”

Chiharu kept her bag over her shoulder, folded her arms, rested a finger against her chin and furrowed her brows. A low murmur escaped her for several seconds.

“If you already have plans, that's fine.”

Konatsu spoke rather hurriedly, for some reason sounding a little flustered.

Perhaps Chiharu simply didn't want to go with her. No, that wouldn't be fine at all. But Konatsu had enough sense to accept it if that was the case.

As it turned out, Chiharu really did seem to have a prior commitment. She took out her phone.

“Let me check.”

She glanced into the classroom before operating her phone, and Konatsu had a fairly good idea who the prior commitment involved.

Even so, she pretended not to know either its identity or the guilt that came with it, and waited for Chiharu's answer.

“They've read it immediately, so give me a moment. If there's no reply within a minute, I'll answer you another day.”\
“Ah, right. I'm not in a hurry, so just let me know when you…”

Before Konatsu could finish, Chiharu's phone vibrated.

Bzz.

A reply had arrived.

Konatsu's heart pounded so hard she thought it might leap out of her chest. She clasped her hands together in front of her mouth, watching anxiously, as though she were trying to warm them on a cold winter's day.

Chiharu read the message, narrowed her eyes in exasperation, and opened her mouth as if to let out an exaggerated sigh.

Then she reconsidered, apparently realising that it might hurt the girl standing in front of her, and let a smile form instead.

“My prior engagement has been cancelled. Shall we go together? To the summer festival.”

In that instant, Konatsu clenched both fists, her cheeks flushing with delight.

Her eyes sparkled. The corners of her mouth lifted ever so slightly.

Then she seemed to realise how excited she had become. Her eyes widened as she came back to herself, and she clasped her hands against her chest. Biting her lip to suppress the smile threatening to break out, she bounced restlessly on her heels.

Seeing this adorable, almost childish display from a friend who clearly held affection for her, Chiharu felt as though it had been a long time since she had laughed aloud.

“……”

But then, without warning, her smile darkened.

She loved Hasekura Yukino, and had wanted to spend the festival with her. If Yukino had cancelled their plans, was it really appropriate to fill that time with the person who had confessed to her instead?

Of course, Chiharu had no ulterior motive. She intended simply to enjoy herself with Konatsu as a friend.

But what would Konatsu and Yukino think?

The thought made her expression stiffen.

Konatsu seemed to read her mind from her face alone. It was almost enough to make Chiharu suspect she possessed some kind of supernatural power.

“I haven't had an answer to my confession, remember? For now, we're still just friends.”

Konatsu closed her eyes, gently dispelling Chiharu's misgivings.

Chiharu returned a self-deprecating smile.

“…Yeah. Thank you.”

—I confessed to Chiharu.

Yukino received that single sentence from Konatsu and spent the rest of fifth period in a state of agitation. The moment the lesson ended, she hurriedly got to her feet. Her eyes, clouded with anxiety and unease, turned towards Chiharu at the front of the classroom. Chiharu had her head in her hands, looking troubled and lost in thought. That only made Yukino more anxious.

Had Chiharu accepted Konatsu’s advances? Or had she turned her down?

What was behind that troubled expression? Was she agonising over her decision, or feeling guilty about having rejected her?

Well, whatever the case, Yukino could hardly settle her mind without hearing what had happened.

She accepted Konatsu’s invitation to talk and hurried to the girls’ toilets at the eastern end of the first floor, a place that was gradually becoming something like their own secret hideout. The place where they had discussed the sports festival committee, and then the incident with Chiharu’s notebook. When Yukino thought of Konatsu, or Konatsu thought of Yukino, this was where they ended up.

The break between lessons lasted ten minutes.

When Yukino rushed into the toilets, breathless, all the cubicles were empty, as usual.

The lights were on, illuminating the bright, clean space. Sayama Konatsu was leaning against the washbasins, the overhead lighting casting a faint shadow from her fringe over her eyes. She was idly fiddling with her phone. When she noticed Yukino arriving, she gave a tired little smile, lacking her usual energy, and waved.

“Sayama-san.”

Yukino came to a gentle stop on the linoleum floor, diagonally in front of Konatsu. Konatsu put her phone away.

“Hey. Sorry for summoning you so suddenly.”\
“No, that's... quite all right. More importantly, about the confession...”

When Yukino hurriedly pressed her for the main point, even Konatsu, who was usually so direct, seemed to search for her words with a slightly uncertain smile.

—Well, that’s how it is. We’re not suited to each other, and I don’t think she’s the sort of person who would fall in love.

—But if, by some tiny chance, there were even a possibility... I’d confess.

The other day, Konatsu had spoken those words about her feelings for Chiharu. They had not been a lie.

Which meant that something in Konatsu’s feelings had changed. Or perhaps she had decided there was a chance for her after all.

As Yukino stood there, a sheen of sweat on her skin, anxiously asking what had happened, Konatsu finally began to explain.

“[Actually, Chiharu and I were going out a year ago.]”

A lie. An utterly obvious one, with no discernible intention of concealing the truth.

Yukino felt the unpleasant sensation of thin, watery vomit on the tip of her tongue and furrowed her brows. Half of her reaction was disgust, the other half distrust. She instinctively covered her mouth and fixed Konatsu with a wary stare.

“...W-what are you trying to do?”\
“Sorry. I wanted to find out just how sensitive your condition is... So I tested it. If you can see through that, then there's no point in trying to use lies as a strategy. I'll answer honestly.”

In other words, Konatsu was admitting that she had been prepared to deceive Yukino if it would help her win.

Yukino reflexively gave her a half-lidded glare. Konatsu folded her arms and gave a slightly sheepish laugh.

“So I want you to answer honestly, too. Do you know who Chiharu likes?”

Yukino's face faltered. She pressed her lips into a thin line, clenching both fists at her sides as she struggled with herself.

The answer was simple. She did know.

There might be some wishful thinking and conceit mixed into her conclusion, but if she looked at the situation realistically, the answer was obvious. And yet, she had always avoided putting it into words. But at this point, she could hardly keep it to herself any longer.

After taking several shallow breaths, Yukino spoke.

“I... I’m the one.”

Konatsu closed her eyes for several seconds, as though savouring the weight of that fact, then slowly nodded.

“I won't question whether that's true. I've heard your answer. Now, let's move on. Chiharu came to me for advice about her love life. She didn't tell me who it was, but she wanted advice about someone she had feelings for.”

That was effectively an answer in itself, but Yukino made no comment about the person's identity.

Yukino waited in silence, her expression grave, as Konatsu calmly explained what had happened.

“At that point, the premise I'd been working from all along, that Chiharu wasn't going to fall for anyone, fell apart. Chiharu was someone who could fall in love. She was prepared to change the course of her life for someone else. And she was about to confess. But, put another way, that was proof there was still a chance.”

She paused. Seeing Yukino's face twist as she sensed what was coming, Konatsu continued.

“So I thought this was probably my first and last chance. And I confessed.”

Yukino couldn't help feeling pity for Konatsu, and Konatsu knew it. Even so, she apologised.

“I'm sorry, Hasekura. I confessed because I wanted to challenge the romantic feelings between you and Chiharu.”

Yukino understood the whole sequence of events. Chiharu loved Yukino, and Yukino loved Chiharu. But that very fact meant Chiharu had shown herself willing to pursue a romantic relationship. And so, once Konatsu learned of it, she had no choice but to make her move.

“Chiharu-san hasn't given you an answer yet. She's asked you to wait, hasn't she?”\
“Yeah. Were you worried because she looked troubled in the classroom? I feel bad about putting you through that, too.”\
“No. If your feelings aren't an attempt to steal someone else's partner, then everyone has the right to tell someone how they feel.”

Inwardly, though, Yukino was anxious. She considered Konatsu Sayama an attractive woman.

Even so, now that she had fallen in love with someone herself, she could understand Konatsu's feelings.

But Konatsu seemed to see right through her, hiding her twisted smile behind one hand.

“...You're awfully composed for someone who's confident she'll be chosen.”\
“N-no, that's not it...”

Under ordinary circumstances, Konatsu would never have lost her composure to this extent. She must truly love Chiharu. Yukino's eyes wandered as she brought her fingertips together in front of her chest, trying to defend herself.

In truth, she felt as though she and Chiharu had more or less confirmed their feelings for one another. But then... if that was the case, why had Chiharu asked for time to consider Konatsu's confession?

The question suddenly stirred Yukino's anxiety.

And as though deliberately widening that wound, Konatsu continued with a quiet smile.

“Not long ago, you said you didn't think you could ever live a happy life, didn't you?”\
“I... I did. But what about it?”\
“Does that mean a romantic relationship with Chiharu wouldn't make you happy?”

It was an unpleasant question. Yukino thought about it for a moment, then slowly shook her head.

She did believe that her condition made her unhappy, though perhaps that was an overly self-deprecating way of putting it. But ever since meeting Chiharu, that belief had gradually begun to change. When she was spending time with Chiharu, she could forget about all that unhappiness. Faced with those facts, she could no longer insist otherwise.

“No. I've realised it through the time I've spent with Chiharu-san. The time I spend with her really has made me happy.”

It was almost a rebuttal, but Konatsu nodded with an air of satisfaction.

“I see. So Chiharu makes you happy.”

Yukino regarded her with suspicion, wondering what she was getting at. Konatsu looked up into the empty air with a playful smile.

*

“—Then, can you make Chiharu happy?”

*

“Huh?”, Yukino's voice sounded like that of a fool who had been asked a question from an entirely unexpected angle. She had been talking about her own happiness without having considered her beloved's happiness at all. Konatsu, convinced she had found an opening, gave her a smile that was strangely gentle for the occasion.

“It's important, isn't it? I think both people in a relationship should be happy.”

Yukino's eyes wavered with shock. Her throat had suddenly gone dry, and she struggled to force the words out through the rough air.

“I-I think so, yes. I... I want to make her happy.”\
“I'm not asking what you want. I'm asking whether you can. Think about it. Can someone who believes she herself can't be happy make someone else happy? I sympathise with your condition, but sympathy doesn't create happiness, does it? People who know about your condition might refrain from lying to you out of consideration. That creates friction.”

Unlike Konatsu, who spoke fluently, calmly and at length, Yukino could do little more than repeatedly exhale ragged breaths from her parched throat. Her eyes were wide as she stared at the floor, cursing her own selfish expectations.

She had thought only about her own happiness. About being made happy.

And yet, when it came to making the person who mattered most, Chiharu Miyazono, happy, she had given it no thought at all.

Seeing through the childishness of Yukino's love, Konatsu neither looked down on her nor belittled her. She simply fixed her with a resolute gaze, determined to tell her a plain fact.

“I'll make Chiharu happy. I'm confident I can. What about you?”\
“I... I...”\
“Perhaps you can overcome the problems with your attitude as you grow. But what about your condition? A lie isn't inherently evil. Sometimes ignorance is bliss. Sometimes a little deception is expedient. And sometimes beauty lies in what remains concealed. There's no way two different people can build a relationship in which everything is exactly as they both wish. Each person has to accept the parts of the other they can live with, for the other's sake. They hide their true feelings and swallow them down. That's the lubricant that keeps things running smoothly. Take that away, and all that's left is friction.”

And Konatsu believed that would be unhappiness. She stated her conviction without the slightest hesitation.

Her words, like thick, murky mud, fell like rain upon Yukino's parched heart.

There was no doubt that this was an attack intended to knock Yukino, her romantic rival, out of the running.

But it was also a reality Yukino had been deliberately avoiding. That was precisely why she could not argue back. She fell silent, her eyes darting restlessly from side to side. Several times, words came to mind, only to be swallowed again when she realised they did not even qualify as rebuttals. She clasped both hands into a single fist in front of her navel, her face paling slightly.

Konatsu watched her like that for a while, then checked the time on her phone.

She hesitated. After gazing into the empty air and carefully choosing her words, she quietly pushed herself up from the washbasin.

“I'm your enemy, and I'm going to say things that suit me and work against you. But even putting that aside... if you don't think you can make the other person happy, then I don't think you should be in a relationship at all.”

Those words were entirely sincere. There was not a trace of deception in them. Yukino fell silent, her face stricken with pain.

Konatsu began to walk away. Near the entrance, she turned back towards Yukino.

“Shall we go back?”

Yukino returned her gaze weakly, then immediately looked down at the floor.

“I... need to think about it.”

Konatsu's expression briefly betrayed a trace of guilt. She covered her face with one hand, then left after quietly saying: “I'm sorry.”

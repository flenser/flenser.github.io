The following morning, Chiharu Miyazono woke up and, unusually, made no move to get out of bed. Instead, she lay face down.

The first thing that came to mind was what had happened yesterday. Yukino Hasekura’s words: “I like women.” “There’s a woman I like.”

She buried her head in her pillow. It felt as though steam might start pouring from the top of her head, like a humidifier.

Her body was hot. Her face was hot. Her chest ached.

She had no intention of getting carried away with herself. But if she put together all the information she had gathered objectively, then the person Yukino had feelings for was, in other words, herself. And those feelings were mutual. Which meant she could stop hiding her own like and take a step forwards.

Chiharu lay face down, hugging her pillow tightly. With her face buried in the shampoo-scented fabric, she mumbled something incomprehensible, then finally spoke, as though making a vow to herself.

“I’m going to confess.”

*

She decided to do it on the day of the Yōka Festival. In keeping with its name, it was held on the second Saturday of July.

About a week from now. Chiharu Miyazono resolved to prepare herself for that day.

Calling it ‘preparing herself’ might sound like something out of a romantic story, but in practice it amounted to a rather pathetic exercise in diplomacy. In other words, clearing the ground, making arrangements, gathering evidence. It was all preliminary groundwork.

The plan itself was simple. She would ask Yukino’s friend whether it seemed safe to go ahead and confess.

And, as a bonus, since this would be her first romance in her life, it would be a stroke of luck if she could get some advice.

The person she settled on was their mutual friend, Konatsu Sayama.

At lunchtime, Chiharu told Yukino, unusually, that she would not be able to eat with her that day. Clutching her aching chest, she made her way to the second-year Class B classroom. Just as Konatsu had once come to borrow a textbook, Chiharu opened the front door and scanned the desks, still arranged in attendance-number order, looking around the section for names beginning with S.

The classroom grew suddenly noisy at the sight of Chiharu, the famous beauty, appearing at the door. Amid the commotion, she spotted Konatsu, who had been about to open a convenience-store lunch box, staring back at her in surprise.

“Konatsu, there’s something I’d like to talk to you about. Would you have lunch with me?”

*

Konatsu closed the lunch box she had just opened, slipped it into a plastic bag, and followed Chiharu with a puzzled expression.

The place Chiharu had chosen for their private conversation was the courtyard between the classroom building and the club building, connected by the western passageway. At its centre stood a circular flowerbed and a fountain sparkling in the sunlight, with red brick paving laid out in a ring around them. Paths extended in the four cardinal directions, while the spaces beyond the plaza and paths were filled with grass, shimmering and swaying beneath the midsummer sun in a cloudless sky. Several benches stood around the perimeter. With the direct sunlight beating down on them, the heat today was such that anyone sitting there for long would soon find themselves breaking into a gentle sweat.

Both Chiharu and Konatsu wore traces of nervousness on their faces as they began eating their lunches.

“Summer’s really come on all at once lately. It’s only the beginning of July, and yet it’s unbearably hot.”\
“I’m amazed. You summon someone here yourself, then the first thing you say when you run out of things to talk about is the weather. The weather.”\
“Let’s talk economics, then. What do you think about yield curve control?”\
“I take it back. Let’s talk about the weather.”\
“‘Hey, it’s about to get sunny.’”\
“That’s Weathering with You. It’s already sunny.”\
“Hey, I’m about to start talking.”\
“That’s what I’m waiting for.”

Chiharu kept circling around the subject with one digression after another. Her expression was stiff with nerves, and a peculiar sheen of sweat had appeared on her face. Watching her, Konatsu found her own tension gradually easing.

But what could Chiharu possibly want to discuss that had her this nervous? And why had she chosen to discuss it with her?

Konatsu grunted as she ate her lunch, glancing at Chiharu from the corner of her eye before turning her gaze towards the annoyingly glittering fountain.

Could it be…? Hope. Then disappointment at the thought that it couldn't possibly be. The two feelings rose and fell within her like the pans of a set of scales. She stole a glance at Chiharu’s troubled profile, her gaze fixed on nothing in particular. Somehow, somehow, she felt she had guessed what the subject was. It came as an unpleasant premonition, and Konatsu fell silent.

Perhaps a minute passed.

At last, Chiharu finally broached the subject.

*

“...I’ve fallen for someone. I’m thinking of confessing, and I wanted to ask your advice.”

*

Konatsu Sayama showed little surprise at the very thing she had half expected Chiharu to say.

But her eyelids, which had been on the verge of closing, opened just a little. She glanced sideways at Chiharu.

Chiharu’s expression was tense, as though she were deeply troubled. Her cheeks were faintly flushed, and she swallowed hard as she stared down at the brick courtyard. Konatsu realised that she herself was not at the end of that feeling, nor along the line of Chiharu’s gaze. She was on a parallel line that would never intersect with it. Understanding that, she relaxed her shoulders, looked up at the blue sky, and let her face go blank.

It was a beautiful sky.

So that was how it was.

The good people she disliked could exist in this world, just as the bad people she loved could. The rights and wrongs of things, her own likes and dislikes, other people’s lives, the way the world was. The inside and outside of oneself could only be connected through action. And as long as that was an absolute, unchanging rule, she had to acknowledge and accept the fact that someone she loved could love someone other than her.

The world was shit.

Konatsu closed her eyes and muttered that to herself.

But Sayama Konatsu was even more shit than the world.

Acknowledging that, she opened her eyes again.

Then, looking at the profile of the woman she loved as she searched for the words with which to ask for advice, she spoke.

“Sorry, Chiharu.”\
“...Huh?”

Chiharu blinked in confusion at the sudden apology. Konatsu remained seated on the bench, turning towards her as much as she could.

“I can’t give you advice about this. I’m not nearly as noble or virtuous as you seem to think I am.”

Chiharu Miyazono held her own life in high regard. Konatsu had no intention of criticising her for that. But the reality was that Chiharu had said she had no intention of changing the course of her life for someone else, and objectively speaking, she was the sort of person who would stand by that. Whatever had changed in her heart, Konatsu did not know. But something had certainly changed. And so Sayama Konatsu would struggle against it.

Chiharu seemed unable to conceal her bewilderment at the self-loathing in Konatsu’s words.

“W-why…? I mean, of course I respect how you feel, but…”

Perhaps if she had been a little more proactive in making her feelings known, Chiharu might have looked at her differently now.

Thinking that, Konatsu found that self-loathing outweighed even her regret. She covered her strained smile, which showed the tips of her canine teeth, narrowed her eyes, and wrestled with her final hesitation.

Ten seconds passed. Then she steeled herself.

*

“I like you, Chiharu. I like you romantically, of course. So go out with me instead of that person.”

*

Her braided blonde hair swayed beside her pure white blouse in the summer breeze.

It was probably an expression Chiharu had never shown before. Her eyes widened in shock, her mouth fell open. But it was not simple surprise. A deep sense of guilt showed plainly on her face. Perhaps simply managing to make her look like that made this confession worthwhile. Konatsu took a deep breath, as though trying to calm her pounding heart.

“Ah… I said it. I finally said it. That’s how it is, Chiharu. I’ve been in love with you for a long time, so I don’t want to give you advice about your love life, and I can’t. And there’s more. I’ll say it plainly. Choose me, not that person. I’ll make you happy.”

Chiharu’s expression twisted beneath the sharp gaze Konatsu fixed on her, a gaze that hardly seemed appropriate for a declaration of romantic love.

Guilt. The guilt of having approached someone for romantic advice only to discover that the person she had asked was in love with her. Put another way, she had brought up the name of someone else in front of a person who loved her. But the fact that she felt guilty about it was itself proof that her heart had not been swayed.

Chiharu swallowed nervously, then steeled herself to hurt Konatsu, her face rigid.

“W-wait, Konatsu. I…”\
“...You’re the one who needs to wait, Chiharu. I want you to hear me out.”

Konatsu cut off Chiharu’s conflicted words with a firm interruption. Chiharu flinched.

Having confessed to someone who had come to her for romantic advice, Sayama Konatsu had already acted in a way that could hardly be called socially acceptable. There was no reason for someone like her to worry about appearances now, or to take care over how she phrased things. She would do everything she could to get what she wanted. If she had no intention of doing that, then a dozen seconds ago she should have been offering her congratulations instead.

“I know Chiharu can’t return my feelings. And of course I know the person you like isn’t me. It’s Hasekura, isn’t it? Hasekura Yukino. She’s the one you love.”

A trace of surprise fell into Chiharu’s bewildered expression.

“...You really are perceptive. You’re right about everything.”\
“You chose the wrong person to ask for advice. Do you regret it?”\
“I don’t regret it. But I do feel sorry for you.”\
“Haha. I wonder if Hasekura would have known that was a lie.”

Konatsu twisted her cheek in a self-deprecating smile, then looked at Chiharu, who had lowered her eyes in pain.

“I regret it, though. I wonder if I should have made a move sooner. Or if I should have turned down your invitation just now… I know, I know. I’m an idiot. I was too late. It’s already too late. There are surprisingly many irreversible things in this world, and feelings and time are two of them. But, you know, I just can’t…”

The self-mockery abruptly vanished from Konatsu’s expression, leaving only her usual nonchalant demeanour.

“Because I really did love you.”

Konatsu looked away. Chiharu turned her face in the opposite direction. For a brief moment, silence held sway.

“If things had been a little more settled, if you’d told me afterwards, for example, I could have accepted it and moved on. I could have told myself I was the one at fault for making excuses, telling myself things like how we’re both girls, or how you weren’t even interested in falling in love with anyone in the first place. I could have pretended to be satisfied with that. But if there’s still a chance to struggle, then I’ll struggle. Of course, your wishes come first. If you don’t want me, then you don’t want me. But there are two things I want to ask you.”

She had no intention of forcing Chiharu to become her lover. If Chiharu said no, Konatsu would accept it.

But that acceptance would come only after she had said everything she needed to say.

No one would reject someone after hearing only the suki of suki desu, Konatsu thought, raising one finger.

“‘Chiharu likes Hasekura. That’s why she can’t return my feelings.’ Is that right?”

After a moment’s hesitation, Chiharu slowly nodded, her expression grave.

Having confirmed that premise, Konatsu raised a second finger.

“Then, what about your feelings for Hasekura? Are they really affection, and not sympathy?”

Chiharu’s entire perspective, the very foundation of her thoughts, was shaken. Her vision seemed to sway.

“……………What?”

After opening and closing her mouth several times, she finally managed to squeeze out that single question.

She liked Hasekura Yukino. That was what Chiharu had believed.

But what if her feelings for her were sympathy?

When the question was put to her, she could not simply laugh and dismiss it. In other words, Chiharu herself had not examined her feelings closely enough to deny it outright. Konatsu, who had understood her reaction in much the same way, closed her eyes and suppressed the faint trace of guilt that had crept into them.

“I touched on this briefly earlier, but I know about Hasekura’s condition. She can tell when people are lying, but it makes her physically ill. It’s a terrible condition. If she keeps it to herself, she’ll suffer from mistrust of other people. If she tells them, they’ll be unable to keep anything from her. It creates friction in human relationships. And the two of you became close because of it.”

Chiharu asked, weaving her own inference into the question.

“I felt sorry for her. What about you? Are your feelings really not sympathy or a desire to protect her?”\
“I… No, that’s… E-even if they were…”

Chiharu struggled to make her uncooperative mouth move, desperately trying to put together some kind of rebuttal.

“That’s fine. I’ve said everything I wanted to say. If you still turn me down, I’ll accept it.”

But Konatsu was unexpectedly gracious. She was willing, for now, to accept even Chiharu’s attempt to argue back without really thinking it through. She made it seem as though she had finished saying everything she needed to say.

“Even if your love is denied on the grounds that you feel sympathy for someone, if that’s your decision, Chiharu… I’ll accept it.”

Those words were decisive.

Chiharu’s face twisted. She pressed her lips together, lowered her eyes, and sank into thought.

I don’t know.

To be honest, if she were asked whether she felt any sympathy for Yukino, she could not deny it.

But admitting that it was sympathy here and now felt as though she would be denying her romantic feelings for Yukino. She did not want to do that. Yet to deny it with nothing but words would seem like an insult to Sayama Konatsu, who had found the courage to confess to her.

Like a defendant waiting to hear the sentence pronounced, Konatsu sat waiting for her answer. Chiharu forced the words out.

“...That’s a rather underhanded way of putting it.”\
“I told you I wouldn’t choose my methods… No, I don’t remember saying that. Oh well.”

Konatsu laughed self-deprecatingly, but it was obvious at a glance that her smile was strained.

Did she really love her that much?

The thought crossed Chiharu’s mind. And yet, perhaps now, she could understand just a little of that feeling. Even the feelings of those who had confessed to her in the past, only to have their hearts broken.

“I need some time to think. I want to give you a proper answer.”

Chiharu offered that as her answer for the time being. Konatsu let out a quiet breath of relief and smiled.

“...I’ll wait as long as you need. Take your time.”

Then Konatsu took out her phone and began typing a message to someone.

A moment later, she suddenly stopped. She glanced sideways at Chiharu, who sat beside her with a pained expression.

“By the way, would it be all right if I told Hasekura that you came to me for romantic advice?”

Chiharu lowered the corners of her eyebrows and agonised over it for a dozen or so seconds.

She did not know how much Konatsu intended to tell her. But at this point, both of them already knew about each other’s romantic feelings, so there was no disadvantage to be had. Then why was Konatsu asking?

Most likely, when they had first met the other day, they had each realised that the person the other was in love with was herself, Chiharu Miyazono.

After letting out a quiet sigh, Chiharu gave a small nod.

## Chapter 32: Overlapping

\
The two of us walked home hand in hand along the road at dusk. Our strides were uneven, and on the opposite side from our joined hands, our matching scrunchies swayed.

The orange-coloured scenery and the warmth travelling through our joined hands. They inevitably brought back memories of the past. The days when we'd walked home together from after-school care, and later, from the after-school club once we'd reached the upper years of primary school. Back then, Ren was still smaller than me, wasn't she?

As though laying those memories over the present, I gazed at Ren beside me. Her expression, her build, everything about her was different now.

"Come to think of it, Ren, you've really grown."\
"What's that all of a sudden?"\
"When we were in primary school, we used to walk home together just like this. Compared with back then, you've become so much more grown-up."\
"I suppose."\
"You were so tiny and such a cry-baby. You were always running to me in tears. I couldn't even imagine you walking to and from school on your own. Then you joined a club in middle school, and you suddenly shot up in height. You've really grown."\
"...Why do you only remember all the embarrassing things?"

Her hand tightened around mine in protest. I couldn't help smiling.

Ren had changed. Her expression, her build, everything about her.

And yet, strangely, I felt that the outline of who she was had remained exactly the same.

Our relationship had begun in the closest place imaginable, as family. We'd grown apart, and now we were beginning to draw close again. I didn't know where that would lead us. But one thing had never changed: the wish I'd always held. The vow that had first taken root on that day.

I want to protect Ren.

As we walked, I counted on my fingers the things that had changed and the things that had stayed the same. Because the everyday life I wanted to treasure could change shape without my noticing, unless I kept my eyes firmly on it.

Just as Dad had left our family. Just as Ren had drifted away from me.

That's why I wanted to cherish the present as we walked. I squeezed Ren's hand tightly. Then I gently stroked the matching scrunchie resting above my wrist, its colour sinking into the reddish evening light. Precisely because I'd enjoyed every moment of today, I quietly embraced the loneliness that was welling up inside me.

Let's go out again.

The words stopped in my throat. Because I was happy right now, the thought of hoping for an 'again' frightened me just a little. The thought that Ren might leave my side once more frightened me.

At some point, I'd begun surrounding myself with those little safeguards. And it was Ren who broke through them.

"Um... Next week, there's an important tournament for the club. The newcomers' tournament... Big Sis, would you come and watch?"\
"I will!"

I looked up at Ren and answered without a moment's hesitation.

Ren blinked, seemingly taken aback by my enthusiasm, then murmured:

"Thank you."

Without caring who might be watching, I pulled Ren into a tight hug.

"I'll absolutely come and cheer you on, so do your best!"

I gazed straight into Ren's eyes.

Our relationship had suddenly drawn closer. I didn't know where it would lead. But for now, I wanted to hold on to this change, brought about by a single word from Ren. The club was a place she'd found for herself after moving away from me. Being invited into that part of her life was something special to me.

At the hour when the sun and moon crossed paths, the scrunchies, their colours so different, swayed together as though overlapping.

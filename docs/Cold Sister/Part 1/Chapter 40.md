## Chapter 40 – A Promise

\
I could hear Mum and Dad arguing in the living room. Under the covers, Ren was crying, frightened.

“It's all right.”

I held Ren tightly and gently stroked her head, soothing her. I wouldn't cry. After all, I was her big sister. If I cried, there would be no one left to protect Ren.

At some point, Dad had started coming home less and less. And even when he did come home occasionally, he would argue with Mum before leaving again, not to return for a while. It had become a familiar cycle. The word *divorce*, which I'd seen on television recently, crossed my mind.

Perhaps we wouldn't be able to live together as a family any more.

As though trying to chase away that fear, I hugged Ren's little body even tighter.

But that fear eventually became reality.

One day, Dad came home, something he rarely did these days, and called Ren and me into his room.

Then, haltingly, he began to speak.

“Dad won't be able to live with you and Ren any more, Ai.”\
“I'm sorry. I'm so sorry.”

He repeated those words over and over, tears streaming down his face. I'd never seen Dad like that before. He was always so cheerful in front of us. Beside me, Ren began to cry too, as though caught up in his tears.

I didn't cry. Because if I did, it would mean blaming Dad.

In that room filled with the sound of sobbing, I stood still, holding Ren's little hand tightly.

Dad disappeared from our home. Mum had work, so she was away during the day, and there were plenty of days when she came home late. We started attending an after-school care centre, where children could stay until their parents came to collect them.

In the living room, whenever Mum was late coming home, Ren would often cry. Every time she did, I would sit her on my lap and hug her from behind, enveloping her little body in my arms. Then I'd stroke her head, soothing her. Perhaps it made her feel safe, because before long, she would stop crying.

“Thank you, Big Sis.”

Every time she said that, her cheeks bright red, perhaps embarrassed that she'd cried, I would think:

*As her big sister, I have to be strong.*

Driven by that sense of responsibility, I never cried. Not even once.

The town was wrapped in the glow of dusk. Surrounded by rice fields, Ren and I walked home together as we always did, my hand leading her little one.

“Do you think Mum will come home early today?”\
“...Maybe she'll be late again. Mum's busy with work. But that doesn't mean she's stopped loving you or me. She's working hard for our sake, so let's be good and wait for her, all right?”

I said those words to reassure Ren. She nodded, desperately holding back her tears. I stroked her head.

It was then that we saw a parent and child approaching from the opposite direction. A tall father was carrying a little girl on his shoulders.

Suddenly, a memory from long ago flashed through my mind. Sitting on Dad's shoulders, looking out at a world so much higher than the one I knew, stretching endlessly before me. The warmth of Dad's hands holding me securely so I wouldn't fall.

The same warmth trickled down my cheek.

“Big Sis...?”\
“Huh? Why...?”

By the time I realised what was happening, my vision had already blurred. And before I could stop them, tears began to spill out. I stood there in a daze, feeling their warmth on my cheeks. Sobs rose from my throat.

“I'm sorry, Ren. It's all right.”

I desperately wiped at my eyes. I was her big sister. I wasn't supposed to cry. I mustn't cry.

But the tears wouldn't stop. As I continued wiping them away, a small warmth touched my head through the gaps in my blurred vision.

Right in front of me, Ren stood on tiptoe and stroked my head.

“Don't cry. Don't cry, Big Sis.”\
“Th-thank you.”

I somehow managed to squeeze out those words between my sobs.

Bathed in the evening glow, I stood there, gazing through my orange-tinted, tear-blurred vision. The parent and child who had passed us were already out of sight. All I could feel was the warmth of Ren's little palm.

How long had we stood there like that? Suddenly, Ren's hand slipped away. Through my tears, I instinctively looked at her face.

Ren stared intently at me, then took a deep breath, as though steeling herself. And then, as if trying to blow my tears away, she declared with all her might:

“When I grow up, I'm going to marry you, Big Sis! So you won't be lonely, and you won't cry. I'll stay by your side, forever and ever. I'll always be right there beside you!”

As she spoke, she linked her little finger with mine.

“Promise.”

Ren smiled, desperately holding back her own tears. She was trying to reassure me. Trying to stop me from crying.

Looking at her like that, I thought:

*If Ren stays by my side, just as she's promised, then I'll keep protecting her forever.*

*I'll devote my whole life to protecting my beloved little sister, so small, so fragile, so adorable, and so brave.*

“Thank you.”

With those words, I tightened my little finger around hers.

My tear-filled vision was bursting with light, and the world was filled with nothing but Ren. Beside that promise, I made a quiet vow of my own.

◇◇◇

The morning air was a mixture of dreams, memories, and reality. I gazed at Ren's sleeping face. Her peaceful expression was unchanged from when she was little.

Ren had probably forgotten that promise long ago. But I still remembered. I remembered the kindness and courage she had shown me that day.

The promise that had saved my heart, and the vow engraved within it. I hugged Ren's body tightly and whispered:

“I'll always protect you, Ren.”

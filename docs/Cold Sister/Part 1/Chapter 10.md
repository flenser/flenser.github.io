## Chapter 10 – Straightforward?

\
At last, lunchtime arrived.

I hurriedly wolfed down my sweet bun, eating faster than usual. I hadn't been given a specific time, but I didn't want to keep the other person waiting too long.

My nerves made the bread catch in my throat. I forced it down with some tea, then got up from my seat.

“Off you go. Good luck!”\
“Thanks. I'll be back.”

With Tomoka-chan seeing me off, I left the classroom.

It was still early in the lunch break, and there were few people in the corridor. I walked along with my head bowed. In time with the dull, incessant pounding of my heart, all sorts of disjointed thoughts came and went.

What sort of girl would be waiting for me behind the gymnasium? The handwriting in that lovely, carefully written letter made me think it was a girl, but what if it was a boy? Either way, I was going to turn them down, but I would be frightened if he were a big, muscular boy.

Actually, was I really about to be confessed to? No, for that matter, was it even a confession? Tomoka-chan had insisted that it was, but there was still the possibility that they wanted to tell me something else entirely. Perhaps it was all just a prank, and nobody would even show up behind the gymnasium.

As I piled one hypothetical scenario on top of another, I made my way along the corridor, down the stairs and across the landing. I passed through the connecting corridor lined with water fountains.

Even the fleeting sights along the way brought Ren to mind. For a moment, I forgot the dull pounding in my chest. And then, into that brief blank space in my thoughts, the figure of a girl suddenly appeared.

At last, I reached the back of the gymnasium. A girl stood there, leaning against the wall, gazing vaguely at the ground as though lost in thought.

When she heard my footsteps, she raised her head and turned towards me. I could finally see her properly.

She was almost as tall as Ren, with an intelligent-looking face. Unlike Ren, who still had a certain endearing cuteness about her, this girl's eyes were narrowed with an almost nervous intensity. Her gaze carried a force that seemed to pierce straight through me.

I recognised that look.

It didn't take me long to realise where I'd seen it before. She was one of Ren's teammates, the girl who had been staring intently at me yesterday.

“Um, I'm sorry to have kept you waiting. Are you the one who sent me the letter yesterday?”\
“Yes. My name is Rira Shimamoto. I came here today because there's something I wanted to talk to you about, Kawai-senpai.”

The girl who introduced herself as Rira Shimamoto spoke clearly and briskly. There was no room for misunderstanding. Her composure was so far removed from nervousness that, for a fleeting moment, I hoped she hadn't come here to confess to me after all.

But the very next moment, that hope was dashed.

“Senpai, please go out with me.”

Her words rang in my ears, direct and unwavering, without the slightest hesitation.

The sheer simplicity of her straightforward declaration caught me off guard, and a jumble of words slipped out.

“Um, well... Why do you want to go out with me?”\
“Because... I've loved you ever since I first saw you.”

Shimamoto-san answered, averting her eyes from mine, perhaps out of embarrassment. Yet her cheeks retained their natural colour. I thought she really was a mature, self-possessed girl.

I remembered how Ren's cheeks had turned crimson yesterday, and the contrast struck me.

Even so, I couldn't quite believe that someone like her had fallen in love with me at first sight. I couldn't make it feel real. But I did understand one thing: I owed it to her to respond to those straightforward words.

I looked directly into her eyes and told her:

“I'm sorry. I'm happy to know how you feel, but I still don't really understand what it means to like someone, or to be in love. So I can't go out with you.”

I told her exactly what was in my heart, sincerely and carefully, just as Tomoka-chan had advised.

But the line of feeling I'd tried to extend so directly began to tremble. Before it could reach Shimamoto-san, it snapped.

The girl who had seemed so composed suddenly fell apart.

“Um... That won't do. I need you to go out with me! I need you to! Because otherwise, Ren will...”

She grabbed my shoulders and shook me, bombarding me with words. Then, for some reason, she blurted out Ren's name. As though suddenly realising what she'd said, her eyes widened.

“Um, no. That's not... I'm sorry... I'm really sorry. I have to go.”

As though trying to erase the words she'd just spoken, Shimamoto-san said that and hurried away.

She crossed the connecting corridor and passed the water fountains.

I stood there, staring blankly at her retreating back.

The shoulder of my uniform where she'd grabbed me was crumpled.

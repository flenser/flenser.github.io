## Chapter 17 - First Love Is Unrequited

\
It was love at first sight.

I had joined the girls' basketball club on a whim, simply because I was tall. And there, I found Ren. The moment I laid eyes on her, I encountered my first love.

Although Ren, like me, had joined the club as a complete beginner, she quickly made her presence felt. First, there was her natural athletic ability. She moved like someone who had been playing for years, improving rapidly, and her height meant she was soon involved in matches. She was a sudden sensation, a prodigy who had appeared out of nowhere. And with her naturally cheerful personality, she fitted into the club in no time. Both the older girls and her classmates held her in high regard.

But more than anything, Ren was beautiful. Long black hair, and cool, lovely eyes that reminded me of a cat. She drew people's attention simply by being there. Enough to make someone as unremarkable as me fall helplessly in love with her.

Ren was always at the centre of everything, shining brightly like the sun. I could only watch that radiance from afar, carrying a heat in my chest that threatened to consume me.

Although Ren and I were both first-year members of the girls' basketball club, we belonged to different worlds. So I had given up on my feelings from the very beginning. There was no point reaching out. I would never be able to touch her.

My first love should have ended quietly, with me simply thinking of her without ever exchanging a word. That resignation began to change around two months after I joined the club, during the rainy season.

Ren suddenly cut her hair short. And, as though in response, her natural cheerfulness faded. The dazzling radiance she had once scattered all around her was gone. It was as if she had become a different person. No longer the sun that illuminated everyone, she was like the moon, quietly lingering in the night sky.

Everyone was bewildered by this sudden change. The charming eyes that had once drawn people in were now tinged with melancholy, and her beauty had become so striking that nobody dared approach her casually.

Before long, the people around Ren began to disappear. Like a work of art so delicate it might break at a touch, everyone watched her beauty from a cautious distance.

I was one of them. She seemed far too out of reach for me to approach her of my own accord.

It was Ren who came to me.

I suspect it was because I was by far the quietest and most reserved person in the basketball club.

There was no particular reason for it, no special moment that brought us together. At some point, she simply began spending more time with me. During journeys between places, while waiting before matches, and at other such times. We wouldn't necessarily do anything, but she increasingly found herself beside me. We hardly spoke. In the silence, the only thing I could hear was my own heart, beating unbearably loudly.

The first love I had given up on was suddenly right beside me. Even when we could barely manage a proper conversation, simply being next to her made the world take on a sweet, beautiful hue.

Walking at the same pace as Ren, changing clothes beside her, practising together, the moment our hands met after I scored a basket. Every fleeting touch of warmth remains etched in my memory, vivid as ever. I wished these peaceful days would go on forever, that this soft, swelling heartbeat, so full it felt ready to burst, and my first love would never end.

I didn't wish for my love to be fulfilled. I only wished it wouldn't end. That was how hopelessly pessimistic my longing had become.

But happy times could not last forever. One day, as we were walking home, Ren spoke up, which was unusual for her.

“Shimamoto-san, there's something I'd like to talk to you about. Is that okay?”

Her voice was like a bell, trembling against my eardrums. Her eyes, glistening with the reflection of the evening sun, gazed at me as though pleading for something. All I could do was nod.

“Of course.”

After taking a breath, Ren began to speak, her expression pained, as though she were confessing to some terrible crime.

“This is about a friend of mine, but... My friend fell in love with someone she shouldn't have. Or rather, I think she's probably been in love with that person for a long time, but recently she realised that those feelings weren't normal. Anyway, she has to hide them, and being close to that person makes her feel as though they're going to spill out, so she decided to keep her distance. But it's painful, and every time she deliberately acts cold towards them, it feels like her heart is breaking. She's troubled by it, and... what do you think she should do, Shimamoto-san?”

I had never seen Ren speak so much. The anguish in her manner spoke volumes about the depth of her “friend's” feelings. Each word she uttered felt as though it were tearing my heart apart.

She waited intently for my answer. Clutching the love I could neither confess nor hope to have returned, I stood there in a daze, somehow managing to squeeze out a reply.

“I don't know how to solve that kind of problem. But I do understand a little about how painful unrequited love can be. So... tell your friend that. Tell her she's not alone.”

Ren's eyes widened, and she nodded.

“Okay. I'll tell her. And Shimamoto-san, if you're struggling with that kind of love yourself, you can talk to me, too. If there's anything I can do, I'll help.”\
“Thank you. Maybe someday.”

I managed a weak smile.

There was no way I could talk to her about it.

The remnants of my shattered first love could neither be pieced back together nor thrown away. They simply drifted endlessly in the palm of my hand.

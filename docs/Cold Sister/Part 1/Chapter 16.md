## Chapter 16 – Two Liars

\
I had arrived at my conclusion entirely thanks to Souji-sensei.

The latest book by Souji-sensei was part of a series about a younger brother who was in love with his older brother, and a friend of that younger brother who was in love with him. In this latest instalment, the friend confesses to the older brother, rather than the younger brother he loves, in an attempt to make him give up his forbidden love for his own brother.

“Unless I misheard her, she muttered Ren's name as she was leaving.”

The moment I heard Ai's explanation, that was the first thing that came to mind: the plot of Souji-sensei's latest novel. And then there was this:

“The girl who confessed to me seemed to be one of Ren's teammates. I remember seeing her among the group of basketball club members when I passed Ren near the water fountains yesterday.”

I remembered that girl, too. The tall one who had been glaring at me so intensely. At the time, I'd thought it was just my imagination, but apparently it hadn't been.

And now, the girl whose appearance matched that memory stood before me, looking perplexed.

“Who are you?”

Shimamoto-san asked, her otherwise neat-looking features contorted by confusion.

“I'm Takatsuki Tomoka. A friend of Kawai Ai, the girl you confessed to.”

At my introduction, Shimamoto-san's expression changed to one of utter astonishment.

“How do you know about that...?”

Her reaction was so delightfully telling that I began to enjoy myself. Then I remembered my anger, too, and, caught up in a strange mixture of emotions, launched into a lengthy explanation.

“I know. And not just that you confessed to Ai. I know quite a few other things, too. For example, that you don't actually like Ai, and that you're in love with her little sister. And that you're following her around right now to get in the way of those two spending time together.”

Shimamoto-san's eyes widened at my words.

“How do you know that much...? That's creepy.”

She muttered petulantly.

Her selfish reaction was so thoroughly human that I almost laughed. Right. This was what people were like. Spending so much time with Ai had made me forget.

For the first time in a while, I felt thoroughly disgusted. Anger joined that feeling, and finally took shape as words.

“Let me make one thing clear. I hate you several times more than you hate me right now. You're afraid of getting hurt, so you run away from your own feelings. And yet you cling to someone in this roundabout, half-hearted way, even going so far as to lie. Don't drag other people, especially Ai, into your own cowardice.”

It felt as though I were cutting into her with blades made of words.

And, in fact, Shimamoto-san looked as though she'd been struck down by them. Her lips trembled, and a faint voice escaped between them.

“But I can't help it. When I'm beside Ren, I can see it so painfully clearly. There's absolutely no chance my love will ever be returned. Everything about her, her words, her expressions, they're all directed towards her older sister. There's no way I could compete with that abnormal degree of attachment. That's why I thought I'd make Ren see sense...”

It was exactly the confession I'd expected.

With an exasperated sigh, I continued.

“So you made a false confession to Ai. You're all over the place. First of all, your little sister's feelings towards Ai might be rather excessive for a younger sister towards her older sister, but they're not something that should get in the way of your love. You've been friends with her for quite a while, haven't you? You know about those sisters' circumstances and what they've been through. Given all that, it's only natural that their feelings for each other would be so strong. But to say that it's romantic love? That's the sort of thing that only happens in novels, not in real life.”

I mean, even I sometimes twist the relationship between those sisters into BL when I'm talking about them. But that's just me indulging in the antics of a troublesome fujoshi. I don't actually believe that something straight out of a novel could happen in real life.

However, Shimamoto-san answered my words with what sounded almost like a sneer.

“You don't understand anything, do you?”\
“What do you mean?”\
“Exactly what I said. Besides, you're running away from your own feelings, too, aren't you? You love that older sister as well, but you're hiding your feelings and staying by her side, aren't you? You have the same expression as Ren.”

Shimamoto-san twisted her lips into a triumphant smile as she spoke.

I didn't understand what she meant. It was nothing but a baseless accusation. I immediately tried to object.

“That's not...!”

But just before I could put all my feelings into words, an idea suddenly flashed through my mind.

What could I do for Ai? What could I do to protect the peaceful life of those sisters?

Before I knew it, the words were spilling out of my mouth.

“Actually, that's right. I've been in love with Ai for a long time, too. But I've been afraid to tell her how I feel, so I've just kept running away. That's why I was so harsh towards you earlier. I suppose it was a case of seeing my own faults in you. I'm sorry.”\
“What are you suddenly talking about?”

Shimamoto-san was bewildered by how completely my attitude had changed. I seized the opportunity and continued.

“So, why don't we work together, since we're both in love with someone who doesn't return our feelings? For the sake of our respective romances. Think of it as an alliance.”

Faced with my sudden proposal, Shimamoto-san fell silent, apparently considering it. Then she answered in a firm voice.

“Fine. I don't like you, but I'll agree to this so-called alliance. However, in exchange for accepting your proposal, I want you to grant me one favour.”

Why is she acting so high and mighty? And she's speaking so casually to me despite being my junior, too. For someone who looks so serious, she certainly has no manners.

I complained inwardly, but made absolutely no outward sign of it.

People driven mad by love are unpredictable. You never know what they'll do. So I'll keep her under my control by giving her some convenient reason to cooperate. Those sisters, especially the younger one, are finally beginning to move closer to each other. I won't let someone like this interfere with that. If it's to protect Ai, this much is nothing.

Having made that decision in an instant, I realised something belatedly.

I'm just as much of a liar as Shimamoto-san.

After silently ridiculing myself, I answered.

“Fine. I'll do whatever you ask.”

At my words, Shimamoto-san let out a long, relieved breath before making her request.

“Then tell that older sister to come to the back of the gymnasium again after school.”

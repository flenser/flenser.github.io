## Chapter 24 - A Promise

\
The throbbing of blood, the pounding of my heart, the warmth washing over me. It was far too sweet to be called mere horseplay.

Caught somewhere between welcoming it and enduring it, I peered up from between Ren's arms and voiced a thought that had just occurred to me.

“Come to think of it, don't you have morning practice? Aren't you going to be late?”\
“I do.”

Her answer said one thing, but her embrace only tightened. My heart was beating wildly, as though Ren's very existence were pressing into it.

“But just a little longer.”

Ren whispered again, right by my ear. I could feel the heat gathering in my face.

“Honestly, you…”

I tried desperately to regain the initiative with those words. It was a good thing my face was buried against Ren's chest. If she'd seen how red I was right now, my dignity as her big sister would have crumbled beyond repair.

I was just beginning to feel reassured by my limited field of vision when Ren suddenly pulled away.

“I'm getting up.”

She announced it curtly, then got to her feet without even looking at me, as though nothing had happened.

My pounding heart gave way to a sense of longing. For a moment, I almost said, *More*, but hurriedly swallowed the words. Surely that would be going too far… wouldn't it?

As if trying to shake the question marks from my mind, I sprang up and called after Ren, who was heading out of the room.

“I'll get up too. I'm making coffee. Would you like some, Ren?”\
“...Yes.”

After a moment's hesitation, Ren nodded.

◇◇◇

I opened a packet and hooked a drip-coffee filter over a cup. Not one, but two. Seeing the cups sitting side by side made me smile.

A click announced that the water had boiled. I lifted the electric kettle beside me and poured the water between the two cups. The familiar aroma wafted into my nose with the rising steam.

I poured a little water at a time, letting the coffee bloom between pours, and soon it was ready. I removed the filters and carried the cups to the dining table.

Ren was already there, in her school uniform, eating toast. She was sitting in the same seat as yesterday, beside mine.

Beyond her, the television had been switched on without much thought, displaying the time and day of the week. It was Wednesday, the midpoint of the week, and the cheerful newsreader was smiling brightly as she urged everyone to keep going.

I spoke to Ren, who sat there with an expression as blank as could be, the complete opposite of the television presenter.

“Coffee's ready.”

I set the cup on the table and sat down.

“...Thank you.”

Ren stared for a while at the black liquid rippling in the cup before her, then brought it to her lips.

Immediately, her expression twisted.

“Sorry. Was it too hot?”\
“...Yes.”\
“You have to blow on it properly before drinking.”

I demonstrated, blowing gently across the surface of my coffee before taking a sip. Its familiar bitterness slipped down my throat.

Ren nodded silently. After sitting still for a moment, she copied my movements, blew on her coffee, and brought the cup to her lips.

“Urgh!”

But once again, her face twisted. This time, she even let out a groan.

Watching her, I arrived at a certain conclusion.

“Could it be that you can't drink your coffee black, Ren?”\
“...That's not true.”

As though to prove it, Ren brought the cup to her lips with her usual composed expression. But, just as predictably, her face twisted again.

I couldn't help smiling. Come to think of it, Ren had always liked sweet things and disliked bitter ones. I remembered her trying to imitate me and drinking coffee when we were in primary school, only to pull a face.

I'd assumed she'd grown out of it, since she'd grown up and now had such a mature appearance. But apparently her taste hadn't changed at all. Thinking back, even the bread we'd eaten together at lunchtime had mostly been sweet.

Ren was still cautiously bringing the cup to her lips, only to grimace each time. There was none of her usual statue-like beauty in her expression. Instead, all I could see was the adorable little girl she used to be.

My lips relaxed into a smile, and the words slipped out.

“Cute.”\
“Shut up.”

Beside me, Ren's cheeks seemed a little red. Her head was lowered, her neck drawn in. And, as if to rub salt into the wound while she struggled with the bitterness, the television was now showing a feature on sweets.

I gave Ren's head a gentle pat and stood up.

“Shall I add some syrup?”\
“I can drink it as it is, but… please.”

Ren held out her cup, her voice trailing off a little.

I accepted it, smiling again at her reaction, and headed into the kitchen. I took out the syrup I rarely used and considered whether adding milk would make it easier to drink. In the end, I added both, with a little extra syrup.

The once pitch-black liquid turned a soft flaxen colour.

I returned to the table and placed the cup in front of Ren. She cautiously took a sip.

“Delicious.”

The words escaped her before she could help herself. Then she lowered her eyes, looking somewhat embarrassed.

“I'm glad. Honestly, Ren, you really do have a sweet tooth.”

I teased her lightly. It felt as though I'd managed to reclaim a little of the big-sister dignity that had been under threat lately, and I felt slightly better.

In response to my teasing, Ren turned away and looked towards the television as though to escape. It was still showing the feature on sweets.

Not long afterwards, Ren seemed to think of something. She shifted her chair and sat facing me directly, then stared at me with a piercing gaze.

“W-what is it?”

Had I teased her a little too much? I asked cautiously, feeling rather intimidated by her stare.

But Ren didn't speak for quite some time. She simply stared at me, as still as though she'd turned into an ice sculpture.

The television's background noise went in one ear and out the other.

She didn't seem angry… So what was it?

I tilted my head in puzzlement, and Ren suddenly opened her mouth.

“I like sweet things. And there's a parfait at the shopping centre that I've been interested in. But it's a couples-only menu, and on Sunday I don't have club practice, so…”

Ren fell silent again. Even the words she'd spoken seemed to have frozen, stiff and angular, and I couldn't quite make sense of what she was getting at. I tilted my head once more.

Then, as though answering my unspoken question, she offered me just the conclusion.

“D-date.”\
“Eh?”

As if to seal off my question, Ren leaned towards me. Her eyes trembled as she looked up at me, despite the angle making it seem as though she were looking down.

“So, I want to eat that parfait, so go on a date with me. On Sunday.”

Her voice was cold, but there seemed to be a warmth behind her request. Perhaps that was the problem. That warmth always stirred the big-sister instinct engraved in my genes, making me nod.

“O-okay. Let's go on a d-date on Sunday.”

I nodded before I had even properly taken in what the word meant.

At that moment, I thought I saw Ren's eyes light up.

“It's a promise, then!”

As she said it, her long finger curled around my little finger. The familiar sensation, the familiar words. But before I could sink into the nostalgia, Ren's finger slipped away.

After that, Ren drained her cup, stood up, and hurriedly left the dining table.

I watched her retreating back in a daze.

A date.

I rolled the word around on my tongue. I was surprised by how sweet it sounded.

In front of Ren's seat, a little liquid remained at the bottom of her cup, gleaming in the morning light.

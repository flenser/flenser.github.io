## Chapter 15 – A World of Love (Ai's World) - POV Tomoka

\
“That girl who walked off with the junior from the basketball club... That's Kawai-san, isn't it? I wonder how she knows her.”\
“It's not just that they know each other. Apparently, those two are sisters.”\
“What!? They don't look alike at all. Kawai-san is so plain, and she hardly stands out. And her little sister is taller than her, too.”

Listening to the classroom chatter, with everyone saying whatever they pleased, I couldn't help thinking, Shut up already. How could they fail to see what was so wonderful about Ai? They had absolutely no eye for people.

Actually, I remembered that it was precisely this sort of thing that had made me reluctant to associate with other people in the first place. That natural sense of superiority, the assumption that you were entitled to judge others, and the tactlessness of barging into someone else's private sense of self without a second thought. I couldn't stand any of it.

And yet, I knew I possessed some of those very same qualities myself. There was something about the self-loathing that came from recognising my own reflection in people I despised, mixed with an almost obsessive desire for moral purity. Everything surrounding human relationships was difficult for me.

In the past, I'd wanted to escape those entanglements. I cut off my relationships with other people and immersed myself in BL as a kind of disguise. Anything would have done, as long as I could lose myself in it and put a barrier between myself and the rest of the world.

What's more, for me back then, as for many others, the world of male-male romance, portrayed in such an aesthetically beautiful way and so unfamiliar to most people, was the perfect defence against reality.

Of course, BL is now so central to my life that I could practically call it the centre of my universe. But in the beginning, my reasons were far more calculating and thoroughly mundane.

And it was around that time that I met Ai.

Ai was the polar opposite of all the troublesome things I found so unbearable in other people. What I always sensed from her was kindness and compassion, qualities I could hardly believe someone our age could possess. That was why I was so strongly drawn to her.

At first, I wasn't entirely convinced. I thought she might simply be timid, and that if you peeled away a thin outer layer, you'd find the same things in her as in everyone else.

But Ai was different.

She was kind to others and had no attachment whatsoever to protecting her own ego. For instance, even when her little sister rebelled against her for reasons that seemed downright unreasonable, Ai didn't get angry. Instead, she worried that something unpleasant might have happened to her, and repeatedly came to me for advice.

I didn't know anyone to whom the saying “A person's name reflects their nature” was more applicable. Ai was quite literally overflowing with unconditional love.

Once, I'd asked her:

“Why do you care so much about your little sister?”

That was what I'd asked.

And she'd replied:

“Because I'm her big sister. I'm the one who has to protect Ren.”

I realised then that there was no competing with that.

Ai's world was small. And that small world was filled to the brim with kindness and compassion.

It was enough to make me understand why her little sister was such a siscon. The radiance was so dazzling that I could even understand why someone might find it difficult to be honest about their feelings and want to look away. And I could almost envy the unconditional love that continued to pour forth, undeterred by that little pretence of rejection.

Having glimpsed a fragment of Ai's world, I thought that I wanted to be part of it, too.

Because I felt that if I stayed by Ai's side, I might gradually become capable of loving other people, and even myself.

Because I felt that if I stayed by her side and listened to her talk about her little sister, I might gradually become able to believe in the goodness of human nature.

Ai was my salvation.

Even with my distrust of other people having become so deeply ingrained, Ai was someone I could trust unconditionally.

Sometimes, though, I wondered whether my trust in her was simply blind devotion. Whether I was just trapping her inside the character of a “good girl”.

Those doubts made me anxious from time to time. But Ai was always so kind, so honest, and so considerate that my fears never amounted to anything.

A world made of jewels, so gentle and beautiful that it seemed it might shatter at any moment.

A precious friend, the kind of person I would probably never encounter again in my life.

That was what Ai meant to me.

And that's why I wanted to protect her.

Recently, though, something had been threatening that precious friend of mine. If my suspicions were correct, those sinister hands might even now be reaching out towards Ai.

Driven by that premonition, I stood up and left the classroom.

I looked down the corridor. In the distance, I saw two uneven figures walking side by side: Ai and her little sister.

And, just as if to confirm my fears, there was another gaze fixed upon that heartwarming sight, watching them with unmistakable resentment.

Judging by the girl's height, her familiar silhouette, and an expression that was far too easy to read, I felt almost certain of who she was.

I approached her and spoke.

“Are you Shimamoto Rira-san?”

The girl turned towards me in surprise.

Her reaction, together with the expression she'd been wearing moments earlier, was all the confirmation I needed.

## Chapter 39: Marriage

\
The sensation of a warmth that wasn't my own brought me vaguely back to consciousness.

In my hazy, half-awake state, I realised that the source of that warmth was Ren. She usually woke up before me without fail, but perhaps yesterday's match had left her exhausted. Today, she was sleeping soundly, clinging to my body.

Ren's sleeping face looked so young, in stark contrast to her cool demeanour during the day. Her peaceful expression hadn't changed at all since she was a child.

"Big Sis..."

Ren's lips moved faintly.

Apparently, I was making an appearance in her dreams. The thought made me feel strangely ticklish inside. Without thinking, I stroked her head, just as I used to.

In the hazy light of morning, my sleepy mind sometimes carried me back into the past. Bathed in the pale blue light of dawn, Ren's familiar warmth drew me into memories of days gone by.

◇◇◇

"Big Sis!"

My younger sister, Ren, was always following close behind me. She was tiny, a cry-baby, innocent and shy around strangers. Without me, she couldn't do anything.

I thought she was unbearably adorable. Whenever Ren came to me for affection, I'd always wrap her in a tight hug and stroke her head. Whenever some bully made her cry, I'd rush over and teach that boy a lesson.

"That was scary, wasn't it? It's all right now."\
"...Mm."\
"Whenever you're in trouble, Big Sis will always protect you."\
"Thank you, Big Sis. I love you!"

With her face still a mess of tears, Ren would smile.

My little sister. Tiny, weak, adorable, and such a cry-baby. I would protect her, absolutely, no matter what.

We were always together when we were little. Reading picture books, playing house, going to the park. Everything was something we did together.

"When I grow up, I'm going to marry Big Sis!"

At some point, Ren began saying those words frequently.

And she didn't just say them directly to me. She'd announce it at the dinner table, too.

"Mum, Dad! Guess what? When Ren grows up, she's going to marry Big Sis!"

She would say it excitedly, almost bursting with enthusiasm.

"I see. I can't wait for the wedding!"

Dad would laugh and play along. He was a cheerful, easy-going man, tall and athletic, and he often gave us piggyback rides on his shoulders. I loved the view from so high above the ground, so much higher than I could ever reach myself. He was kind and funny, a father I was proud of. Mum would smile beside him.

I loved scenes and moments like those. I loved Mum and Dad. And, of course, I loved our whole family, Ren included.

I was older than Ren, so I knew that sisters couldn't marry each other. But I sometimes thought that, if it were possible, perhaps that wouldn't be so bad. After all, if Ren and I got married, the four of us could live together forever as a family. Surely that would be wonderful. For our present happiness to continue for ever... What more could anyone ask for?

Those were the innocent thoughts that occupied my little mind.

### **Translator's notes**

- **ケッコン (kekkon)** means marriage. The title deliberately recalls Ren's childhood declaration that she would marry Ai when she grew up.\
- **肩車 (kata-guruma)** is when a child sits astride an adult's shoulders. I've rendered this as “piggyback rides on his shoulders” to distinguish it from carrying someone on one's back.\
- **おままごと (omamagoto)** is children's pretend play, particularly playing house or imitating family life.\
- The expression 「家族四人でずっと暮らせる」 is important. Ai's childhood interpretation of marrying Ren isn't really about romance. She imagines it as a way of preserving the happiness of their family of four indefinitely.

### **Thoughts**

Oh, this is a very significant flashback, particularly given everything we've seen over the past few chapters.

The author has taken us back to the beginning of Ai and Ren's relationship, and there's something quite poignant about the fact that the childhood memories are triggered by the physical sensation of Ren sleeping against her.

The opening is especially tender. Ren is normally the composed one who wakes first, but after the tournament she's exhausted enough to sleep while clinging to Ai. Ai sees the sleeping face of the little girl she remembers, and instinctively strokes her head.

Then we discover that **Ren's desire to marry Ai goes all the way back to childhood.**

What's particularly interesting is Ai's reaction to it back then. She knows that sisters cannot marry, but she nevertheless entertains the possibility. Not because she understands romantic love, but because she imagines that marriage would allow their family to remain together forever.

That last detail changes the emotional significance of the childhood declaration.

For little Ren, marrying Big Sis is an expression of love and attachment. For little Ai, accepting the idea is a way of preserving the perfect family life she cherishes.

And there's a rather sad irony in that.

The family she wanted to keep together eventually fell apart. Her parents divorced, and Ren subsequently became distant. The two things Ai had imagined marriage would protect, her family and her closeness with Ren, were both lost.

Now, years later, Ren has returned to her side, but Ai is still frightened of losing her again.

I also think this flashback adds another dimension to Ren's current behaviour. Her feelings didn't suddenly appear when she became a teenager. Whatever those childhood declarations meant to her at the time, her attachment to Ai has a very long history.

One detail I particularly like is Ai's recollection of protecting Ren from bullies. She remembers herself as the strong, capable older sister who could always rush to Ren's rescue. That contrasts beautifully with the present, where Ren is now the accomplished athlete, and Ai is the one who finds herself overwhelmed by her younger sister.

And there's something rather bittersweet about the father's presence in these memories. He is cheerful, affectionate, and part of the happy family scene. We know from Chapter 21 that the parents' marriage eventually ended, but Ai's childhood recollection remains warm. She hasn't rewritten those memories simply because the family later changed.

**The most important distinction, I think, is that Ai's childhood wish was to keep her family together, whereas Ren's present-day wish is to be chosen by Ai.**

Those aren't necessarily the same thing, even if they grew from the same childhood closeness.

And with the chapter titled *Marriage*, I suspect we're being invited to reconsider the significance of Ren's old declaration in light of everything that's happened since.

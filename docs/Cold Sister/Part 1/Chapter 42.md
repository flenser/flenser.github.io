## Chapter 42 – Tangled Arrows - POV Tomoka

\
“Shimamoto-san, I messaged you earlier. Why did you come?”\
“I thought you'd be lonely because you couldn't have lunch with me, so I took pity on you and came anyway. But more importantly, her sister aside, why is Ren here too?”\
“...Well, thank you for your concern. Ren came to have lunch with Ai, but they can't use the rooftop in this rain, so we're eating together. Since you've come all this way, Shimamoto-san, would you like to join us?”

Shimamoto-san fell silent at my invitation.

Well, fair enough. Things must be awkward between her and Ai after that confession business, even if it was entirely her own doing. And seeing those sisters interacting at such close quarters might well be enough to make her collapse.

Nevertheless, after some hesitation, Shimamoto-san nodded.

“Then I'll take you up on that.”

Her agreement was somewhat reluctant, but she nodded all the same. I couldn't help admiring her sheer tenacity. Lately, I'd begun to find something rather appealing about the strength of her feelings, the straightforward intensity of her attachment to Ren. And that had nothing to do with the fact that her older sister was Souji-sensei.

“Right, let's go.”

I said that and returned to my seat, with Shimamoto-san following behind me. The classroom grew a little noisy again. Apparently, Shimamoto-san was something of a celebrity too, though not quite to the same extent as Ren. Well, judging by her looks alone, she was exceptionally attractive, her figure was every bit as good as Ren's, and she was a talented basketball player. It made sense, really. I was a little frustrated by the thought, given that I knew what she was like underneath that polished exterior.

With those thoughts in mind, we reached our seats.

“So, is it all right if Shimamoto-san eats with us too?”\
“Of course!”

Ai nodded without the slightest hesitation. Despite everything that had happened with the confession, the fact that she could respond so readily was quintessentially Ai.

“Thank you! And, once again, I'm sorry about what happened the other day!”\
“Y-you don't need to apologise. Please, raise your head. And go ahead and sit down.”

At Ai's words, Shimamoto-san cautiously raised her head and took the seat she was offered. Her politeness and sense of responsibility reminded me that, all things considered, she really wasn't a bad person. I'll leave aside the fact that she used polite speech with Ai but not with me.

As I was thinking that, Ren, who had been watching the exchange between Shimamoto-san and Ai intently, muttered under her breath:

“Rira knows my Big Sis?”

There was a note of wariness in her voice.

That earlier remark about “my Big Sis” had been another example. Ren seemed to be extremely possessive of Ai. Whether the true nature of that feeling was familial love or something else, I couldn't say. But one thing was certain.

Confronted with the sheer weight of those feelings, which were obvious to everyone except the oblivious Ai, Shimamoto-san looked as though she'd taken a punch to the gut. She then hurriedly composed herself and answered.

“We've had a few things happen between us. But don't worry, I'm not going to steal your sister.”

She smiled, apparently trying to put Ren at ease. Shimamoto-san was being more self-effacing than I'd ever seen her. The power of love really was something.

“It's not as though I'm worried about that.”

Ren said this, but the relief was unmistakable in her voice. She was so transparent. I could easily imagine how her every little gesture must be wearing away at Shimamoto-san's mental state on a daily basis. My sympathies.

Meanwhile, the person at the centre of all this, Ai, was watching Ren and Shimamoto-san chatting with a cheerful smile. Her obliviousness was, as ever, impressive.

And so, our impromptu lunch for four proceeded amicably enough on the surface. Beneath it, however, all sorts of feelings were tangled up in one another. Ai seemed completely oblivious to the whole thing, cheerfully offering her innocent observations.

“By the way, Tomoka-chan and I went to watch your basketball match the other day. You were both really amazing! So cool!”\
“Thank you very much!”\
“I worked hard for Big Sis.”

Unlike Shimamoto-san, who responded brightly with a polite bow, Ren casually dropped another emotionally loaded remark. She shifted her chair closer to Ai, pressing her body against her.

Shimamoto-san had gone completely rigid at the sight of all this. It must have been a complicated experience, seeing the usually cool girl she loved clinging so shamelessly to her big sister from such close quarters. I felt genuinely sorry for her, so I decided to throw her a lifeline.

“By the way, Shimamoto-san, don't you treat Ai and me differently?”\
“Huh? How?”

Unaware of my consideration and sympathy, Shimamoto-san answered brusquely.

“Right now, for instance! You use polite speech with Ai, like a proper junior, but you're completely casual with me! And you call me ‘you’ and all sorts of things! What's with that difference?”\
“Do I?”\
“You hadn't even noticed? So you really haven't been showing me any respect at all, have you...”\
“Sorry, sorry. But I don't feel like starting to use polite speech with you now.”\
“Why not? I'm a perfectly wonderful senior!”\
“In what way? You're an insufferable otaku, your enthusiasm is weird, and you're foul-mouthed. There's absolutely nothing about you worthy of respect.”\
“Everything except the otaku bit applies to you too, you know!”

As the conversation, which had begun as an attempt to throw her a lifeline, escalated into a full-blown argument, Ren quietly murmured:

“Good friends.”\
““We're not!””

Our denials came out in perfect unison, quite unintentionally.

“You're perfectly in sync.”

Ai smiled at us, as though returning the favour from earlier.

“Has Ai developed a bit of a mean streak? In a bad way, I mean.”

I let the playful complaint slip out between wry smiles. I wanted the old Ai back, the pure-hearted girl who would never tease anyone.

Joking aside, though, Ai seemed more lively than usual today. Perhaps it was because Ren was sitting beside her. That thought crossed my mind, and I felt a little cloud pass over my mood. Not as dark as the rainclouds outside the window, but there all the same.

As I sat there feeling unsettled, Shimamoto-san unexpectedly offered another sort of lifeline by changing the subject herself.

“By the way, Ren. The captain told me when we passed each other in the corridor earlier. Apparently, we're both going to be attending the A-team training camp before the summer tournament. They'll explain the details at a meeting later, but that's what I heard.”\
“...I see.”\
“Is being on the A-team a big deal?”

I seized the opportunity to join the conversation.

“Well, Ren and I are the only first-years who've been selected.”

Shimamoto-san puffed out her chest proudly.

“That's amazing! Congratulations, Ren!”

Ai offered her praise with complete sincerity. Ren, however, looked a little subdued as she asked Ai:

“But if I go to the training camp, we'll be apart. Will you be all right without me, Big Sis?”

Oh... She sounded as though they were about to be separated for the rest of their lives. The sheer emotional intensity of it made me groan inwardly. I was beginning to feel that Ren could turn absolutely any topic into an opportunity to cling to her big sister.

I quietly asked Shimamoto-san, who looked as though she was about to collapse on the spot.

“Is the training camp really that long?”\
“No, just two days and one night.”\
“Seriously?”

All this emotional intensity over being apart for just one day. It really brought home just how deeply Ren depended on Ai.

And Ai, for her part, answered Ren's question with her cheeks faintly flushed.

“I might be a little lonely. But I'll be cheering you on, so do your best...!”

After that exchange, the two of them gazed at each other and nodded softly.

What is this? A couple who've only just started dating?

I hurriedly shook the question marks out of my head. They're just close sisters. They have to be.

Left on the sidelines, Shimamoto-san and I chewed away at our lunches, feeling as though we'd had far too much sugar just from watching the scene in front of us.

As I savoured the somewhat stodgy texture of my bread, I stole a glance at Ai's expression. At the sparkle in her eyes as she looked at Ren, and the faint blush on her cheeks.

What was Ai thinking right now? What was hidden in the arrow of feeling she directed towards her little sister?

Until recently, I'd understood it all too well. She wanted to protect Ren as her big sister. She wanted to do things for Ren. Those were the only feelings I'd ever sensed from Ai.

But now, I couldn't tell.

When the two of them went out together, and now as I watched them here, the Kawai sisters seemed so effortlessly capable of creating a world that belonged to just the two of them. Was what lay between them really nothing more than familial affection? Or could something that should never have been possible actually come to pass, just as Shimamoto-san feared?

And, more than anything else, what did I want to happen? That was what I understood least of all. I couldn't understand myself, or anyone else. Where was my own arrow pointing, and in what direction was it heading?

Beside me, trapped in that dead end of tangled thoughts, Shimamoto-san was gazing intently at Ren.

The unmistakable clarity of her feelings brought me a little comfort.

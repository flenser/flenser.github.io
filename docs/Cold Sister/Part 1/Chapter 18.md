## Chapter 18 - Even If My Love Can Never Reach Her

\
From then on, Ren began coming to me for advice regularly, always prefacing her questions with, “This is about a friend of mine…”

“She feels lonely because she can't talk to the person she loves. But she's afraid that if she talks to them even a little, her feelings will spill out.”\
“She wants to at least express how grateful she is for everything, but she's completely forgotten how she's supposed to act around them. She doesn't know what to do.”\
“Every time she ignores them, it hurts. Her heart aches. But even though she's been treating them so coldly, they still make an effort to talk to her instead of giving up on her. And a little part of her is happy about that. She hates herself for feeling that way.”

The words pouring from Ren assailed me without mercy. Her feelings for someone I knew nothing about were shattering my own love into pieces.

Ironically, the more often she came to me for advice, the closer we grew. Before I knew it, we'd started calling each other by our given names. The more my feelings for Ren were broken apart, the deeper my relationship with her became. There was no word for it but irony.

And as time went on, Ren's demeanour softened. She wasn't quite as dazzlingly cheerful as she had once been, but she had regained her gentle composure, and people began gathering around her again. And so, the days passed.

Then, one day, as Ren and I were walking down the corridor, two girls approached us from the opposite direction. From the colour of their indoor shoes, I could tell they were a year above us. The one who was a little shorter than us was talking excitedly about something. The other girl listening to her was even shorter, and her uniform sleeves were just a little too long for her. There was something so adorable and gentle about her that, even from a distance, it was hard to believe she was a senior.

As I gazed vaguely at the two approaching girls, Ren suddenly froze for an instant. Immediately afterwards, the shorter senior turned towards us for some reason and smiled. Then—

“Ren!”

She called out, waving her hand. Her uniform sleeves fluttered.

Ren didn't answer. She stopped for a moment, then quickened her pace and walked on without even looking towards the senior. The shorter girl kept her smile fixed in place, but her shoulders drooped sadly. The other senior gently patted her shoulder, consoling her. That was the last I saw of them as we passed by.

“Your little sister hasn't changed, has she?”

I faintly heard someone say from behind us.

After we'd walked a little further, Ren suddenly stopped and stared at the two seniors' retreating backs. Her expression was sorrowful, filled with such intense longing that it seemed her heart might burn itself out.

I understood immediately, from her eyes and expression. Because the feeling I saw there was exactly the same as the one I directed towards Ren. I asked, hesitantly,

“Do you know them?”

Ren murmured softly, as though embracing a secret.

“...She's my big sister.”

The moment I heard those words, I looked up at the sky.

That love was futile. I had an older sister myself, but I couldn't imagine falling in love with her.

And yet, of all people, I knew better than anyone that Ren's feelings weren't just a passing infatuation. Having listened to her talk about them time and time again, I'd been made painfully aware of that.

That was why she needed someone to talk to, I realised. She must have understood better than anyone that the path her love followed was strewn with thorns.

And I understood better than anyone that, no matter how thorny that path might be, she couldn't stop loving her.

Even so, I couldn't help thinking about it. If it were me, I wouldn't make her suffer like that. All Ren had to do was return my feelings, and she would be saved.

Ren never stopped coming to me for advice. Just as I couldn't abandon my love, no matter how brutally it was shattered, she couldn't abandon her love for her big sister. And so we went round and round in circles, endlessly revisiting the same ground.

Ren turned down every offer of a sports recommendation and went on to a local public high school. It happened to be the same school that had been my first choice. But of course, she wasn't going there because of me. She wanted to attend the same school as her big sister.

And yet, the fact that I was happy simply because we'd be attending the same high school together showed just how hopeless I was.

So even after we entered high school, the same futile cycle continued. I was thoroughly fed up with it. Exhausted, accustomed to the pain, and yet the feelings for Ren that I still clutched in my palm remained as hot and painful as ever. I couldn't make them go anywhere.

One day, desperate for some kind of salvation, I confided the whole story to my older sister.

My sister, who was somewhat lacking in certain aspects of basic human decency. Or rather, quite considerably lacking. In response to my troubles, she made an outrageous suggestion.

“Then why don't you go out with the older sister of the person you like? Maybe that'll make the person you love come to their senses.”

It was a solution that seemed to have been devised by someone who had left their heart somewhere behind.

And yet, to me, it looked like a thread of hope dangling from the heavens.

By the time I'd acted on that ridiculous suggestion and slipped a letter into her shoe locker, I already knew that I was the biggest fool of all. *Ah, I'm making a terrible mistake right now,* I thought. I knew it when I came face to face with Ren's big sister and caught a glimpse of the goodness in her, enough to understand why Ren was so utterly captivated by her.

I knew it, too, as I secretly followed Ren on her way to meet her big sister. I knew that doing something like this wouldn't change a thing.

Even without Takatsuki Tomoka telling me so, I knew all that.

But precisely because I understood, Tomoka's criticism had struck a nerve, and I'd felt an anger so intense it was almost like lashing out at her for something that wasn't her fault. That was why my accusation that she had feelings for the big sister had been half a desperate guess, born of sheer frustration. Yet that reckless guess had sent things in an unexpected direction.

Tomoka had suddenly shifted from accusing me to confessing her own feelings and proposing that we form an alliance of unrequited lovers. Swept along by her momentum, I found myself overwhelmed by a jumble of thoughts and emotions: a love I couldn't give up on, the guilt I felt towards both Ren and her big sister, and the desire to have someone with whom I could share my unfulfilled feelings without hiding anything. I was so confused I could barely make sense of myself.

By the time I realised what was happening, I'd already nodded.

And even amid all that chaos, I found myself trying to establish at least one clear condition with Tomoka.

“Then please tell her. Ask her to come to the back of the gymnasium again after school.”

Perhaps even that was simply something I wanted because it would make me feel better.

In any case, I am now waiting for her, on top of all those tangled threads that have piled up between us. Behind the gymnasium, just as I was on the day I made that false confession. This time, so that I can make a genuine confession.

A gust of wind brushed against my back and passed through towards the covered walkway. As though drawn along by that breeze, her big sister appeared. When she spotted me, she hurried over at a light run.

“Sorry I'm late.”\
“No, I'm sorry for calling you out here so many times.”\
“So, what was it you wanted to talk about...?”

She asked, looking uneasy. I took a deep breath and let the words out.

“I'm sorry. My confession the other day was a lie. There's actually someone else I love.”

Saying it aloud made me realise all over again just how awful I'd been. It was something I could never expect to be forgiven for.

And yet, her big sister smiled warmly.

“I'm glad. I've felt terrible ever since, thinking that I'd hurt you, Shimamoto-san. It's been weighing on my mind.”

She spoke with such genuine relief.

And I understood, more painfully than ever, why Ren was so fixated on her. Why nobody else would do. Things like their family circumstances were, in the end, only one factor among many. For Ren, it had to be this person.

That truth was thrust before me, and I despaired. And yet, my own feelings, which I still couldn't bring myself to abandon, were just as real.

Even if my love could never reach her.

I steeled myself and told her.

“I... I'm actually in love with Ren. That's all I wanted to tell you today, along with my apology.”

At my words, her big sister's eyes widened.

That little gesture was frighteningly similar to Ren's.

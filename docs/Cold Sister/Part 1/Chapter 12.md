## Chapter 12 – A Singing Voice?

\
I planned that evening's dinner with the help of the sports nutrition book I'd bought the other day.

Mum had prepared the side dishes and soup the day before, making sure they'd keep well, so all I really needed to do was make the main course.

After giving it some careful thought, I decided on a pork and tomato stew, something I'd made often enough to be comfortable with. It was rich in vitamins and helped with recovery from fatigue, making it perfect for Ren, who was still sleep-deprived after club activities. And for me, too, since I was in the same state.

I put on an apron over my casual clothes and took out the pork I'd bought on the way home. Then I gathered the seasonings and set to work, my hands moving with practised ease.

As I went through the motions, I found myself thinking about what Tomoka-chan had said earlier. She seemed to have some idea what was behind Shimamoto-san's words and actions, but what exactly had she noticed? Actually, it was impressive that she'd been able to form a hypothesis from my utterly disjointed explanation. I suppose that's what you'd expect from someone who's good at her studies and such an avid reader.

I read quite a lot myself, but Japanese literature was about the only subject I was particularly good at. Unlike her, I wasn't someone who could handle just about anything with ease.

Would Tomoka-chan, who was used to receiving confessions, have known how to respond properly?

I remembered Shimamoto-san's desperate voice and pleading eyes. I was sure I'd done something wrong. But when it came to romance and things of that sort, I was completely hopeless, so I couldn't even work out what it was.

I sighed at my own helplessness. At this rate, becoming an adult capable of protecting Ren and Mum was nothing more than a distant dream.

A moment of self-doubt threatened to overwhelm me, but I remembered how Tomoka-chan had encouraged me and managed to pull myself together.

For now, I just had to do what I could.

I was wrestling with those thoughts, as well as the frying pan, when I heard the sliding door rattle open. Footsteps approached, moving at a slightly quicker pace than usual, and Ren appeared in the kitchen.

“Welcome home.”

I turned towards her and smiled.

Usually, she would only nod or turn away without saying anything in response. But, just as she'd done that morning, Ren moved her lips.

“I'm home.”

She said it in a quiet voice, then fell silent. She stood there, rooted to the spot, looking very much as though she wanted to say something.

“What's wrong?”

I asked, keeping an eye on the heat and checking that the food was cooking properly.

Ren hesitated for a moment before quietly asking:

“Big Sis, did someone confess to you?”\
“Yeah. I was surprised someone like me would get a confession, though.”

Ren's eyes widened. Then she opened her mouth again.

“What did you say?”

The pork sizzled in the frying pan.

Only now did I realise how embarrassing it was to talk about. My face grew hot, caught somewhere between embarrassment and awkwardness. I carefully chose my words before answering.

“Well, I felt it was far too much for someone like me, but... I turned her down. I still don't really understand what it means to go out with someone.”\
“I see.”

Ren muttered as though she had no particular interest, then quickly left the kitchen.

I watched her go. A little while later, I remembered what she'd said yesterday.

“It's not okay for you to go out with anyone.”

What on earth had she meant by that?

Yesterday, those words had seemed to carry a real sense of urgency. But given how little reaction she'd shown just now, perhaps I'd simply misunderstood. For that matter, maybe I'd misheard her in the first place, what with how flustered I'd been at the time.

While I was turning the matter over in my mind, the cooking was finished.

I turned off the heat, divided the food into three portions on separate plates, covered them with cling film and put them in the fridge.

On days like today, when Mum was away at work, we each ate at whatever time suited us. Mum and I sometimes ate together if our schedules happened to coincide, but Ren usually ate in her own room, so we rarely had meals together.

I felt a little lonely thinking about this routine that had become so ordinary at some point.

Since it was still too early to eat, I got moving again to take care of some other household chores.

I went upstairs, brought in the laundry, folded it and put it away in the wardrobe. Then I headed to the washroom, which also served as the changing room, to put away the towels.

The door was closed. It seemed Ren was taking a shower, and I could faintly hear the sound of running water.

I opened the door and went inside, intending to put the towels away quickly.

And then I heard something I could scarcely believe.

*

　As you grow up,\
　may that season\
　not be filled with sad songs

*

What I could hear from the bathroom was the sound of the shower, and a singing voice.

It was a cheerful voice that I could hardly believe was coming from Ren. In fact, she was singing so loudly that it was practically a full-blown performance. She was singing a song that Dad used to play in the car when we were little.

Had something good happened to her?

I simply couldn't reconcile the Ren I knew, who was always so cool and composed, with the exuberant singing voice now echoing through the bathroom.

Feeling as though I'd accidentally witnessed something I wasn't supposed to see, I hurriedly put the towels away and left the room.

## Chapter Four: The Snow Melts into the Clod of Earth - Part Seven

\
I'll confess that I'd been devoting almost all my thoughts to such things, but even I couldn't manage it every day. It was impossible to dispel the suspicion that something was amiss if I kept driving her to school in the morning and visiting her at night, day after day. Under the ordinary routine of life, with no year-end business to use as an excuse, finding time without everything falling apart was proving rather difficult.\
In the end, I hadn't managed to find an opportunity to see Kai for about four days, and Saturday had arrived.\
Even though we exchanged messages over the phone, if I didn't experience that atmosphere in the hotel with Kai, I was overcome by restlessness. It was the sort of thing that might well have a medical name.\
My husband went off to work at the onigiri shop on Saturday, as he often did. It had always been like that: our days off rarely coincided. My daughter had gone to the convenience store to buy a prepaid card for in-game purchases. I was keeping a watchful eye on her, thinking that as long as she didn't get too carried away, it was probably fine. Still, spending money on games was a culture entirely foreign to me, and it made me a little uneasy.\
And, needless to say, our beloved dog Maron had gone along with my husband. That was simply what Maron did.\
I thought how nice it must be to be Maron. She could stick to my husband as much as she liked, and nobody would ever accuse her of cheating.\
What was so nice about that, exactly?\
And so, left alone at home, I finished the housework, then took the car out of the garage and spent a pleasant time under the winter sky splashing water over it.\
In reality, I could feel my body temperature dropping steadily. It was astonishingly cold.\
Lately, I'd been driving along the coastal roads more often, so I'd taken to washing the car every weekend.\
As though I were washing away the evidence.\
I wonder what next week will be like. Will I be able to see Kai?\
“‘Be able to see her’...”\
As I sprayed water over the window, I found myself objecting to the phrasing that had come out so naturally.\
Why was I thinking about Kai so much?\
Perhaps because of the cold water rebounding off the car and splashing over me, I suddenly came to my senses. These moments came along from time to time. My sanity, sunk at the bottom of a pitch-black sea, would abruptly draw breath again, thrashing at the surface in a desperate struggle.\
What was Kai to me?\
Something I kept being drawn towards, even as I continually looked away.\
A strange planet, yellow-green, visible in the distance. Something that wavered. The sea of another world, boasting infinite depths.\
When I gazed into it, I felt as though I, too, began to glow.\
...Wasn't there something else I could think of? Something like how inscrutable she was, or how cheeky.\
Though her cheekiness was sometimes a rather charming seasoning, bringing out the flavour of a fourteen-year-old...\
Bringing out the flavour of what, exactly?\
More importantly, the real question was why I was still seeing Kai, why I kept trying to see her.\
I went round and round this problem and its contradictions in my head, endlessly evading any conclusion.\
Because... Kai had feelings for me.\
And I was touching the bottom of a middle-school girl, and kissing her.\
“................................”\
Had I, perhaps, already done something from which there was no going back?\
Why ‘perhaps’?\
The belated realisation burned so fiercely that not even being doused in cold water seemed likely to extinguish it.\
Well, setting aside all the various things, all the miscellaneous details and whatnot...\
First of all, wasn't doing that sort of thing with a fourteen-year-old girl a crime in itself?\
Kai was affectionate towards me, and I'd let myself get swept along by the atmosphere until one thing after another had happened almost by default... But if Kai decided to take this to the authorities and press charges, that alone would destroy me. All at once, I was frightened.\
I'd been entirely lacking in any sense of just how close to the edge of a cliff my life had been walking.\
My resistance to Kai had become numb. The common sense I'd spent a lifetime cultivating had no answer for the connection I had with her. It couldn't develop any immunity to this unfamiliar kind of relationship, and the invasion was advancing day by day.\
I couldn't go on like this. Even if it was already too late.\
What should I do? What, throw everything away and pretend none of it had ever happened?\
I couldn't do that now. As I'd just said, if Kai decided to act, I would be finished. Besides, I'd already experienced what it was like to be apart from Kai, across the turn of the year. In the end, I couldn't stop thinking about her. I couldn't stop worrying about her. I couldn't get her out of my head. I could admit that much. There was absolutely no way I could convince myself she didn't matter to me.\
Kai kept finding things I'd put away in the storeroom, things I thought I'd never use again, and bringing them out one after another, spreading them around as if to show them off. Then, as though she thought the things she'd laid out were firewood, she would set them alight.\
The smell of them burning made me impatient, for better or worse, and drove me onwards.\
A girl who was in love with me. A twenty-four-year age gap. I felt as though my head might come apart. After all, being twenty-four years younger meant that when I got married, Kai hadn't even been born yet.\
Back then, there was no Chitaira Kai anywhere in the world.\
People don't know the moment they were born. They can't truly grasp that the world existed before they came into it.\
But I could vouch for Kai's existence.\
I had been born into a world without Kai, and then come into a world where Kai existed.\
...Even I was beginning to lose track of what I was trying to say.\
I wondered what it was like for her. For Kai.\
What did it feel like to be in love with your friend's mother?\
I suspected she'd give me a very detailed answer if I asked. That would be a problem in itself.\
“...This isn't good. This really isn't good.”\
The succession of kisses since our reunion might be a sign that the nature of our relationship was changing. For starters, letting her suck my toes had definitely been a bad idea. No, letting her suck my fingers was bad, too. So did that mean everything was off-limits?\
The increase in physical contact was... undoubtedly dangerous.\
I knew this feeling. It was the same as when I'd been dating my husband and the distance between us had gradually grown smaller. The feelings one had for someone one loved were a mixture of all sorts of things. At first, one could conceal the gritty texture of desire, like gravel, but when two people drew close enough to touch, there was no avoiding it.\
At some point, I'd come to understand that the idea of pure love was an illusion.\
In the end, the self inevitably became mixed up in one's emotions. That was why they could never be pure.\
Kai, too, was becoming increasingly overt in what she wanted from me. The other day, she'd even... And next time...\
Remembering what she'd pointed out, I discreetly reached back to check for myself. Then I felt ridiculous and quickly withdrew my hand.\
“It's not particularly big...”\
At least, I didn't think so. I'd never gone through life worrying about people's sizes, so I had no basis for comparison.\
Compared with Kai, of course I was more generously built. But that was because Kai was far too thin. She was slender and short, and even when I brushed away the hands reaching for me, I found myself taking care not to hurt her. Deep down, I didn't want to hurt Kai.\
I could say all sorts of things, but the fact was that I was becoming absorbed in Kai.\
I knew the hidden loveliness beneath her surface. I knew her circumstances. I knew her wounds, her wishes, her kindness... though she didn't seem to have much of that. I knew the occasional glimpse of her youthful side, too.\
And I couldn't forget the beauty of her eyes.\
Those yellow-green eyeballs, like the outline of the sun burned into one's vision.\
More than anything else, they dissolved the distance between Kai and me.\
...There I went again, thinking about nothing but Kai.

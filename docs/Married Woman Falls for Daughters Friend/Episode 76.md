## Chapter Five: The Clod of Earth Is Sweeter Than Snow – Part Two

\
“Mum, have you got more grey hairs?”\
“Huh?”\
Barely had we exchanged our morning greetings when my daughter suddenly launched an attack on me.\
“Being told I've suddenly started looking old is making me feel like crying, you know.”\
“Have I?” I said, pinching a strand of my hair between my fingers. Then I realised what she meant.\
I'd forgotten to dye my hair. I normally did it at the beginning of each month, but February was already the third.\
A little of my natural blonde was beginning to show through, and apparently it looked like grey hair.\
“Sorry to put you through so much hardship, dear wife.”\
My husband joined the conversation as he carried the breakfast plates over. He, too, examined my hair before pronouncing, “It looks positively radiant.”\
“Not just grey hair. Looks like you've lost some of it, too.”\
“This conversation is getting out of hand.”\
I didn't think that was something to laugh so cheerfully about, daughter dear.\
“Joking aside, did you forget to dye your hair?”\
“I only realised when you mentioned it.”\
Until now, I'd never once missed my monthly appointment with the dye. Two days ago... Ah. I looked away.\
That had been the day I'd been so preoccupied with going to see Kai.\
“I knew you dyed it, but what colour is your hair naturally, Mum? Red?”\
“It's not quite that exotic. ...Blonde.”\
“Your hair is said to be like golden thread, or so the story goes.”\
“Don't go spreading such ridiculous rumours.”\
I wondered who had told my husband that. Perhaps my mother? My father had been absent for as long as I could remember, but my mother was still alive and well at my childhood home. She was enjoying a bountiful retirement, with spoiling her granddaughter as her greatest pleasure. Perhaps that was why my daughter was so attached to her. There had even been talk of her moving in with us, but in the end, my mother had declined. I like this house, she'd said.\
“Actually, Dad's never seen your hair in its completely natural state, either.”\
“I was already dyeing it by the time I went to university.”\
“Blonde, huh...”\
My daughter let out a little giggle she seemed unable to contain.\
Lately, I'd noticed her getting rather excited about things. It had started after she'd suddenly messaged us on a day off to say she was going to eat out. Perhaps something really had happened that day after all. I hoped she wouldn't get hooked on something more dangerous than spending money on games. I found myself worrying about her while conveniently forgetting my own situation.\
I had already made Kai the centre of my world.\
But I had experienced what happened to me when too much time passed without seeing Kai. I had resigned myself to the fact that there was no going back now. All I could do was move forwards, carrying everything I had with me, whatever path lay ahead, even if there was no path at all. Retreat was no longer an option.\
The three of us finished breakfast, then hurried off to prepare for the day. Getting dressed, putting on make-up, and in my case, sending a message.\
I'm coming to pick you up today.\
I gazed out of the window.\
February had arrived, and unfortunately, it was raining outside. Snow was a rare occurrence around here.\
I folded the umbrella we'd left open in the entranceway to dry and handed it to my daughter.\
“Watch your step. The ground's slippery.”\
“More importantly, it's cold, Mum!”\
My daughter held out her hands, asking for help, so I took them and rubbed them between mine, warming them together.\
“That's warmed me up just enough.”\
“Oh, you've rubbed off all the cream I put on!”\
“Drive carefully, too, Mum!”\
With my daughter now sporting a pair of hands shining like polished glass, she cheerfully headed off to school.\
“Have a good day!”\
I waved her off with a smile. Why was this woman smiling? Was it some kind of self-deprecating joke?\
I was a mother who handed an umbrella to her daughter and sent her off to school, only to go and pick up a different middle-school girl.\
I was a woman who waved her daughter goodbye with a gentle smile before hurrying off to see Kai.\
“Are you heading out already, too?”\
My husband asked as he hurried around checking the locks on the house. Our dog Maron was trotting along beside him, looking up at me with those round, innocent eyes.\
“I'll take care of locking up.”\
“Thank you. As for your hair... If you remember, let's dye it when you get home.”\
“Make sure you write the appointment on your palm, then.”\
“I didn't even do that when I was in primary school!”\
We shared a gentle laugh. I waved goodbye to Maron as I left, but Maron simply continued looking up at me. Didn't bark, didn't object, and didn't even come over to show me any affection.\
Our usual morning was still here, in this house.\
At least, the outward appearance of this family hadn't changed.\
But the happy home I'd supposedly built through my own choices was undoubtedly beginning to fall apart from within, its foundations being undermined by a worm eating away at its very heart.

## Chapter Four: The Snow Melts into the Clod of Earth - Part Eight

\
I must have been staring into space, because I'd been directing the water at exactly the same spot for ages.\
My mind was elsewhere. And as for where it was, I didn't want to pin that down.\
Come to think of it, there had been a time before when Kai had suddenly turned up while I was washing the car, catching me completely by surprise.\
“...Washing the car is sooooo much fun.”\
I hadn't been expecting anything in particular. I was simply trying to give myself a little encouragement.\
Then, without warning, my phone rang, nearly making me jump out of my skin.\
“...You've got to be kidding me.”\
It was Kai. Was I being watched or something?\
My heart was already racing, and the hurried, forward-leaning pace of my steps soon left me short of breath.\
I turned off the water, set the hose down for a moment, and checked the message.\
"*What are you doing right now?*"\
Are you watching me from somewhere? I wondered, glancing around without thinking.\
"*Washing the car.*"\
"*What a productive way to spend your day off.*"\
She was saying something she didn't mean. If I ran that through a translator, it would come out as I couldn't care less.\
"*What about you?*"\
"*I'm bored, so I'm talking to you, Yuki-san.*"\
“Right, sure.”\
Recently, Kai had got the hang of using her phone, and she hardly made any typing mistakes any more. She'd even started sending me pictures of snacks she'd bought at the hotel shop. The other day, she'd sent a photo taken with those people connected to her through the mysterious bond of being her hotel friends. There was Kai, expressionless; the self-proclaimed genius novelist, standing with her arms folded in a smug pose; and the smiling cosmic beauty looking on at them. Even crouching down, the latter still hadn't quite managed to fit herself properly into the frame.\
"*It's not as if I'm bored.*"\
"*If you are, Yuki-san, would you like to go on a date with me?*"\
Listen to what I'm saying, will you?\
“A date?”\
You don't just casually invite a married woman out like that.\
Before I could reply, messages began piling up one after another.\
"*The way I phrased that, as a question, was a lie. Let me put it another way.*"\
"*I want to go on a date with you. Please.*"\
“...A date...”\
Once again, I faltered over the same word. A date with Kai... But how would that even work?\
We couldn't even walk together down a main street. Not that I was going on a date with her in the first place.\
Another message arrived almost immediately. I was about to click my tongue at her for being so impatient, but it was a different notification, this time from my daughter.\
"*I'm going to eat lunch out.*"\
“...I did wonder why she wasn't coming home.”\
It seemed she'd made some last-minute plans. I wondered if she was meeting someone outside.\
"*With a friend?*"\
"*Yeah.*"\
"*Don't stay out too late.*"\
"*I'll come straight home after eating!*"\
As we exchanged messages, a thought occurred to me that would never have crossed my mind before: perhaps this friend was a lie, and she was meeting a boyfriend instead. My daughter was fourteen, after all. She was an honest girl, but of course she'd have secrets from her parents. And yet, what made the thought so painful was that I wasn't being suspicious because of anything she'd done. It was because I was the one keeping secrets.\
People who have something to hide become suspicious of others. I'd ended up learning that unpleasant truth through firsthand experience.\
If my daughter was eating out, I wouldn't have to prepare lunch either. My husband had unusually overslept today, so there hadn't been time to make him anything to take for lunch. The house and my schedule were both completely clear, leaving only the car and me.\
My husband was at work. My daughter was out and wouldn't be back for a while.\
I was alone at home.\
“Ah.”\
A thought suddenly struck me.\
If I wanted to go and see her, I could, couldn't I?\
I could sneak out in the car while my husband was at work.\
“Don't be ridiculous.”\
As if I'd go to such lengths just to see Kai.\
What exactly was different about that and what I was already doing?\
Shut up, I snapped at my own smart mouth, irritated by its incessant chatter.\
"*Haven't you replied yet?*"\
You shut up, too.\
“Just wait a minute, will you?”\
Obviously, saying it aloud wouldn't do any good. But I found myself speaking into the phone anyway, despite there being no call connected.\
"*Wait.*"\
"*If you're hesitating, you should go on the date.*"\
Her advice came flying in, entirely self-serving. If you're hesitating... The truth was, I shouldn't even be allowed to hesitate.\
There was no way I could bring excuses like looking after Kai or acting as her guardian into a private meeting on my day off. What exactly would I be looking after? What would I be protecting her from?\
If I didn't see her today, I didn't know whether I'd be able to make time next week either. My eyes darted about.\
You want to see her, don't you? In the end.\
The version of myself pretending to be irresponsible was now trying to take responsibility, telling me I could at least say I'd been pushed into it. But enough. Enough, enough, enough... Set everything else aside and face just one question: did I want to see her?\
I closed my eyes.\
When I opened them again, the world had clouded over. My vision was blurred.\
"*Just for a little while.*"\
I sent the reply, and immediately wanted to take it back. But I couldn't.\
Words were like that. Once they stepped forward, they couldn't turn back.\
My heart and the pulse in my wrist were racing wildly, each refusing to match the rhythm of my steps. It was hard to breathe.\
"*I'll be waiting.*"\
"*It's not a date.*"\
The message was marked as read, but no reply came. Irritated, I sent another.\
"*I have to get ready, so it'll take a little while.*"\
"*I'm eagerly awaiting your arrival.*"\
"*Don't rush me.*"\
Even so, I couldn't afford to take my time.\
I felt guilty about going out without telling my family, and I had no room to spare in my mind.\
I put away the hose and returned the cleaning equipment to the garage before going inside. I left a note for my daughter in the living room, washed my hands in the bathroom, then headed for the bedroom. I'd have to hurry with my make-up, too.\
A day off that had held no plans after I'd finished washing the car had suddenly become hectic. It reminded me of my university days, when I had time to spare and would dash around my room in a flurry after an unexpected invitation to go on a date.\
Why was I devoting so much time to getting ready to see Kai?\
Even if I could leave my clothes as they were, I still needed to make at least the bare minimum preparations.\
Because it was a date.\
“It's not a date.”\
I was a grown woman, and I had to draw that line, mindful of my position.\
We might have kissed, but I wasn't going to allow a date.\
The white lines I'd drawn on the ground were probably not a single one of them straight.\
Once I'd put on a face I could show Kai, I hurried towards the front door and slipped on my going-out shoes.\
How long had it been since my last date?\
Not that this was a date.\
In the car I'd only just washed, I was heading out to be covered in sea breeze and sand all over again.\
Would there come a day when even I could no longer wash the smell of the sea from myself?

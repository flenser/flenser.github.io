## Chapter Four: The Snow Melts into the Clod of Earth - Part Nine

\
As soon as I entered the hotel car park, I spotted Kai. Had she come outside to wait for me, rather than staying in her room? I parked the car and went to pick her up. Just looking at her, I felt the tension drain from my entire body, so much so that I nearly said her name aloud. Kai... She was strange. She made me feel both at ease and ill at ease at the same time.\
Kai, however, didn't get into the car straight away. Instead, she beckoned me to come outside.\
I tilted my head in puzzlement and got out. Kai, still wearing her uniform despite it being the weekend, took my hand.\
“What is it?”\
She laced her fingers firmly through mine, then swung our joined hands back and forth.\
“It's a date. A car park date.”\
“I've never heard of that kind of date before.”\
To begin with, it wasn't a date. Kai, labouring under a serious misconception, took my hand and started walking.\
“Let's go all the way round the car park.”\
“...And here you go, starting something weird again.”\
I sighed, but decided to humour her. At least here, we didn't have to worry too much about being seen.\
After all, nobody else was going to wander around a car park of their own free will in this bitterly cold wind.\
Besides, if Kai had genuinely given some thought to what a date might be and come up with this proposal, there was something rather endearing about it. Kai was always trying to steer things in an indecent direction, so when she offered me something wholesome, I wanted to seize it and treasure it.\
The car park, exposed directly to the sea breeze, would have been pleasant in a milder season. But now the wind slipped through even the tiniest gaps in our clothes, making me shiver. The only thing that had a special warmth was the hand I held with Kai. Its insistently distinctive heat was a little embarrassing.\
“Are you cold?”\
“I'm used to being cold, so...”\
Kai trailed off, then suddenly looked at me as though an idea had struck her.\
After inserting a rather contrived little cough, she said:\
“It's much colder when I can't be with you, Yuki-san.”\
“First of all, stop saying whatever happens to pop into your head.”\
It was a little endearing, though.\
“Well, there's something romantic about a date by the sea.”\
“That's right. Look at those palm trees swaying like crazy.”\
The wind howled in intermittent, slicing gusts. It wasn't so much romantic as simply windy. My carefully arranged hair had already fallen apart, and unless I held it down, it tickled my skin as it blew across my face.\
“I thought I wouldn't get to see you again this week, Yuki-san.”\
“...I suppose.”\
“I didn't really expect you to come, either.”\
“...I suppose not.”\
“This is starting to sound like the speech given by a student representative at a graduation ceremony.”\
“....................................”\
She was certainly talking a lot, but there wasn't much substance to it. From the hurried, almost breathless succession of remarks, I began to understand.\
“Are you perhaps embarrassed?”\
“No. Not at all.”\
The answer was unmistakable.\
Honestly, the things she usually did and said were far more likely to make someone blush.\
How strange, I thought, and found myself smiling.\
“It's just that when we're walking around holding hands, it feels like we're showing everyone that we're a couple.”\
“Uh... Even setting everything else aside, I don't think anyone would mistake us for a couple.”\
Given the considerable difference in our heights, from a distance we would probably look less like people on a date and more like a slightly over-affectionate parent and child out for a stroll.\
“Hmm...” Kai seemed aware of it herself. She looked up at me, as though measuring the difference in our heights.\
“No. We're a lovey-dovey couple.”\
“Wro-ng.”\
As if to prove her point, Kai swung our joined hands vigorously.\
I felt that only made us look even less like a couple.\
The sea breeze, carrying what felt like grains of snow, sent Kai's unkempt hair and uniform flying about. As her uniform clung to her body, it emphasised just how slender her torso was, so thin that it looked as though the wind might scatter it into pieces. Setting aside the weekday afternoons when she was at school, did she make sure to eat properly in the mornings and evenings?\
While I was studying her, Kai was also gazing intently up at me. Whatever shape those eyes took, whatever they brought with them, they first pierced straight through me, then poured their feelings towards me without the slightest restraint through whatever gaps they found. It wasn't so much that my resistance was weak. It was that the moment she looked at me, my resistance was already being pierced.\
“What?”\
“Looking at you again, Yuki-san, even in your ordinary clothes, you're pretty sexy.”\
“Would anything I wore be good enough for you?”\
I asked lightly, exasperated by Kai's indiscriminate compliments.\
Kai paused for a moment. Then, expressionless, she answered:\
“I suppose so.”\
She agreed without the slightest hesitation.\
“...Oh. Right.”\
I shouldn't have asked.\
“I don't understand what makes you embarrassed.”\
“Probably, the things you don't think are embarrassing are the things that embarrass me.”\
“...Honestly, you're so twisted.”\
“Not as much as you, Yuki-san.”\
The way she answered was already the height of perversity.\
“Kai, don't you ever want to wear something else?”\
“If it's your clothes, Yuki-san.”\
“I really don't understand what you're trying to say.”\
I couldn't be bothered to correct her any more, but I wasn't Yuki-san.\
“I thought I'd made myself quite clear.”\
“...........................You pervy little brat.”\
That was seriously creepy. If Kai hadn't been the one saying it, I thought I might have taken her to the authorities and pressed charges.\
But because it was Kai, it could be dismissed with a single insult.\
And now, looking back, I regretted having let someone like this wear my clothes before, even if it had been an emergency.\
...What sort of thoughts had she had while putting on my underwear? I was afraid to ask. Afraid to know.\
We slowly made our way round the not particularly large car park, and by the time we returned to the car, we were thoroughly frozen. Still, our joined hands and faces had been protected by their own warmth, so they were a little better off. They did feel rather tingly, though. Our circulation must have been excellent. Kai seemed much the same. Her already pale cheeks had acquired a pleasant flush of colour.\
“That was a lovely date.”\
“Not a da-a-ate.”\
I started the car. Now then, where should we go?\
Even if it were a date, I mused as I drove... No, it wasn't a date. I hurried to correct myself.\
It was troublesome how easily I forgot. There was no date, no date between us.\
Then what was there? I didn't know. After all, there was a twenty-four-year age gap between us.\
If someone we knew saw us walking side by side, they'd surely find the pairing incomprehensible.\
Which meant we couldn't exactly go wandering around outside without a care.\
Two people who couldn't go anywhere. It seemed to reflect the nature of our relationship rather well.\
“If going somewhere in town would be a problem, why don't we just go for a drive?”\
“...Yes, I suppose.”\
“We could even go to the rice-ball shop.”\
“Don't even joke about that.”\
I shot her a sideways glare. Kai simply gazed out at the scenery, pretending not to notice.\
“Hmmm.”\
“What is that suggestive little noise supposed to mean?”\
Kai turned her gaze back to me, wearing a slightly irritating, provocative smile.\
“You don't want your husband to know about me, do you?”\
“...What?”\
“So you really are conscious of me, after all.”\
“...It's just difficult and troublesome to explain.”\
That was half the truth. My husband wouldn't welcome Kai, in any sense of the word. He was mild-mannered, but he would never accept anything that harmed his family. ...Yes. Surely, not even me.\
If my husband heard everything I'd done up to this point, he wouldn't laugh it off and forgive me.\
What on earth was I doing? The question kept coming back, over and over.\
Of course my family was still precious to me. That hadn't changed. I was still the same person. No. That was a considerable lie. I had changed. I'd been lying to my family and going to see Kai.\
Even now, I'd left home with nothing more than a brief note. I was driving in precisely the opposite direction from my home.\
I was trying to change the centre of my world.\
It was tantamount to setting out for a new world. A truly terrifying thing to do.

## Chapter Four: The Snow Melts into the Clod of Earth - Part Two

\
In the quiet atmosphere of the office, the sound of fingers striking keys punctuated the silence.\
All through working hours, I could think about something else entirely while another version of me handled my tasks without a hitch.\
Since meeting Kai, I had acquired a strange new talent.\
“…ai.”\
I covered my mouth with my hand, just as I was about to murmur Kai’s name.\
Was Kai really all right as things stood?\
Why did I have to worry about Kai? My thoughts were jumping back and forth with such ferocity that it felt as though two separate lines of reasoning were following different routes to my brain, each arriving to argue its case. My head was unbearably noisy.\
I’d been like this a little while ago, too, thinking about my Santa outfit. Did I have no sense of time passing, no ability to make progress? Kai, Kai, Kai. Shut up already. Whether I was denying it or affirming it, all I ever seemed to do was say Kai’s name. I was going to lose my mind. No, I already had. That was why my thoughts were splitting apart.\
Why not go and see her?\
The thought leaked into my typing, and I hurriedly deleted it.\
What would happen if I went to see her? What would become of me? I had made the right choice. I had decided I couldn’t see a girl who told me she loved me despite my being married. A girl… or rather, a child. So why was I trying to make the wrong choice?\
Why did I want to make the wrong choice?\
The impure thought that I wanted to make the wrong choice circled through my head, and its sheer absurdity made me angry.\
I had a family. My beloved husband, daughter and dog were waiting for me at home, and there wasn’t the slightest shadow over the love I felt for them.\
You know, going out of your way to say that makes it sound rather unconvincing.\
I brushed away the imaginary Kai as she laughed at me, telling her to shut up.\
The fundamental problem was that I was the one who saw Kai as endearing. I was the one at fault. The problem was that I had found so many things to cherish in my interactions with her. When Kai told me she loved me, something had come into being inside me, and it would never disappear.\
Kai loved me.\
A middle school girl, harbouring sexual desire for a married woman who had just turned thirty-eight. It was a naked, unvarnished desire that couldn’t be explained away as a passing whim or a consequence of her age. Remembering how Kai had licked various parts of my body, my cheeks and ears suddenly grew hot. The turmoil inside my chest asserted itself with such force that it became almost unpleasant.\
I wanted to hear Kai’s voice. My true feelings clawed at my own skin, threatening to spill out along with the blood.\
I wanted to peer into Kai’s eyes. I wanted her to peer into mine. I wanted to gaze at her. My heart, captivated by those inexplicable colours, felt as though it might dissolve into the light at any moment, leaving me completely white and empty. If nobody had been watching, I would have stamped my feet hard against the floor and ground my teeth until the urge subsided.\
Shut up. For goodness’ sake, Kai was a middle school girl.\
A second-year middle school student.\
And that Kai loved me.\
How many times did I have to turn it over in my mind before I was satisfied? And why, each time I did, did my consciousness seem to go soft and round, as though I were drifting into a daze?\
It had been so long since anyone had confessed their feelings to me that, despite my age, I was still thoroughly shaken.\
It was as though I were once again bathing in the same passion I had felt when love split my head open like a stone, sending blood spraying out in a torrent that left even the blood itself boiling with excitement. Where I had once been parched, Kai’s blood was soaking into me now, painfully.\
I couldn’t speak for Kai, but for me, openly revealing one’s affection was an act of courage.\
Once a confession had been spoken aloud, there was no taking it back.\
Whether for better or worse, existing relationships were always liable to break apart.\
“Hm.”\
“Ah… oh!”\
At some point, the head of the office had come up beside me and begun peering at my screen. Our eyes met at close range, and I nearly jumped out of my skin.\
“Is something the matter?”\
I fiddled with the hair brushing against my ear, somehow managing to maintain my composure.\
“No, it’s just that your work seems more original than before, somehow… I feel like the motifs you use in your designs have changed.”\
“…I haven’t really been conscious of it.”\
In truth, perhaps it was precisely because I was leaving things to my unconscious mind that I was producing work so different from before.\
“I think it’s interesting. As long as the clients are happy with it, there’s absolutely no problem.”\
“Yes…”\
“Has anything happened to you lately?\
Well, I met a middle school girl who licked my toes.\
“Nothing in particular.”\
It was the only answer I could give.\
After the head of the office moved away, I looked over the design I had most recently worked on.\
The background, built around yellow-green, formed numerous circles like eyes, drawing the viewer into them as though they might be swallowed whole.\
…Apart from the colours, that was.\
Viewed objectively, I felt there was more to draw people in than there had been before.\
Perhaps it was because I was putting onto the page the passion I felt for something that had drawn me in.\
That Kai’s eyes stirred something special in me… was something I had no choice but to acknowledge. I would accept that I was captivated by those eyes. But I would look away from what that might lead to, from whatever feelings might begin to take root there. The age gap, our genders, our respective positions, my family. Everything I had built up to this point could be brought crashing down.\
And yet, the fact that I understood this meant that, in a way, I was already acknowledging it.\
My stomach hurt. It had been hurting constantly, clenched tight by intense stress. When it got worse, I began hallucinating the interior of the hotel. I felt as though I had gone there, and my feet would grow unsteady beneath me.\
I was in a bad way. It seemed only a matter of time before the strain on my mind began to damage my physical health, too.\
And all this had come about simply because I had been forcing myself, little by little, not to go and see Kai.\
Forcing myself?\
I had made the decision not to see her. What, then, was I enduring?\
I had chosen the obvious course, putting my family before Kai, and the feelings I was bottling up as a result were unbearable. Something that lay at the very opposite end of happiness remained undigested, stagnating inside me.\
Was I going to spend every day from now on, endlessly bewildered by the absent Kai, letting time slip away just like this?\
I didn’t think I could bear being burned alive by that eternal restlessness.\
The path to salvation had been visible from the beginning. No matter how far I kept running down some other road, I would eventually find a fork waiting beside me, ready to offer me that same path. Or perhaps I had merely been pretending to run, without taking a single step forward.\
If I went to see her, I could be free of this pain.\
And then I would come home carrying an even more devastating regret.\
Fate lay entirely exposed before me. Never before had I been able to see through a crossroads in my life so clearly. I had been granted the power of prophecy, the ability to see the future. Avoiding it was almost ridiculously easy.\
“…………………………………………”\
Even the sensation of my fingernails digging into my arm through my clothes took the shape of Kai’s name.\
Someone was protecting me.\
Someone who would show me even the future itself, gently admonishing me that I didn’t have to make the wrong choice.\
The goodness inherent in common sense, something everyone possessed.\
And yet, I was still trying to go down the wrong path. I wanted to go down it.\
Like someone so thirsty that they would drink from a river even if they knew its waters were poison.\
Longing to be released, I kept making one mistake after another.\
I picked up my phone. My lips remained half-open, as though I were about to breathe life into it.\
Looking down at the screen, my eyes felt so dry that the sensation itself seemed capable of provoking tears.\
“I’ll be a little late coming home today. Probably only a little.”\
I was foolishly convincing myself that, by setting a limit and promising it would only be a little, I could keep myself from straying too far off the path.

## Interlude One

\
I decided that, after all, I ought to thank her.\
During the short break after the first lesson, I went over to her desk. It felt as though an invisible membrane surrounded it, keeping everything around her at a certain distance.\
You could call that isolation. At the same time, though, there was something almost noble about it, as if she allowed no impurities to approach.\
“Chitaira-san.”\
When I called out to her, her peach-pink hair shifted slightly, and she raised her face, which had been hidden beneath it. I remembered her as having an expressionless face, almost as though she were wearing a mask. Her appearance was so childlike that she hardly seemed our age. Not merely a year below us, she gave the impression of being a primary-school pupil.\
And then there were her yellow-green eyes, undoubtedly the rarest colour in the classroom.\
“Thanks for yesterday.”\
“You're welcome.”\
She was so rarely seen talking to anyone in the classroom that there might have been classmates who had never even heard her voice. It was like snow: a voice in which an almost-melting warmth coexisted with an ephemeral fragility.\
Yet there was a distance and coldness to it, as though nothing could ever settle upon either my ears or my heart.\
“I'm glad your cold's better.”\
There was no change in her tone, and she showed no particular interest. But at least she hadn't brushed me off, so I rubbed my fingers together with a little, “Heheh.”\
“I stayed in bed yesterday as a precaution, but I was mostly better. I saw you through the intercom.”\
“Hmm… Was your mother home all day yesterday?”\
“No. She came home early from work.”\
“Oh, she works, does she…?”\
I was surprised to see Chitaira-san's expression soften ever so slightly. But I was equally puzzled as to what there was about what I'd just said that could have prompted that reaction.\
Meanwhile, I could feel my friends watching me as I stood there talking to Chitaira-san. I kept shifting my feet awkwardly, making little movements from side to side. Chitaira-san's eyes followed me vaguely.\
“Is that all?”\
She tilted her head as if asking whether I had anything else to say, then continued.\
“Well, I suppose so. You and I aren't friends, Amamiya-san.”\
She said it quite plainly. It was true that we'd hardly spoken since being put in the same class.\
The uncomfortable thing about being around Chitaira-san at school wasn't Chitaira-san herself. It was everyone else's attention.\
Chitaira-san was avoided by everyone in the class. No, if my impression was correct, even the teachers seemed to keep their distance from her. There were rumours going around about the Chitaira family and all sorts of things, but I wasn't comfortable with anything that strayed into the territory of malicious gossip. If I wanted to know about her, I should talk to her directly.\
Otherwise, it felt as though the light above my head wouldn't turn on properly… as if I might be swallowed by darkness. It made me uneasy.\
“Ah, well… How about we become friends starting today?”\
Since we were in the same class, I thought I ought to welcome the opportunity to make a new friend.\
Chitaira-san widened those peculiar eyes of hers, then looked away.\
“Rather carefree, aren't you?”\
“Did you say something?”\
Her eyes immediately returned to me.\
“Sounds nice. I look forward to it, Amamiya-san.”\
“Yeah!”\
We exchanged our agreement in words, as though shaking hands.\
Watching her behave in such a gentle, courteous manner, I couldn't understand why the other children avoided her instead of talking to her. What happened in her family had very little to do with us, after all.\
“…Amamiya-san, you just added me as a friend, didn't you?”\
“Huh? Oh, yes. I did. You're my friend now.”\
“Well, this isn't exactly repayment for yesterday, but… I'm stupid, so could you help me with my studies?”\
Chitaira-san tapped the side of her head, as though demonstrating that there was nothing inside.\
“I don't mind, but… which subject?”\
“Lots. All the textbooks.”\
She picked up the textbooks she'd prepared for the next lesson and shook them vigorously. Naturally, nothing fell out from between the pages. I almost tilted my head, wondering whether she was serious. I had no idea what Chitaira-san's grades were like, but I couldn't remember the teachers ever giving her any particular warnings or extra instruction.\
Then again, perhaps that was because she was kept at such a distance that nobody even said anything when she skipped physical education.\
“You mean at my house?”\
“Yeah. You absolutely shouldn't come to mine. If you did… your mother would get angry with you.”\
Once again, Chitaira-san's mouth softened, as though she were imagining it. I began to understand that her sense of humour was focused rather differently from mine. I felt as though the gap between us had grown a little wider, too.\
Still, studying at my house would be convenient. We wouldn't have to worry about the uncomfortable atmosphere at school.\
Making friends with someone new was something that appealed to my curiosity.\
“Sure. But my room and I might still have a bit of a cold lingering, so you could catch it.”\
“It's fine. I've never caught a cold before.”\
“Really?”\
As though evading the question, Chitaira-san simply hummed and turned towards the blackboard.\
“What was that about?”\
The break was almost over, so I returned to my seat. One of my friends nearby cautiously spoke to me.\
“Hmm? I just went to thank her.”\
“Thank her for what?”\
“Ah, well, yesterday, you know. I was absent, so…”\
After that, my friends asked me all sorts of questions.\
I'd only gone to thank her for yesterday. Was that really so strange?\
It made me feel as though I was the strange one, and that frightened me a little.\
Oh, but speaking of strange, there was one thing that certainly was.\
Chitaira-san and I had become friends today. Which meant that yesterday, we hadn't been friends.\
And yet, Chitaira-san didn't live anywhere near me. So why had she gone out of her way to deliver something to my house?\
That really was strange. I stretched my neck from my seat and secretly peeked over at Chitaira-san.\
Maybe she's just an incredibly nice person who volunteered to do it, I thought, laughing at how far-fetched my own idea was.\
Then our eyes met.\
Chitaira-san gave me a rare, faint smile and waved.\
When her expression changed and a little light appeared in her eyes, she seemed like a completely different person.\
I was simple-minded, just like my father. Perhaps she really was an incredibly nice person after all.\
That was what I found myself thinking.

## Chapter Four: The Snow Melts into the Clod of Earth - Part Four

\
For the first time in ages, I woke feeling refreshed, and let out a long sigh.\
My head was clear. Nothing had been resolved. If anything, the situation might even have worsened. Yet the disappearance of a single worry had given me room to breathe, and I was mistaking that for a genuine improvement.\
Everything concerning Kai had merely been put on hold, but even that was enough to create the illusion.\
Perhaps that was the only way I could keep my relationship with Kai going. I…\
I sat up without any surprise. I had known that seeing her would make me feel at least a little better.\
I had been worrying about both Kai and my family. If Kai disappeared from my life, all that would happen was that I would be left feeling anxious and alone over my family instead. See? Things were a little easier than before. What kind of subtraction was that supposed to be?\
If I really went through life doing nothing but subtracting, what would be left at the end?\
I climbed out of bed, where my husband was no longer beside me, and felt the cold air directly against my skin, without the futon between us. “Brr, cold,” I muttered. For someone with my name, I was surprisingly bad at winter. I wanted the weather to warm up soon.\
Even after leaving the bedroom, the confusion and emotional instability that had plagued me until yesterday had receded, leaving my vision clear.\
The absent-mindedness in which everything simply slid across the surface of my mind and flowed away had lifted. My emotions and the sensations beneath my feet had taken root again. My steps felt dramatically lighter. It was almost enough to make me believe I had suddenly become half my age.\
I hurried to the washroom and rinsed my mouth. When I finished and raised my head, the face in the mirror looked remarkably alert for someone who had only just woken up. My lips and cheeks were relaxed to just the right degree. The habitual severity of my expression, something even I was usually conscious of, had eased.\
What on earth was I so pleased about?\
“……………………………………”\
Even with the winter holidays in between, I had left her alone for two weeks.\
And that girl still liked me just as much as ever.\
That ridiculous relief had loosened something inside me.\
I had rejected Kai after she told me she loved me, yet if that had made her stop loving me, I suspected I would have been displeased. I was concealing my selfishness no better than Kai, and had no business criticising her for it. But what if Kai started treating me coldly? What if she lost interest in me, and I became just another person among all those she looked at without a second thought?\
Would I be able to accept that? Would I be able to do nothing?\
“...It's not a question of whether I could.”\
I had to. I reminded myself of that as I opened the door to the kitchen, where my husband was waiting.\
“Morning. Someone's up a little early today?”\
“Yeah. I woke up, so I thought I might as well get up.”\
I pulled out a chair and sat down. My husband held out a small bowl.\
“First, try this.”\
The bowl, coloured like a goldfish, was filled with mandarin oranges preserved in syrup.\
“What are these?”\
“Our little princess loves them, so I figured this was Dad's chance to show off his skills. Thought I'd give it a go.”\
“Oh, really?”\
“If the season's right, I think grapes would work well, too.”\
Speaking of cooking, Kai had told me she wanted to try something I'd made myself.\
I knew there was no way I could serve her anything that would satisfy her, so I wasn't particularly keen on the idea.\
Besides, I didn't really understand what the point of cooking for Kai would be in the first place.\
“Still, having made these, I have to say, canned fruit from the shops is pretty well done. So convenient.”\
“Hm…”\
At any rate, I tried one.\
The sweet-and-sour flavour leaned towards a refreshing tartness, leaving a very pleasant aftertaste. The juice and syrup went down smoothly, too. There was no doubt it would be exactly the sort of taste our daughter would enjoy.\
“It's delicious. Did you put lemon in the syrup…?”\
“Mum, since when did you become a member of the Gourmet Club?”\
“Are you underestimating my palate?”\
We both burst out laughing. It wasn't the syrup's sweet-and-sour kind of laughter, but something calmer and more settled.\
Sweetness and acidity were, after all, ingredients found in abundance in emotions that had yet to mature.\
Like those of a middle-school girl, for instance.\
“What about breakfast? It's still a bit early.”\
There was still time before the day began in earnest, before I had to get on with maintaining everything I had spent so long building.\
What that time meant to my husband and me, however, was different.\
My husband naturally put his family first in everything he did. It was dazzling.\
So dazzling that I almost felt tears coming.\
I was supposed to be the same.\
“If I'm already up… I might as well eat and go to work…”\
“Huh?”\
My husband recoiled at my words. I knew he was putting on an act, but my heart still shrank.\
“I've just witnessed a radiance in your words like the rising sun itself.”\
“Whatever else, once you get there, there's always work to do.”\
Not that I was actually going in, of course.\
Someone laughed. Probably me.\
It frightened me that I could lie so naturally, but what chilled me far more was how little guilt I seemed to feel. No, the guilt was still there. My heart was simply resorting to tricks to keep it from showing.\
As I prepared breakfast with my husband, I stole glances at his face when I thought he wouldn't notice.\
What expression would he make if I told him that yesterday I'd gone to a hotel and kissed a middle-school girl? Disgust. Confusion. Contempt. Anger. A torrent of emotions, impossible to sort into a single category, would surely come crashing down on me.\
That was why I said nothing.\
In other words, I did feel ashamed of my relationship with Kai.\
None of it was something I could openly talk about.\
And yet, as far as Kai was concerned… I didn't think the affection I felt for her was anything to be ashamed of.\
It wasn't.\
I tilted my face upwards and whispered the words so softly that even my husband, standing this close, couldn't hear them.\
After breakfast, before beginning the rest of my preparations, I went into the bedroom and picked up my phone. If Kai was going to walk to school, she'd have to leave quite early, so I hurried to contact her before she set off.\
I'll come and pick you up.\
Now that Kai and I had reconciled? I ought to drive her in the mornings again, as I used to. I could increase the time we spent together while making it look like nothing more than an act of goodwill.\
At last, my sluggish guilt was beginning to reach my heart.\
And yet, even as that made me feel uneasy, Kai gave me no response.\
Again. The message was marked as read almost immediately, but there was no reply. Irritated, I switched off the screen.\
Yesterday, she'd kept my mouth occupied the entire time, leaving me no chance to get angry.\
Today, I decided, I would give her a piece of my mind.\
And so, in order to see Kai, spend time with Kai, and talk to Kai, I began cutting into the time I spent at home.\
Days in which everything revolved around Kai.\
As long as I wanted to, I could still turn back. I could go back to the point before I reached Kai.\
But I could no longer return to the person I had been before I met her.\
Kai had forcibly reset my point of origin.

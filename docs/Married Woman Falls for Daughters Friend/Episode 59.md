## Chapter Four: The Snow Melts into the Clod of Earth - Part Three

\
I left the office and entered the church grounds. Inside my thoroughly chilled car, before starting the engine, I glared at my phone in the darkness.\
What was wrong with me, getting irritated at the thought that I hadn’t received a single message or notification? I felt as though I was asking her, 'Are you really all right with things as they are?' What was I doing, turning into such a tiresome woman at my age?\
But once I had come to know Kai’s less-than-honest manner and what lay hidden beneath it, I simply couldn’t suppress my fixation. I couldn’t let go of the sense of superiority I felt at being the only person who had touched the essence of who Kai really was.\
Shivering in the cold, my breath and hands trembling, I hesitated.\
I turned my phone face down. Sat still. Began to feel sick, so I tried to stop myself and put it away in my bag. Turned to face forwards, ready to go home. My arms and legs felt heavy, as though something were pulling at them. I felt ill. Unsettled. I rummaged through my bag. Grabbed my phone. Put it away again before turning it on and entering my passcode. Looked away. Couldn’t settle. My stomach hurt. Picked up the phone. Let out a long breath.\
I took deep breath after deep breath. The cold winter air was a blessing now.\
I took my time, waiting for the murk inside my chest to settle a little. Then I realised that, even as I sat there, the time I had left was slipping away. At last, I faced the truth.\
I had to go and check on her.\
I had taken responsibility for her halfway, so I could at least do a little more.\
We should talk things through properly and bring this to an end.\
I had already told my family I would be late.\
And besides… there was no besides any more.\
I had prepared four or five excuses, and only then…\
My fingers moved hesitantly. I covered my face with my hand as I pressed send.\
“I’m coming over now.”\
I’d sent it. I’d really done it.\
The thought that I had done something irreversible made my head feel heavy. My eyelids twitched.\
Despite it being winter, an unpleasant sweat broke out across my back.\
All because I had gone just over two weeks without seeing her.\
I felt something akin to despair at myself for sending exactly the same message as I had several weeks earlier, without a single thing having changed. I had decided not to go, yet it had been so easy to turn back on that decision.\
I was tormented by the convenient fantasy that, if I turned around, Kai might be crouching there, waiting for me. No, knowing Kai, perhaps she had deliberately kept waiting because she expected me to feel this way. Kai was the sort of child who played those kinds of games.\
My message was marked as read almost immediately, but no reply came.\
What the hell? I nearly muttered aloud. I had almost let my heart turn into Kai’s.\
I tossed my phone onto the passenger seat and began driving towards the hotel. I entered the dimly lit road, weighed down by guilt.\
But the tightness in my stomach had eased, if only a little.

After parking at the hotel, I wondered what I would say to Kai about the fact that she still hadn’t replied. Am I really going to see her? I asked myself before getting out of the car.\
I had already made up my mind when I set off for the hotel, yet I was still reluctant to admit it. I didn’t want to give my decision my wholehearted approval.\
Finishing work and not going straight home, stopping somewhere else instead, lying to my family.\
Everything about it was something I couldn’t bear to expose to the light.\
My footsteps echoed strangely loudly as I crossed the car park. I felt as though every person in the world must be able to hear them. The light spilling out of the hotel seemed to burn my eyes and my chest.\
I stepped into that light. The brightness of the lobby nearly blinded me. Then I spotted the strange, exceptionally tall cosmic beauty from the other day, so I kept my head as low as possible as I headed for the lift.\
The doors opened almost immediately, and I was carried upwards.\
Leaving the ground behind, ascending towards Kai’s level.\
I’ve come to see you. Something about that sounded wrong, and I scratched my head, struggling to work out how to greet her.\
After everything that had happened, after everything she had said, I had come back again.\
What was I hoping for between Kai and me?\
My throat tightened and my breathing grew ragged as I knocked on her door.\
It opened almost at once, without any unnecessary suspense.\
“…Ah.”\
A small sound escaped me before I could stop it.\
Kai.\
Chitaira Kai, in her school uniform, was looking up at me intently, exactly as she had two weeks ago.\
Ah, those eyes… So alien, so beautiful, so absolute.\
At the sight of Kai standing there to welcome me, something deep in my chest grew thick and sweet, like melting syrup.\
A heat unlike relief, unlike warmth, reignited inside me.\
What had Kai thought of me, after I had stayed away for so long… a whole two weeks and a bit?\
From those green eyes, drawing my heart towards an endless swamp, I could sense nothing but a beauty so intense it raised goosebumps on my skin.\
My footing was so unsteady that I struggled to maintain even the outward composure of an adult.\
“I thought it wouldn’t be right to… just leave you like this, so I only came to check how you were…”\
She didn’t even give me time to finish my excuse.\
Kai sprang at me. She hooked her arms around my neck, jumped up, and then…\
She kissed me with such force that our cheekbones nearly collided.\
Unlike the fleeting kisses before, this was a meeting of lips that clung to me like a bite. Kai wouldn’t let go. Her weight bore down on my neck and shoulders. I arched backwards to support her, my knees buckling, and the two of us toppled straight to the floor.\
I cradled Kai protectively as we went down in a tangle, taking the brunt of the impact beneath her. While my attention was occupied by the pain, Kai climbed on top of me and interlaced our fingers, pinning my hands in place.\
I found myself on my back, as though I had been thrown down, the world turned upside down before my eyes.\
A chill ran through me when I realised I could see the corridor through the door, which had not been shut completely.\
“Kai, wait, stop…”\
Before I could finish, our front teeth collided, and sparks seemed to scatter across my vision. Kai, still straddling me, continued kissing me without pulling away. She clamped her lips against mine as though she had no intention of ever letting go again, sealing my mouth completely. Her green eyes drew back, then came close again. Just watching them sway like that was enough to make me feel drunk.\
“Just, just stop for a momen—mmph!”\
Ignoring my announcement, Kai’s lips demanded another kiss, almost as though to prevent me from speaking. Again and again, our lips struck one another with a violence that felt like knocking bones together. Even with Kai on top of me, I could probably have thrown her off if I had really tried. But the shock of her sudden attack had yet to wear off, and I could barely resist.\
It wasn’t that I was letting her do this because I wanted to kiss her.\
The kisses continued. Continued. Continued… At this point, I was feeling less romantic than I was fearing for my life. Struggling to breathe, I repeatedly patted Kai on the back, trying to embrace her enough to get the message across. She looked so fragile that I was afraid she might break if I hit her too hard.\
Perhaps Kai had finally reached her limit, too, because at last our lips parted for a brief moment.\
For some reason, I found myself staring at Kai’s trembling lower lip as she caught her breath.\
“You idiot, I’m going to suffocate!”\
I was so desperately short of breath that I could feel tears gathering in my eyes. Yet even after I snapped at her, Kai paused for just one breath before pressing her lips firmly against mine again. Her own face was bright red, flushed by any number of emotions.\
It was only making it hard to breathe, so why…?\
Was it really?\
Was there nothing to my kisses with Kai except the difficulty of breathing?\
I had no room to analyse the answer while deprived of oxygen. My fingertips were beginning to go numb, too.\
“Kai, let me ta—”\
Before I could get as far as talk, she kissed me again. Kiss, kiss, kiss… Without even closing the door, abandoning the privacy of a sealed room, we sank into a sense of liberation that seemed to promise the destruction of everything. Our reunion swept away every sensation except that of our lips.\
Kai didn’t let me say a single word.\
As though words were unnecessary, she celebrated our reunion with kisses.\
No, of course they’re necessary. Normally.\
That was what I thought, but as my breathing grew weaker, I gradually gave up resisting.

To cut straight to the conclusion.\
On that day, the girl barely said a word. Instead, she relentlessly kept pressing her lips against mine. She had probably calculated that this would leave the strongest impression on me, making it impossible for me to forget her.\
Kai wasn’t a child who couldn’t understand other people’s feelings. She was far more perceptive than that, far more intelligent.\
She understood perfectly well, and she had no hesitation about crossing the line anyway.

The two of us had spent an absurd amount of time rolling around on the floor. Though perhaps it hadn’t been as long as it felt to me.\
Even so, I might have spent more time kissing her in that brief encounter than I had ever spent kissing my husband.\
Kai seemed to have no intention of finding an end to the kissing. We were both already running out of stamina, yet she kept coming towards me, swaying and staggering. The sight made my hair stand on end.\
I was certain that if Kai caught hold of me now, I would never leave this room for the rest of my life.\
“I-I’m going h-home!”\
I announced my retreat in a feeble voice, grabbed the door, which was still half open, and stumbled out into the corridor, bent halfway over.\
“Happy New Year.”\
I caught sight of Kai through the narrowing gap of the closing door. Her eyes had grown distant, as though she had only just remembered the occasion. Then I fled. I had no time to compose myself in case someone saw me. Still gasping for breath, I tumbled into the lift, hurried through the lobby, out of the hotel and back to the car park.\
The fierce cold, carrying the sea breeze, struck my skin. Alongside the heat coursing through me, it felt almost pleasant.\
I climbed into the car and drew breath as though I had just surfaced after swimming dozens of metres underwater.\
What the hell was wrong with her?\
The question about Kai, repeated countless times.\
A monster who kept stealing people’s lips had been born during those two weeks. What kind of place had I come to? A little mouth, little hands, and those alien eyes, all endlessly demanding me and nothing but me.\
I knew I shouldn’t have come.\
I shouldn’t have.\
Come.\
I had to think that.\
Quickly.\
From the bottom of my heart.\
“…Idiot.”\
The heat Kai had brought with her melted away all my regret.\
As though anticipating that very change, my phone vibrated.\
“Will you come again tomorrow?”\
“As if I’d come, you idiot.”

Kai, who hadn’t changed at all.\
Kai, whose desire for me remained undimmed.\
Those two facts stirred both relief and anxiety in me.\
What I had thought was a severed connection between Kai and me had, all along, been the same path we were walking together.\
A selfish path that forced other people to get hurt.

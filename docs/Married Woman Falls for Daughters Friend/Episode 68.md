## Chapter Four: The Snow Melts into the Clod of Earth - Part Twelve

\
We set off down the road again. Moving forward without a destination. Or perhaps the act of moving forward was the destination in itself.\
With a glance at the belt of trees planted to protect the area from the sea wind and sand, I spoke to Kai.\
“If we keep going straight, we’ll eventually reach Hakone.”\
“Hakone… Oh, the hot-spring place.”\
“That’s right. You’re not interested, are you?”\
“Yes,” Kai replied, her answer stiff and brief. Every time I imagined the marks carved into her body, my anger at the injustice inflicted by that house flared anew. I felt my teeth might grind with the fury of it, unable to forgive the past that had cast such a dark shadow over Kai’s life and placed such limits on it. I wanted to scoop it all up, everything that had accumulated there, every scar.\
But I couldn’t do that. I couldn’t drive out whatever had taken root inside Kai.\
I was powerless.\
And knowing that, choosing my words was terribly difficult. Even so, I couldn’t keep them to myself. I squeezed the feelings out of me, as though they were tears.\
“I want you to be able to live without getting hurt as much as possible. And I’d like to… help you towards that kind of life, even if only a little. I know I can’t make irresponsible promises, but… that’s what I hope I can do.”\
Still facing forwards, that was all I could manage to say, after thinking as hard as I could.\
It was something I could only say while the car was carrying me away from home, away from my family.\
“...Thaaat’s why…”\
Kai broke into a smile, as though the usual sarcasm in her expression had melted away in the light.\
Her small, white teeth seemed to show the innocence she still had left.\
“When you say things like that, I fall in love with you, I’m telling you.”\
“...That’s a problem, then.”\
If every honest feeling I shared with Kai was going to have that effect, I wouldn’t be able to say anything at all.\
And yet I wanted to talk to her.\
I was probably no longer capable of enduring a relationship in which there was nothing to say.\
We kept driving until we spotted the sign for a hamburger restaurant and turned right. We took one turn after another, going round the houses before finally heading towards the car park. It would be convenient to use the drive-through, since we could eat in the car.\
“Is here all right for lunch?”\
I asked, glancing at the restaurant sign. Kai answered simply, “Yes.” I’d expected a casual, anything-is-fine sort of response, so I couldn’t quite understand why she was staring so intently at the hamburger restaurant.\
“What do you want?”\
“...A hamburger.”\
“I’m asking which burger you want.”\
“I don’t know the different kinds.”\
I noticed the small fists resting on Kai’s skirt.\
“I’ve never been somewhere like this before.”\
“...I see.”\
Knowing about Kai’s home life left me at a loss for words. All I could think was, I see.\
Perhaps sensing the mood, Kai immediately spoke again.\
“After all, I’m a sheltered young lady.”\
She’d turned it into a joke herself, making things easier for me, too.\
Kai was more perceptive of other people’s feelings than I’d realised. She simply chose to ignore them much of the time.\
…Wouldn’t that actually make things worse?\
I’d been planning to eat in the car, but I changed my mind. I parked, and Kai and I got out.\
“Is that all right?”\
“Why wouldn’t it be?”\
We were well away from the places where we usually spent our time, and people were far less interested in strangers than one might imagine.\
And, more importantly…\
“Isn’t this a date?”\
Without thinking, I took Kai’s small hand in mine. The slightly startled look on her face felt good.\
She squeezed my hand in return. I was sure she was using all her strength, but her grip was still fragile.\
“Are you doing this on purpose now, Yuki-san?”\
“Doing what?”\
People often misunderstood me because of the way I carried myself, but I wasn’t particularly rational, nor was I especially sensible.\
If anything, I’d made it through life by acting on one emotion after another, with all my feelings laid bare.\
Even now, I was simply prioritising how I felt in the moment.\
“It’s not a date, though.”\
“You’re a complicated woman, Yuki-san.”\
I thought so myself. Blown by a sea breeze different from the one in the hotel car park, we made our way to the restaurant entrance. A large menu was displayed on the wall there, like an enormous signboard.\
“Now then, my lady, do take a look at the menu.”\
“Your efforts are appreciated.”\
She sounded more like a lord than a lady.\
“Hey, you there, young woman. Come a little closer.”\
“How long are you going to keep acting so high and mighty?”\
“Let me enjoy it a little longer.”\
Kai looked thoroughly pleased with herself, and I found myself smiling, too.\
“Oh, I know cheeseburgers.”\
“Right… Then why not have one of those?”\
For a moment, Kai really did seem like a little girl. Pity and affection began to take root in me, making the path towards the other person seem easier to walk, more beautiful. But I couldn’t tell how much of that Kai was deliberately trying to bring about.\
What I did know was that she wasn't nearly as carefree as she seemed. She was trying harder to get through life than I had realised.\
And I… Well, I’ll say it plainly. I think I found Kai endearing.\
“I’ll try a fish burger. This is made with fried fish, isn't it?”\
“That’s right. Why not try something else, too?”\
“This is a little late to mention, but I haven’t brought any money.”\
“When a child comes out with an adult, she doesn’t need to worry about that.”\
Of course, I’d intended to pay for everything from the start. Back when my husband and I went on dates, we used to split the bill, though.\
Well, this wasn’t a date.\
Kai’s shoulders gave a sudden little twitch.\
Perhaps her startled reaction was unconscious. She seemed not to notice it herself, and instead made a joke.\
“Mummy~”\
“…………………………………”\
“Don’t look so horrified.”\
“...I was just surprised to find myself with a grown-up daughter all of a sudden.”\
She seemed unaware of what had happened, so I covered up the fact that I’d caught my breath with another explanation.\
Kai had shed the tiniest trace of a tear. At least, that was what it looked like to me. The liquid welling from the faint tremor in those green eyes was transparent, and each eye held its own distinct kind of radiance.\
The tear seemed unused to falling. It couldn't quite escape her eyes, and was drawn back in instead. Perhaps the beauty of Kai’s eyes had been cultivated by tears that had never been able to spill over, a beauty born of sadness.\
“Then I’ll have this corn soup, and… I like orange juice, so that as well.”\
“Right. And you can get things as a set, too…”\
That ought to be enough, I decided, and headed inside with the parking ticket in hand.\
The restaurant was spacious. Light poured through the large glass doors facing the road, filling the place with an airy brightness even without the lights on. The walls were covered with children's colouring pictures, many of them impressive efforts. There seemed to be a second floor, too, and the sound of a baby crying drifted down from above.\
I seated Kai on a sofa in the middle of the restaurant and went to order. There was a short queue in front of the counter, so I joined it. Even while I was waiting, I could feel Kai’s gaze fixed on my back. And the place where her eyes were resting was so blatant that I muttered, Stop staring so much, without turning around.\
When I returned with the order and two paper cups of water, Kai’s gaze had grown even more intense. She was always looking at me. Her eyes, and the way she looked at me, had a presence so powerful that ignoring them was practically impossible.\
“What is it?”\
“I was just thinking what a sexy woman you are, Yuki-san.”\
“Don’t use such crude expressions.”\
It wasn't the sort of thing a fourteen-year-old middle-school girl ought to say. Sexy woman, indeed.\
“Then how should I put it?”\
“Well… ‘A good person’ would do, wouldn't it?”\
“You’re not a good person.”\
“What?”\
“Oh, I mean, you’re certainly far too kind for your own good, but that’s not what I’m trying to say.”\
“Were you complimenting me just now, or making fun of me?”\
“What I mean is… you’re beautiful, with a combination of elegance and sensuality.”\
“...Thank you.”\
Being praised so unreservedly made me feel rather self-conscious. I had been called beautiful a few times before, so I did have some confidence in that regard. My husband was one of the people who had said so, too. But as I approached my late thirties, this middle-school girl was the only one who would say it so directly to my face.\
The usual “pervy old lady” was probably another expression of the same sentiment, but… I still didn't like that one. Kai silently mouthed something at me. At first I tried earnestly to decipher it, but by the second syllable I knew exactly what she was saying. I gave a sharp little huff and turned away. Here it came again, already.\
“This is fun, going on a date.”\
“Not at all.”\
I watched the staff bustling about and remembered that my husband was still at work, which brought a pang of guilt.

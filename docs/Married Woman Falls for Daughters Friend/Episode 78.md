## Chapter Five: The Clod of Earth Is Sweeter Than Snow – Part Four

\
“Let’s be more careful, shall we?”\
Being addressed in a tone deliberately reminiscent of an elementary-school teacher left me utterly mortified.\
Once again, on the way to dropping Kai off, I got caught in traffic on the main road and ended up running late.\
At least I managed to get Kai there before the school day began.\
“I’m sorry.”\
There were no strictly fixed starting hours at our office. Even so, I had begun arriving noticeably later than usual, and it seemed that even the director could no longer overlook it.\
“Well, I’m sure you’ll be fine from now on, Amamiya-san.”\
The director’s assessment was optimistic, but, with all due respect, I could only conclude that he was blind to what was happening.\
Things would probably get even worse from here. Or rather, as long as I continued fixating on Kai, there was no way my life was going to become any less hectic.\
I had been trying to balance my family and my work, carving out time with Kai from the everyday life I had built. But I was beginning to reach the limits of what I could manage.\
After sitting down at my desk, I checked my schedule for the day. There were no unexpected jobs that had suddenly been added, and if I worked through everything in order, I should be able to make time that evening. I had only just been reprimanded, but it looked as though I might avoid having to force another change to my schedule before the words had even left the director’s mouth. Lucky me. I had survived another day.\
How many more times would I have to draw this lucky hand before I reached retirement?\
My life was no longer unfolding as a continuous whole. I was struggling just to get through one day at a time. When had the road I walked become a place where night was the only thing that ever came?\
Whatever the case, I had no choice but to get to work.\
I had to avoid being driven to resigning through any further carelessness in my duties...\
“Resigning…”\
The word escaped me as I stared at my monitor.\
Quit my job…?\
The possibility suddenly occurred to me. If I were no longer tied down by work, I would have more room to make time. I could take on all the housework and odd jobs, then find opportunities to slip away to the hotel whenever I had a spare moment.\
Ah, but even if I could go to the hotel during the day on a weekday, Kai would be at school, wouldn't she?\
“……………………………………”\
I slapped my cheeks. Twice, then three times. A reprimand to myself.\
What was I doing, seriously considering such a thing?\
Quitting my job for Kai’s sake alone? Did I no longer care about my dedication to my work, or the pride I took in it? Hadn’t I continued in this profession even after getting married and having a child because I loved what I did? And yet, for just a moment, I had come to resent that very responsibility and tried to run away from it.\
My thoughts were unquestionably turning grey. The colour of decadence. I was beginning to follow the familiar pattern of trying to patch up the cracks, only to make the whole thing fall apart even further. Somehow, I had managed to stop myself from sinking into it, and had just barely dragged myself back onto dry land. Now that I had survived, I had to live through today as sensibly as I possibly could.\
There was a selfish kind of tragic desperation in all this. I had driven myself into a corner of my own making, then lamented how hard things had become.\
And yet, despite that nerve-fraying turmoil inside me, there was something troubling about standing in front of a mirror. My complexion had actually improved. I wasn't haggard from worrying constantly or anything of the sort. I looked perfectly healthy.\
My mind and body had become completely disconnected. My body had cast aside my ailing mind and was independently storing up nourishment. Perhaps the reason I kept behaving in ways that contradicted my own thoughts was that my body was making decisions of its own accord.\
And, of course, the thing taking root in my body and drawing up nourishment was my physical intimacy with Kai.

“Are you swamped with work?”\
I had messaged my husband to say I'd be late, and his reply came almost immediately. I nearly recoiled.\
“More or less. There's not actually that much work, but there's one thing that's taking a little longer than expected.”\
The words seemed to slide right past my eyes. My fingers were moving automatically, without any conscious thought.\
“I don't understand much about art, so I can't offer any advice. Must be frustrating.”\
“I'm not sure you could really call it art.”\
“Hang in there! Maron beside me says so too.”\
“I don't think that's true.”\
Maron might already be closer to my husband than to me. But that was how it ought to be.\
Maron, who was devoted to my husband, deserved to have that devotion rewarded.\
“What's for dinner tonight?”\
“Curry.”\
“Too quick! I was going to send you three options to choose from.”\
I loved my husband. That much was still unquestionably true.\
I loved him, and yet I was betraying him.\
“Beef or chickeeen?”\
“Are we on an aeroplane?”\
And it had only been a choice between two things.\
And so, once again, I piled up lies that night. They were fragile lies, ready to crumble under their own weight. If my husband happened to run into the director in town and they struck up a casual conversation, the whole thing would collapse.\
The only reason I could still stand in a place of light was an extension of the simple fact that the Earth happened to exist, that there happened to be air, and that, by chance, it hadn't run out, allowing human beings to live. Yes, I was only still getting away with it because, by sheer chance, no one had caught me yet.\
The air of the winter night was bitterly cold, and as I descended the stairs, my back seemed ready to buckle. It would be dishonest to blame the weather alone for how especially bleak this winter felt. The one continually lowering the temperature of the reality surrounding me was none other than myself, through the consequences of my own actions.\
On the church grounds, I looked up at the chapel in the night. The lights had already been turned off, leaving only the walls, difficult to distinguish from the sky. The way I felt about this place hadn't changed since the old days. I was relieved that at least some things had remained the same.\
A church. If God existed, would He really pay any attention to the petty misdeeds of human beings?\
And if He ever took it upon Himself to punish someone, I wished He would punish only me. Kai should be spared.\
The fact that, at the root of my actions, there was a desire for that child to be happy gave me just a little, truly just a little, selfish comfort.\
“You should just go home, you know. Be honest with yourself…”\
I hummed the self-deprecating little song as I started the car, then drove off towards the hotel without the slightest hesitation.\
I wonder if my husband is waiting for me. In our warm room. With our warm family.\
“Someone like me ought to just die.”\
At last, I had muttered the words as though giving up on myself entirely. The scenery blurred. However self-centred I might be, my tears were as transparent as ever. Once I became conscious that no one was watching, even sobs began to escape me.\
I had failed at something, something fundamental. But no matter how far back I looked, all I could find were reasons to blame myself. I couldn't narrow it down to just one. In other words, it wasn't merely one thing I had done wrong. Everything was wrong. And if that was the case, there weren't many possible answers.\
I parked in the hotel car park, now so familiar to me, and was exposed without mercy to the cold sea breeze of the night. The wind cut into me, so cold it hurt. My memories with Kai seemed to consist entirely of things that made me shiver, like winter and rain.\
I wished spring would come soon. I wished it would melt all the snow, so that I could walk across the clod of earth.\
I entered the hotel, but before heading to the room, I stopped by the toilets. I checked around my eyes to see whether I looked as though I'd been crying. Perhaps I'd finally reached my limit, because I'd cried quite a lot while driving. But ironically, after thinking of my family and sobbing my heart out, I'd calmed down considerably.\
I touched up my ruined make-up and left the washroom.\
I'm going to Kai.\
I made that decision quietly and set off.\
The wait as the lift carried me upwards, the momentary ticklish sensation beneath the soles of my feet, was frustrating. It should have been an incredibly short journey, yet I found myself looking up, willing it to arrive sooner.\
When I stepped out of the lift and walked across the carpet in my shoes, I became conscious of just how different this place was from home. There was nowhere inside my house where you walked across carpets in outdoor shoes. I was choosing to be outside the home I had spent more than a decade building.\
I knocked on the door, and it opened without a moment's delay. I'd often wondered whether she was waiting right there in front of it. Kai wasn't one to be straightforward about her feelings, but the little things she did without ever saying a word were so endearing that they touched my heart.\
And that same Kai naturally closed the distance between us and threw her arms around me.\
“Shouldn't you save this sort of thing until we're inside the room…?”\
“I've been longing for you. I feel lonely every day.”\
It was as though we were reuniting after a long separation, and I nearly let myself be swept away by it. No, no, I told myself, shaking my head.\
“But we saw each other this morning.”\
“I was waiting for some proper time alone with you, Yuki-san.”\
Lately, Kai had taken to closing the distance between us immediately, as though it were perfectly natural for her to cling to me. It made me feel as though she was opening her heart to me even more, and I found myself letting down my own guard in all sorts of ways, accepting it.\
Still embracing, I pushed Kai backwards, almost as though we were sumo wrestlers, and made our way into the room.\
“Me too…”\
Day after day, I was driven by something close to a sense of duty, telling me that I had to get here as soon as possible.\
By now, being with Kai was when I felt most at ease, when I felt most relaxed. Because there was nothing dark hanging over us. More accurately, everything was pitch-black, to the point where I could no longer distinguish one thing from another.\
I understood, from personal experience, why someone who had committed evil deeds could not escape them, even when persuaded to do so. I was a weak person who had surrendered to evil.\
I spotted the lid of an empty chicken soboro lunch box on the table. So this was the one she'd mentioned. I narrowed my eyes.\
Was this really all she'd been eating?\
“You need to eat a wider variety of things.”\
Even as I did things with Kai that a mother and daughter could never possibly do, every now and then I found myself looking at her through a mother's eyes.\
After all, I had been a mother for exactly as many years as my daughter had been alive.\
“It's too much trouble to think about it and choose.”\
“…I think you should eat more.”\
“That's just what a mother would say… So, are you my mama after all?”\
“Have you been finding your mama an awful lot lately?”\
The difficult part was that I couldn't tell her I would cook for her here. Things were already falling apart as they were. If I added the time it would take to cook for Kai to my schedule, I really would have no choice but to quit my job.\
“Work, huh…”\
How would my family react if I suddenly announced that I was resigning? In terms of our finances, to be honest, we could probably manage on my husband's rental income alone. Of course, we'd be more secure with my income as well, but I couldn't deny that, more than anything, my job had been something that gave me personal fulfilment. At least, that had been true until now.\
In that case…\
If Kai was what fulfilled me, then was there really any point to my work?\
Someone was whispering temptations to me, trying to make me take the easy way out.\
Day after day, all I could think about was seeing Kai at home.\
It was a foolish act, making a dead end into my own dwelling place.
